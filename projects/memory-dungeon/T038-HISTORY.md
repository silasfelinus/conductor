## 1. North star and non-negotiable experience

Build an **art-led, single-screen, console-grade dark-fairy-tale RPG** in which remembering visual pairs opens a real geographical world, and selecting a revealed square introduces an individually authored moment: character, monster, treasure, danger, mystery, puzzle, stranger, clue, consequence, or secret. **The overworld is not 64 turns of ordinary Memory Match; a revealed position is a place to visit.** Encounters may invoke a nested tactical two-card matching fight, but dialogue, treasures and revelations must not demand a redundant battle board. Difficulty comes from interesting choices and changed rules, not endless click labor.

Players begin as an adult contemporary Earth data researcher tricked into an exchange of lives with **Morrow**, an inquisitive fey changeling who wants a mundane, understandable life. The **Reimaginer** can remake realities from incomplete desires: the player will later inhabit Morrow's Earth-side viewpoint and Scylla's mermaid perspective, discovering the costs and gifts of changing home. We inhabit Morrow's fairy-tale existence; at first glimpses of Earth suggest he is ruining ours. Eventually he becomes a **playable, imperfect, sympathetic second protagonist** in a funny, exasperating Earth portal chapter. The eventual moral reckoning concerns lives, relationships, and children formed since the exchange, without erasing the initial injustice.

Recurring party canon is **Scylla and Charybdis/Char**, identical sturdy adult mermaid princesses who look different in color but alike in features, proper versus genuinely goth, and **Oren Pike**, their permanently hooded, axe-bearing logistics expert. Support their comic energy *and* meaningful agency. Dark Grimm with affection, humor, complicated loyalties, surprise and consequences; wholly original writing. Avoid temporary placeholder-prince canon or naming fantasy characters generically by species.

**Target quality:** a responsive, deeply playable 2D/illustrated **AAA-console-feeling** experience, not an admin dashboard, web form, emoji prototype or static story catalog.
