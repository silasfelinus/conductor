## Whose childhood?

**Vesper**, the existing dusty-grey moth-folk cartographer with feathery antennae and veined wings, a rogue and map-smuggler from Briar and Dark Briar, grew up in Hoshimori. Their *established* betrayal must survive this backstory, not get explained away: Vesper sold a duplicate safe route to rescue someone they loved, unintentionally furnishing exploiters with the location of displaced residents. In Briar 3,2 the player encountered the missing-name map; in Dark Briar 3,2 Vesper had to decide whether to verify publicly or honor confidential restitution. Those specific choices have earned callbacks. A child Vesper once learned to draw evacuation routes that revealed **where you could travel without recording who was hiding**. Their talent for safe paths became a talent other people could buy, a blessing and a curse.

World 7 opens with Vesper returning to their childhood cedar bridge, not Pip's meal debt. The village was formative: a protective network of household spirits, reclusive people, warm communal meals and private roads. Its kindness is real; so are its blind spots. Vesper isn't Japanese-coded by default. The folklore setting has original local spirit characters with independent agency, not cultural identity pasted onto a non-human character. Hoshimori is a fantasy setting respectfully informed by Japanese supernatural folklore rather than a historical claim about Japan.

**Pip remains important but secondary:** his apprenticeship at the ledger house and the donor he can't name still matter to the Red Oven investigation, but he no longer headlines the e07 opening, southern resolution, marketing blurb, or the e08 emotional question. He and Vesper can have different, sometimes incompatible views about recording a rescued person's name. Do **not** delete the ledger encounters, Oven hearings, witness consent, or earned Pip flags.

### e08: The village that cannot finish drawing itself

**Visual north star:** disturbing, original black-ink cosmic/body horror with impossible recursive architecture, human-scale intimacy, strange anatomy, repeated village silhouettes and panels that appear to continue beyond their borders. Junji Ito is a tonal reference for psychological dread and grotesque metamorphosis, **not** a request to copy identifiable drawings, characters, compositions, panels, or signature creatures. The art pipeline should use original, physically concrete Comfy prompts, no artist-name shortcut. This must not become simple black-and-red color grading of e07.

**Narrative reversal:** Hoshimori used to hide endangered households by leaving their doors off maps. In the dark reality the routes have become sentient *and will not let anybody remain unlocated*. Paths fold into rooms, footbridges return to the same shore, paper maps crease into bodily tissue, shadows insist on impersonating the people they protect, and an enormous silent procession of yōkai insists every resident occupy the correct address. The true villain is compulsory perfect traceability presented as safety. Horror grows from the very skills Vesper was praised for as a child.

**Vesper's dilemma:** Destroy the maps and make safe travel uncertain, publish a redacted route with community accountability despite the hazard of another copy, or personally guide residents through a route only Vesper remembers while surrendering control of the atlas afterward. No ending can simply absolve the earlier betrayal. Scylla is the e08 POV lens; her own recent catastrophe and time-twisted sea experiences make her challenge any arrangement that promises safety by taking away choices. Vesper is the emotional protagonist, Pip the counter-witness, Oren a competent rescue planner and Char's presence dependent on the earned World Four/World Five branches.
