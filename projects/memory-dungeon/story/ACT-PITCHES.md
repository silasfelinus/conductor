# Story Pitch: Four paired acts and the convergence

[Writer's Room](../WRITERS-ROOM.md) · [Map plans](MAP-PLANS.md) · [Quest briefs](SCENE-ROOM.md) · [Ending matrix](ENDING-MATRIX.md)

**Editorial status:** current 2026-10-30 authoring direction. Do not confuse a pitch with implemented, tested gameplay. Reveal levels belong to the player experience; the following includes spoilers.

## Act I · Pip, the displaced researcher and Briar's underside (e01/e02)

**Opening:** A contemporary Earth researcher wakes trapped in fey changeling Morrow's life. Pip is the character-focused guide through two twisted fairy-tale worlds, initially shown through the researcher's existing in-game perspective. Making Pip directly controllable in these earlier stages is a separate runtime change. Scylla and Char are identical adult mermaid princesses with very different dispositions; Oren Pike, their hooded royal headsman, organizes impossible logistics. Vesper, a dusty-grey moth-folk cartographer, helps navigate forbidden paths. The wolf road, House That Remembers and refugee camps present supposed predators who may be protectors.

**Inversion:** The same 8×8 Briar map gains threatening alternate testimony and a 6×6 mirrored House. Vesper's copied map helped one person escape and exposed another household. Decide between inviting witnesses into the village's protection and keeping village gates sealed, carrying real later-world consequences. **This is the researcher's first ethical choice, not a clean good/evil meter.**

**Gameplay engine:** Reveal geography with memory pairs; interact with authored coordinates; descend to matching-combat or witness puzzles when the scene calls for them. Fixed four cross-realm sigils are *unconnected promises*, not missing throwaway rewards. [Briar source brief](../BRIAR-MARCHES-FIRST-WORLD.md).

## Act II · Morrow, ordinary life and the day the city broke (e03/e04)

**Opening:** Play Morrow on Earth. He discovers office politics, paperwork, commuting and ordinary friendship are more difficult than magic. He slowly becomes sympathetic without excusing his decision to steal the researcher's identity. Photograph evidence hints at the real life Morrow is forming.

**Inversion and irreversible climax:** Familiar Earth offices and transit routes are assaulted by magic. At a failing tide gate the player chooses to open it and rescue commuters while Scylla sees Char die holding the anchor; seal it so Char survives but passengers stay trapped; or divert the surge so Char survives and an abandoned district is destroyed. Warning signs and affected people must be visible **before the choice**.

**Consequences:** Scylla is hurled into the past on *all three branches*; she carries very different guilt and foreknowledge. Commuters, district residents, and witnesses retain obligations through the finale. [Ending axes](ENDING-MATRIX.md).

## Act III · Scylla, the last days before shore (e05/e06)

**Opening:** Scylla wakes in her younger mermaid body in the royal palace, days before she and Char first ran to the human world. If Char died in the present, Scylla saw it happen. Her younger sister sits alive at the breakfast table, knowing nothing. If Char survived, Scylla still remembers the people harmed in the World Four choice.

**Act-level choice:** Repeat the escape, stay below the sea, or tell Char what happened and let her decide. The choice belongs to the younger Char too. The second underwater world twists the route into competing memories, impossible royal decrees and altered consequences; it cannot declare time travel a free resurrection. The pair's ending separately decides how the twins' testimony is shared or kept private.

**Perspective:** Scylla is the playable lens here. Char's later playable role in the finale reflects the history, not a random playable resurrection.

## Act IV · Vesper, Hoshimori and the procession of impossible doors (e07/e08)

**Opening:** Vesper returns to the cedar bridge of their childhood. Friendly yōkai, living household tools, refuge paths and a communal Red Oven show why someone would want to hide endangered addresses. Vesper learned to map safe routes here. Years later they sold one copy to save someone, thereby exposing displaced families. They must choose between public accountability and private restitution; neither guarantees forgiveness.

**Countervoice:** Pip's ledger and anonymous childhood benefactor are still an important parallel ethical story about private memory and charity, not the protagonist's introductory plot.

**Inversion:** Revisit the *same* 64 landmarks in an original ink-heavy nightmare of impossible architecture, recurring corridors, living paper, silent night processions and anatomy transformed by coercive record keeping. The horror is about a map that insists all people be found. Scylla remains the established e08 viewpoint lens, while Vesper is its emotional protagonist; Oren plans real rescues, Char's presence responds to earned history. [Vesper's detailed canon and 16 dark-site pitches](../VESPER-HOSHIMORI-CHILDHOOD-AND-HORROR.md).

## Finale · Four returns, four worlds collapsing together (e09–e12)

**e09 · Playable Pip returns to Briar.** The House's corridors intersect an office floor and saltwater root network. Pip must use his memories of witnesses and broken promises to navigate the overlapping rules. Old wolves menace employees who have no idea what a wolf-road oath is. Recover two memory fragments from Earth and the sea before leaving. The player's original injustice is still real.

**e10 · Morrow returns to Earth.** A yōkai household guardian holds the overnight office shift, and a Briar forest grows across the transit timetable. Morrow must choose whom the city protects when his beloved daily life is under threat. Recover two fragments from Hoshimori and Briar. The old future-market choice IDs may survive serialized data, but public scenes must now be about the broken Earth city.

**e11 · Play Char, returning to the sea.** Char moves through the palace as subway arrivals announce alternate versions of her name and Vesper's unpublishable atlas follows coral corridors. The story must establish which Char this is on each e04/e05 branch and allow her to decide what she knows and what she refuses. Recover two fragments from Hoshimori and Earth.

**e12 · Play Vesper in the final Hoshimori.** The Red Oven leaks into the royal tide gate, a Briar wolf checks office passes at the cedar bridge, and the town's roads start choosing who belongs. Recover the last two fragments and face the final *informed* decision: rebuild crossings by consent, or separate damaged worlds with negotiated safe routes. The protagonist and every affected community retain stakes in the ending.

These four maps should form **one escalating plot**, with real fragments carried across maps and recurring cast/monsters, not four filler biomes or four restarts. At every location the writer asks: *Which rules from two past worlds are now in conflict, what can be recovered, and who pays if the player chooses?*
