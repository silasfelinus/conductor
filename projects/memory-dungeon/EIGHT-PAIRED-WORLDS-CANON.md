## Structural contract

There are **eight geographical overworlds, sixteen experienced incarnations**. The original incarnation is immediately followed by a **twisted version of the SAME overworld**. The second experience must retain recognizable geography, then transform the moral meaning, dangers, traversals, witnesses and visual treatment. The pairing is sequential and earned, not a chooser with e07/e09/e11 open simultaneously. No public level-count, spoiler-filled map picker or list of unseen realities.

## World Four (e04): the present-day choice, Char's contingent death

**Revised by Silas, 2026-10-30 PT:** Char's death is **not predetermined in the distant past**. It may happen at the *end of World Four*, the distorted Earth reality, through a major and fairly telegraphed player decision.

- **Open the tide gate to evacuate trapped commuters:** the player accepts the disclosed risk to the sea-side anchor. Scylla **witnesses Char die in the present** holding the collapsing crossing. The commuters escape. This is a real on-screen consequence, not a purported future or a hidden retrospective gotcha.
- **Seal the gate:** Char survives in the present, but evacuees remain trapped. Scylla and the party now owe the stranded people help.
- **Divert the surge:** Char survives, but the supposedly abandoned maintenance district takes the damage and may have unseen occupants. This is not a flawless third option.

Each choice writes a distinct durable flag, shapes dialogue, later witnesses and material rescue obligations, and cannot be toggled afterward by reloading a screen. The two surviving paths are not cosmetic stand-ins for the fatal path. The player should have meaningful information about risk without being told a simplistic morality answer.

## World Five (e05): Scylla gets her second chance

Immediately after the present-day World Four climax, Scylla unexpectedly wakes **back in her younger life**, in the last days at the mermaid palace before she and Char first ran away to the human world. This is *actual subjective time displacement*, not a passive dream, archival slideshow or a replay of a childhood film. The sisters are once again young. Char does not remember the future. **Scylla does.** The trigger may be a wish or a reality correction; the ultimate mechanism is an authored mystery, not a prerequisite for understanding the choice.

In the tragic branch, Scylla is freshly grieving the death she saw in the present, then finds the person she watched die speaking to her across breakfast. In surviving branches, Char survived World Four, but Scylla remembers the stranded Earth commuters or destroyed district and still fears which choice might lead to a worse future. **All branches enter the same e05/e06 maps**, which is economically and narratively stronger than building a third sea realm or locking the entire time-travel pair behind Char's death.

World Five must let Scylla choose:
1. **Run away together again**, knowing the price the future may demand.
2. **Stay beneath the sea**, giving up the sisters' original dream, with political and familial costs and no guarantee the future improves.
3. **Tell Char the future**, honestly sharing the impossible knowledge and the decision instead of choosing her sister's life for her.

These are game-state choices with different saved flags, character dialogue, map consequences and later Archive/finale callbacks. A survivor branch changes the emotional pressure, not the requirement for a real decision. Char has agency and is never a silent reward whose life the player simply buys.

## World Six (e06): the twisted second timeline

The pair's second sea world reuses the same 8×8 royal geography and departure routes, distorted by the attempt to change history. If Scylla ran again, the court may trap the party in loops of the same departure. If she stayed, the court offers a suffocating guarantee of safety and a furious Char. If she told Char, competing testimony and institutions attempt to confiscate the sisters' free choice. The World Four loss/survival branch affects what Scylla remembers; the World Five departure decision affects what the younger Char knows and what World Six physically permits. The player faces at least one **additional** major e06 pair-exit decision with persistent consequences.

**No automatic resurrection:** World Four's witnessed loss remains true in the timeline that produced it. A changed past is a different causal route with uncertain fallout, not a cheap eraser; later chapters must account for people saved, left behind, or harmed in both histories. A living younger Char after World Four is explained by the time jump, never contradicted. Oren remains hooded, pragmatic and self-directed. The protagonist/Morrow exchange and Earth photo chronology remain intact.
