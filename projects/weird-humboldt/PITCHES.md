## A. The County Has a Frequency (recommended spine)
A low, impossible radio frequency turns up at the Minor, on Kinetic race PA systems, in car radios on 299 and even in a shell held to an ear. Recordings predict different endings to one conversation. No single conspirator explains it; evidence slowly reveals multiple Humboldts lying on top of each other. You can tune the frequency, help stop it, exploit it, or ignore it and have a perfectly satisfying run of bizarre experiences.

**Mechanic:** Evidence categories (radio, memory, geography, witnesses), not linear MacGuffins. Finding three categories opens an optional final interference scene. **Best feature:** allows truly self-contained comedy cards without losing callbacks. **Risk:** abstract cosmic mystery could drown local humanity. Keep relationships as the decisive finale currency.

## B. Everyone in Humboldt Knows Something (community mystery)
A fictitious, extremely mediocre county office keeps a register of local anomalies, and every town resident has their own reason not to tell the whole truth. The town knows what's happening better than any outsider. A reporter gets sources, a mechanic gets trust, a local learns who has vanished. Bigfoot's family has factions and chores, not a special plot explanation.

**Mechanic:** Goodwill network and recurring faces; different archetypes hear different versions of the same report. **Best feature:** richest character comedy and emotional resonance. **Risk:** conversations can feel like clues to collect. Write stand-alone choices that change people's lives.

## C. The Longest Memorial Day Weekend (high-concept clock)
A March fundraising plunge, May sculpture race and October midnight film all occur on the same strange night. Someone is repeating the year; the Kinetic Grand Championship seems to be its reset switch. Player can try to save the county, accidentally erase a Tuesday, or make the whole event loop again to fix one personal regret.

**Mechanic:** Repeatable festival moments and one hard finale decision. **Best feature:** legible central objective and exuberant visual climax. **Risk:** forced urgency replaces the open-world road-trip charm. Better as an optional high-Weirdness finale than the whole game.

## D. The Unlicensed Department of Strange Affairs (episodic tabloid mode)
You do a mundane errand and encounter something inexplicable; each episode resolves with a newspaper clipping and consequences. The county itself is the protagonist. One week you mediate a Sasquatch custody dispute; the next, investigate a theater cat nobody remembers having died.

**Mechanic:** weekly-style independent cases, local reputation, clippings, no required metaplot. **Best feature:** highly expandable beyond 120 cards and great short sessions. **Risk:** no sense of forward motion. Counter with chapter-specific anchors and recurring witnesses.

## Ending families, not prescribed plot reveals

1. **Tourist with Unbelievable Photos:** finish, but nobody believes you.
2. **The Last Honest Reporter:** publish the truth and lose several sources.
3. **The County Employee of the Month:** accept a job keeping reality paperwork up to date.
4. **Bigfoot Family Friend:** choose a small, difficult kindness over the grand answer.
5. **Champion of Glorious Confusion:** complete the kinetic finale with your own contraption.
6. **The Signal Goes Quiet:** dampen the overlap; some beloved weirdness vanishes too.
7. **Everybody Gets Another Tuesday:** reset a day to protect someone, sacrificing the evidence.
8. **Another Humboldt Remains:** leave reality changed; your character's old home may be gone.
9. **Return to the Minor:** learn the house has been projecting alternate stories for decades.
10. **Just Stay Here:** decline the heroic destiny and become another unpredictable local.

## E. The Festival Is the Lens (latest user-led direction)

A fictional cannabis/counterculture festival opens the journey. The adult player chooses **sober**, **tipsy**, **stoned** or **tripping balls**, deliberately and clearly. A cannabis-smoking woman with a green ribbon is perhaps a forest nymph, potentially a recurring guide in all four branches. Each choice alters observations, choices and comic/psychic complications but **does not produce a different canonical reality**. Sober is an equally rich path. The radio-frequency mystery is real independently of intake and remains optionally solvable.

**Mechanic:** A `current_state` that can fade or shift, immutable `opening_state` callbacks, recoverable Clarity, stable Rizz/Burly/Wits/Local Lore/Psyche stats and state-specific authored dialogue. No covert dose, quantitative drug instructions or sex. Author four real experiences in pivotal cards, not filler prose and not 480 inflated card counts.

**Name rule:** The protagonist *sees* dancing, juggling, mechanical sculptures, theatrical masks, reggae crowds and forest gatherings, not a list of named events. Cannifest, kinetic race, Reggae on the River, Humboldt Juggling Festival, Dell'Arte and Burning Man are editorial references, not necessarily diegetic labels. Satirical inspirations: *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas*, the Church of the SubGenius, *Illuminatus!* and conspiracy card games, and a more mature *Gravity Falls*. Invent the details.

**Risk:** If the entire adventure is "just a high," the beautiful Humboldt premise collapses. Prove objective evidence and character relationships remain meaningful from sobriety. Full contract: [PRELUDE-STATE-CONTRACT.md](PRELUDE-STATE-CONTRACT.md).

## G. ECOTONE — the county's contested futures (user-directed thematic spine, 2026-10-19)

The region is a human and ecological transition zone: living tribal nations and land restoration, historic colonization and extraction, logging, fishing, marijuana boom/bust, artists and universities, poverty and resilience, threatened habitats, possible new data infrastructure and a future no one agrees on. This isn't a nostalgic referendum on the single 'real Humboldt': **the protagonist's commitments during an impossible festival weekend contribute to competing long-term futures**. The spirit of the county survives, but whom it can support and which relationships endure changes.

**Mystery bifurcates:** the *fate of the county* is always at stake; the exact cause of layered future timelines is optional detective work. For details, competing mechanism options, six-chapter moral turns and social factions see [ECOTONE-CORE.md](ECOTONE-CORE.md). For 10 alternate future families and scene-level epilogue conditions see [ECOTONE-ENDINGS.md](ECOTONE-ENDINGS.md). Do not turn actual historical dispossession or contemporary tribal nation rights into supernatural metaphors that erase facts. `Ecotone` is a proposed title, NOT a changed project slug or publication mandate; art remains gated.


## H. Two ribbons and Luna (2026-10-19 direction)

**Supersedes Cedar's older-hippie design.** The grower and the logger are his **parents**, a Humboldt Romeo and Juliet story in the midst of the late-1980s/1990s timber conflicts. In Silas's family, a **green ribbon** once flew from a car antenna; green and yellow ribbons carried complicated, contested local meanings. Calling people “Earth First!” could be pejorative without establishing actual membership; tree spiking was dangerous, and some regional Earth First! organizers publicly renounced it in 1990. **Julia Butterfly Hill's 738-day 1997–99 sit in Luna** provides a second important *documented* turning point for what Cedar's family remembers. A present-day fictional CB transmission is the speculative twist. Do not fictionalize Julia's real actions or assume the player can physically visit the protected tree. See COMPANIONS-AND-PROLOGUE.md and FACT-CHECK.md.
