# Weird Humboldt: directions to explore before art

2026-10-19 PT · None is approved canon. **Recommendation:** combine A's structure with B's interpersonal payoff, leave C as a rare ending and D as a recurring phenomenon.

## A. The County Has a Frequency (recommended spine)
A low, impossible radio frequency turns up at the Minor, on Kinetic race PA systems, in car radios on 299 and even in a shell held to an ear. Recordings predict different endings to one conversation. No single conspirator explains it; evidence slowly reveals multiple Humboldts lying on top of each other. You can tune the frequency, help stop it, exploit it, or ignore it and have a perfectly satisfying run of bizarre experiences.

**Mechanic:** Evidence categories (radio, memory, geography, witnesses), not linear MacGuffins. Finding three categories opens an optional final interference scene. **Best feature:** allows truly self-contained comedy cards without losing callbacks. **Risk:** abstract cosmic mystery could drown local humanity. Keep relationships as the decisive finale currency.

## B. Everyone in Humboldt Knows Something (community mystery)
A fictitious, extremely mediocre county office keeps a register of local anomalies, and every town resident has their own reason not to tell the whole truth. The town knows what's happening better than any outsider. A reporter gets sources, a mechanic gets trust, a local learns who has vanished. Bigfoot's family has factions and chores, not a special plot explanation.

**Mechanic:** Goodwill network and recurring faces; different archetypes hear different versions of the same report. **Best feature:** richest character comedy and emotional resonance. **Risk:** conversations can feel like clues to collect. Write stand-alone choices that change people's lives.

## C. The Longest Memorial Day Weekend (high-concept clock)
A March fundraising plunge, May sculpture race and October midnight film all occur on the same strange night. Someone is repeating the year; the Kinetic Grand Championship seems to be its reset switch. Player can try to save the county, accidentally erase a Tuesday, or make the whole event loop again to fix one personal regret.

**Mechanic:** Repeatable festival moments and one hard finale decision. **Best feature:** legible central objective and exuberant visual climax. **Risk:** forced urgency replaces the open-world road-trip charm. Better as an optional high-Weirdness finale than the whole game.

## D. The Unlicensed Department of Strange Affairs (episodic tabloid mode)
You do a mundane errand and encounter something inexplicable; each episode resolves with a newspaper clipping and consequences. The county itself is the protagonist. One week you mediate a Sasquatch custody dispute; the next, investigate a theater cat nobody remembers having died.

**Mechanic:** weekly-style independent cases, local reputation, clippings, no required metaplot. **Best feature:** highly expandable beyond 120 cards and great short sessions. **Risk:** no sense of forward motion. Counter with chapter-specific anchors and recurring witnesses.

## Ending families, not prescribed plot reveals

1. **Tourist with Unbelievable Photos:** finish, but nobody believes you.
2. **The Last Honest Reporter:** publish the truth and lose several sources.
3. **The County Employee of the Month:** accept a job keeping reality paperwork up to date.
4. **Bigfoot Family Friend:** choose a small, difficult kindness over the grand answer.
5. **Champion of Glorious Confusion:** complete the kinetic finale with your own contraption.
6. **The Signal Goes Quiet:** dampen the overlap; some beloved weirdness vanishes too.
7. **Everybody Gets Another Tuesday:** reset a day to protect someone, sacrificing the evidence.
8. **Another Humboldt Remains:** leave reality changed; your character's old home may be gone.
9. **Return to the Minor:** learn the house has been projecting alternate stories for decades.
10. **Just Stay Here:** decline the heroic destiny and become another unpredictable local.

## Which three are worth writing as proof-of-voice?

- Minor Theatre: an alleged Houdini-era trapdoor whose extremely annoyed ghost wants an apology rather than release.
- Moonstone Beach: Bigfoot's family hires the player for a purely domestic problem, then the family photo reveals something much worse.
- Perilous Plunge: a festival volunteer swims into a different day; saving their memory costs the player a clue.
