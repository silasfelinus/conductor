## The promise

> In one impossibly long Humboldt weekend, the county begins slipping between possible futures. Every person you help, betray, believe or dismiss nudges which future survives. At dawn, Humboldt will still be Humboldt. The question is: whose Humboldt?

An **ecotone** is an ecological transition where adjacent habitats meet. Humboldt County is full of estuary/forest/ridge/shore interfaces. The fictional ecotone is also the overlap of economies, migration, art, technologies, political possibilities and otherworldly ecologies. The game must not rewrite real history, borrow tribe-specific experiences or invent cultural practices as supernatural plot devices.

**Human theme:** "Preserve whom and what, and at whose expense?" Do not flatten this to tech-good versus environmentalists-good, or developers-bad versus anarchists-bad. A corporation can be exploitative; its workers can honestly need wages. A direct-action collective can oppose extraction for solid reasons; an impatient faction can seriously damage people it intended to defend. Housing, healthcare, livelihood, community belonging, ecology, local agency cannot be maximized with one happy button.

**Tone:** PG-13 dark comedy-horror with affectionate, tender human stakes; festival weirdness; Bigfoot as a plausible, messy adult/family culture, not a giant toddler aspiring to theatre stardom. Sasquatches, nymphs, ghosts, aliens and conspiracy radio can coexist with actual poverty, art, history and environmental conflict, but never explain away documented historic injustices.

## Emotional contract: the future can be changed for the better (2026-10-19 direction)

**Ecotone is ultimately hopeful, despite its speculative horror, fantasy, sci-fi, failure states and dystopian ending families.** The point of exposing the county's terrible possible futures is that people's choices and solidarity can change what comes to pass. Avoid fatalism, compulsory misery, and "all endings are secretly bad" cynicism. Hope should be **earned through particular human actions**, without claiming any one future ideology is the universal ideal. Distinguish a humane cost from cynical punishment.

**Newest ending structure (2026-10-19):** The collectible ledger is exactly **4 lead companions × 4 voluntary opening states × 2 earned fates = 32**, half hopeful and half dark. First-meeting pairings do NOT dictate the chosen finale companion: a final lead becomes reachable through meaningful **stay-or-follow companion crossroad choices** in each opening route, not a prelude picker. The ten previously described civic future families are **noncollectible motifs**, not bonus endings. Hope/dark resolves from choices, evidence, skill checks and repair, never sobriety itself. See [ECOTONE-32-ENDINGS.md](ECOTONE-32-ENDINGS.md).

**Four independent optimal companion exploration routes are required:** Juniper's shared ecological stewardship, Kit's public living arts/belonging, Mara's own long-lived studio and apprenticeships, and Cedar's viable forest-and-livelihood future. They are possible on *every* initial Sober/Tipsy/Stoned/Tripping route, even after declining an introduction, and do not force a particular ideology-defined county scenario. **Cedar's grower-family and logger-family parents are still together throughout the story** and his happiest personal arc closes with their lived-in, imperfect and enduring love, not a marriage the player "repairs". Character optimal routes should be authored chapter-anchored adventures, not randomized rare draws. [COMPANION-OPTIMAL-PATHS.md](COMPANION-OPTIMAL-PATHS.md) owns the detailed provisional arcs, continuity invariants and gameplay acceptance tests.

## The authored drama / randomized adventure rhythm (latest, 2026-10-19)

**Prelude state determines the first companion**: Sober→Mara, Tipsy→Kit, Stoned→Juniper, Tripping→Cedar. There is no independent first-lead choice. The player meets the other three main characters in an **increasingly personal sequence of guaranteed crossroad encounters**, shuffled by seed, each offering **stay with the current companion or leave to follow the newcomer**. In between, draw a variable sequence of eligible, **individually written random encounters** from the 120-card regional pool. Their decisions and 2d6 successes/failures alter five skills, clues, allies, local consequences and the ability to realize a hopeful future. **Story order and companionship matter**: characters respond differently if approached directly, after another friend or after the protagonist previously left them behind. A departed guide continues their own work. The last person actively followed frames the collectible epilogue. Original opening lens stays fixed for the 32-ending ID, mutable `current_state`/Clarity colors later scenes, and the accumulated choices/rolls determine hopeful/dark fate. See [JOURNEY-LOOP-CONTRACT.md](JOURNEY-LOOP-CONTRACT.md).

## Values and free will: four intersecting fault lines (2026-10-19)

Ecotone's four current **story axes** are **Autonomy ↔ Solidarity** (individual versus community), **Continuity ↔ Reinvention** (tradition versus change), **Ecology ↔ Engineering** (nature versus technology), and **Agency ↔ Fate** (free will versus determinism). The fourth is a narrative/metaphysical lens, not a conventional political ideology. **No pole is intrinsically good or bad**; real rights and duties do not become tradable through a slider. Characters can hold contradictory positions and grow, especially country-and-tech Cedar. The first Sober/Tipsy/Stoned/Tripping state frames a distinct *question*, not a fixed ideology, virtue or access right. Concrete acts, community commitments and outcomes still decide one of 32 hopeful/dark finale records. Optional summaries belong in a bounded Journal tab, never another giant nonresponsive interface. Detailed design: [IDEOLOGICAL-ECOTONES.md](IDEOLOGICAL-ECOTONES.md).

## The two mysteries, distinct but entangled

1. **Visible mission, now essential:** What **future of Humboldt** will its people help construct? The player's accumulated choices, coalitions, protections and betrayals shape the dawn convergence and far-future epilogue, even if they deliberately skip investigating why time is unstable. Agency has been exercised through small community decisions all weekend.
2. **Hidden metaphysical mystery, optionally investigated:** Why are multiple futures *physically appearing* now? Static, misplaced roads, birds refusing future shorelines, ghosts of hypothetical developments, ferries to new places, branch shadows in a theater projector. The "County Has a Frequency" pitch becomes one *sensor* of this transition, not the full thesis. Several explanations remain on the table:
   - **Natural liminality:** Humboldt has always been a seam between possible worlds; a rare alignment makes future ecologies visible.
   - **The Forecast Engine:** a fictional planning-tech machine, assembled to predict which investments will save the county, inadvertently magnifies the seam. Tech is a tool, not inherently a villain.
   - **The Sanctuary:** supernatural residents have quietly preserved conflicting possible Humboldts; the mechanism that let them coexist is destabilizing.
   These can be layered, but the ultimate answer waits for further user creative input. Never imply that historical genocide, anti-Chinese expulsion or tribal displacement arose from magical technology.

**Essential twist to prototype:** The player doesn't win an autocratic button that rewrites all of Humboldt by decree. They build or fracture a *coalition* and settle specific local commitments, then affect which timeline can remain stable. People and nations retain agency. Sober players and people declining guides still make binding story choices. Failing checks introduces scars and reroutes, not a forced "bad" destiny.

## One weekend, six regional chapters

Retain the 120 distinct canonical card premises (six chapters, four geographic pools and five seeds each), with two or more meaningful cards per chapter plus optional anchors. The whole journey is experienced as one dreamlike festival weekend. The real events that inspire individual scenes occur in different months; the overlapping calendar is *fictional ecotone bleed*, and scenes should describe what the player sees without announcing festival brands.

| Prelude | Composite gathering near the woods | Player voluntarily selects Sober / Tipsy / Stoned / Tripping Balls and an initial companion; witness an egret flying past an aerial cable that hasn't yet been built. |
| 1 — Belonging | Arcata after hours | Which places and subcultures can afford to exist? Who gets called "a local"? |

**Ecological throughline:** Let redwoods, spotted owls, egrets and peregrine falcons remain actual animals, whose habitats, threats and continuity matter. A quiet, ordinary safety-corridor drive watching birds can carry more emotional weight than a new monster. Avoid making climate restoration or human stewardship depend on supernatural symbolism.

## Characters in the future debate

- **Mara, independent tattoo artist:** meticulous, dryly funny and protective of her clients' trust. Her studio, collaborators, creative ownership and professional boundaries are meaningful stakes. She sees through easy promises. No reservation, tribal-grant or culturally coded backstory, art or symbols are in scope.
- **Kit, Hmong American kinetic artist:** a daring modern performer, connected to Humboldt's living Hmong and broader arts community. Her family's specific history is open; she wants a sustainable venue, fair collaborator credit and artistic independence. **The 1885 Chinese expulsion remains a separate historical story, not Kit's ancestry.** Potential future: thriving creative work versus artists being turned into branding.
- **Juniper, forest nymph:** wants to protect living habitats and nonhuman neighbors, but may learn that conserving a place *without room for human futures* is a painful form of exclusion. Her forest magic has limits; she isn't a proxy for Indigenous spirituality.
- **Cedar (working name), younger country Humboldt local raised between a logger family and a grower family:** mechanic-minded, suspicious of simplistic ideologies and endlessly curious about odd CB radio conspiracies. His **father is a Hispanic logger** and his **mother came from a backwoods growing family and fictionally volunteered in the ground-support effort for Julia Butterfly Hill's real Luna tree-sit**. Her specific participation is an imagined family history, not historical testimony. His parents fell in love across the timber-era divisions; memories of green/yellow car ribbons and Julia Butterfly Hill's 1997–99 Luna vigil have different meanings to each. This is an ordinary, fictional generational conflict, not proof the historic tree or Julia caused the county's magic. Cedar's adult trade is still open. **Cedar's optimum now expressly includes the technoutopia track**: locally owned fiber/broadband, sensor-supported watershed and habitat stewardship, energy-aware computing and skilled rural jobs. Dark paths may depict outside capture, broken promises and ecological damage. He must choose whether to build practical futures for forests, technologies and livelihoods together.
- **New antagonist concept: the Civic Futures Consortium (fictional):** persuasive developers who offer wages, housing infrastructure and high-speed connectivity, while quietly privatizing control over the machine and land. Give a charismatic founder understandable motivation and an accountable unethical decision, rather than attributing villainy to all growth.
- **New misguided faction: the No More Tomorrows collective (fictional):** artists, anti-extraction activists, students and some backwoods locals. Their concern over dispossession and habitat loss is legitimate; a splinter leadership plans a reckless action that would cut vital communications or destroy an ecotone stabilizer, endangering bystanders. They need internal disagreements and a nonviolent path, not a cartoonish "all anarchists are terrorists" treatment.
- **Critical additional voices:** public workers, fishers, displaced renters, tradespeople, ecologists, farmhands, harm-reduction volunteers, students, engineers and remote workers. Distinct NPC agency; do not treat homelessness, addiction or poverty as spooky scenery.

## How one choice changes a future

Example: an evening in Eureka. A fictional consortium seeks a community meeting endorsement for a giant battery-backed server complex near the shoreline; a co-op proposes smaller public broadband and housing improvements nearby, while an anti-corporate faction wants to stop any new build. A nesting area and a threatened landlord-tenant dispute complicate the site.

**Choice A: sign the rushed proposal.** Immediate jobs and connections to a worker NPC; later a permit loophole disadvantages local housing and habitat unless the player has secured binding safeguards. A good-faith foreman may become an ally even if the company is suspect.

**Choice B: derail the entire hearing.** The consortium loses momentum and a threatened habitat gains time; a local worker loses an urgent opportunity, while a splinter group grows bolder. Repair requires listening.

**Choice C: do the unglamorous organizing.** Find a relocated site and enforceable labor/community/shoreline safeguards. Harder skill/relationship gates, more work, some concessions on land and cost; no impossible miracle consensus.

**Choice D: ignore politics and help an individual.** Save a stranger's housing or equipment. That seemingly small act becomes a consequential voice in the future coalition. Even players who never chase the hidden mystery meaningfully affect the ending.

Mechanics should not prescribe which option is "the correct ideology"; checks determine follow-through and consequences, while the chosen value is honored and every outcome has costs.

## Branching fate model (story logic, not a morality meter)

Keep the existing `Rizz/Burly/Wits/Local Lore/Psyche`, `opening_state/current_state/clarity`, 2d6 checks, relationships, evidence and seed. Add **four readable *consequence domains***, calculated from authored decision flags, not arbitrary +/- ideology scores:

1. **Ecology:** continuity of habitats, estuary restoration, biodiversity and long-term stewardship.
2. **Opportunity:** accessible local livelihoods, durable employment, creative work and adaptable infrastructure.
3. **Belonging:** housing access, people not priced out, cultural continuity, new arrivals finding community without displacement.
4. **Local agency:** informed participation, transparent governance, enforceable commitments and who controls vital assets.

**Reality stability** can be a fifth technical variable governing uncanny bleed and surreal epilogues, not a moral score. **People's informed consent, public accountability and neighbors' agency are constraints, never bargain tokens on an axis**. Record named choice flags, alliances, safety, commitments, tradeoffs and betrayal; choose an ending from authored thresholds, not an opaque one-slider final vote.

**Review guard:** A player should reach interesting outcomes through diverse play styles. Sober vs intoxicated affects scenes, not destiny tier. No high-Psyche-only "true" ending. Do not turn real historical harms or displacement into morality puzzles or goofy alternate timelines.

## Proposed revelation and finale

At the dawn of the impossible kinetic parade, the county briefly projects three different skylines into the fog: a redwood canopy stitched around farms and low-rise homes, towers with humming communications spines, and an abandoned pier under an advancing tide. Every companion recognizes something worth saving in one timeline and something terrifying in another.

The antagonist tries to *lock* a future by controlling a fictional forecasting transmitter. A splinter faction tries to smash it. Instead of pressing one giant moral button, the player draws on **promises and relationships built across 12+ encounters**. They can expose the forecasts, bargain for independent oversight, protect habitat, help evacuate workers, negotiate a rights-preserving repair, or fail while still salvaging a neighborhood. A magic resolution alone cannot override legitimate local decision-making.

**Epiloque mechanism:** a collage of authored scenes at **1 year / 10 years / 30 years**, selected by collective flags, shows what happened to people, buildings, nature, and the ordinary drives between Eureka and Arcata. Epilogues should contain *one beloved thing preserved, one unexpected loss, and one uncertain possibility*, except deliberately bleak endings. What mattered is recognizable from earlier human relationships, even when the end-state is bizarre.

### Fictional faction ensemble, including internal dissent

The **Civic Futures Consortium** is a fictional private development coalition, not Humboldt County or any named real employer. Its representative **Lenora Voss** previously kept a small communications co-op solvent after a storm and genuinely believes reliable infrastructure saves lives. She nevertheless offers a confidentiality clause that would let her investors veto public audits. She can rescind it, but only if workers and residents have a credible alternative; no automatic villain confession.

The **Bay Trades Assembly** is a fictional cross-trade group. Electrician **Dante Flores** wants paid apprenticeships and safe worksites, not a romanticized return to dangerous work. He will reject a "green" compromise that leaves workers uninsured and will also refuse an exclusive contract that bars independent safety inspections. A lost contract costs his household something visible; an earned alternative requires time and written terms.

The **Tidewatch Cooperative** is a fictional coalition of tenants, habitat volunteers and small-business owners. Organizer **Ada Bell** wants accessible emergency shelter, rent stability and an estuary buffer in the same plan. She does not have a magic unanimous neighborhood mandate: a wheelchair-using shelter volunteer and a fisher who needs the launch both have objections she must hear. The group can make an imperfect, documented bargain rather than receiving a purity bonus.

**No More Tomorrows** is a fictional anti-extraction network, not shorthand for all activists. Printmaker **Rowan Pike** objects to an investor-owned forecast engine and helps publish evidence; a splinter organizer proposes severing an emergency repeater. Rowan can oppose the sabotage and still reject the consortium's land deal. Refusing to condemn a whole movement is compatible with protecting bystanders.

**Agency contract:** None of these people is a collectible endorsement token. Each can disagree with the protagonist, withdraw support for a documented reason, or act independently when not recruited. A faction's good argument never excuses an avoidable rights/safety violation. No real tribe, real elected official, or living organization is cast as a secret supernatural antagonist.

### Authored anchor prototype: "The Minutes Are Missing" (chapter 3)

**Entry:** The player arrives at a fictional warehouse meeting in Eureka. A paper sign reads "PUBLIC HEARING" but the folding chairs are all behind the microphone. A screen promises 200 new jobs beside a rendering of a waterfront tower that does not exist. Dante, in a work jacket with a patched cuff, points at the apprentice wage column. Ada points at a blank relocation schedule. Lenora asks for a signature before the projector cools.

**Dante:** "I can teach somebody to wire a storm-proof cabinet. I cannot teach them to pay rent with a future-tense verb."

**Ada:** "They gave the apartments a dotted outline. That's what you draw when you're trying not to say the word *homes*."

**Lenora:** "The financing expires at midnight. You can have a perfect hearing next year, or a functioning connection next winter."

The player gets **four distinct actions**, each with immediate prose, a visible cost, and a later callback. Use existing 2d6 skills; the suggested targets are design targets pending balance testing, not claims about current runtime numbers.

1. **Demand signed apprenticeship and tenant protections (Wits 9).** On success, Dante obtains a wage-and-inspection addendum and Ada serves a written notice; record `civic_apprenticeship_written` and `civic_tenant_notice_served`, but the consortium withdraws its fastest financing offer. On failure, Lenora agrees to a public follow-up instead of a signature; record `civic_hearing_deferred` and unlock a stage-4 document-gathering repair action. **Failure text:** "The projector dies before the amendment prints. Dante folds the unsigned page into four exact squares. 'I can come back,' he says. 'They'd better still have a table.'"
2. **Back the fast build, on the record (Rizz 8).** On success, the crew gets an immediate paid survey and `civic_apprenticeship_written`, but exclusivity remains and Ada asks for a later independent audit. On failure, the sponsor keeps the endorsement but refuses the wage promise; record `civic_private_exclusivity_signed` and unlock a stage-5 public renegotiation, not an instant dark ending. **Failure text:** "Your name fills the screen. Dante's pay column stays blank. Outside, the person holding the folding-chair cart asks whether a signature counts as a shift."
3. **Fund a small public network pilot (Local Lore 9).** On success, a credible alternative wins time and `civic_network_public_terms`, but the group must defer a shelter repair; record `civic_shelter_repair_delayed`. On failure, the co-op cannot raise the equipment deposit; Ada offers a narrower shared-use test if the player brings independent power and a worker witness in chapter 4. **Failure text:** "The tiny antenna works beautifully. The battery lasts eleven minutes. Nobody laughs, which is worse than laughter until Dante starts sketching a charging plan."
4. **Leave the microphones and help the tenants move their equipment (no roll).** The player keeps a neighbor's refrigeration unit and medicine safe, recording `civic_tenant_direct_aid`, while the exclusive proposal advances without their testimony. Stage 5 offers the neighbor as an eyewitness, not a retroactive policy victory. **Consequence text:** "The lift gate groans. A stranger says thank you with both hands occupied. Through the warehouse wall you hear applause, and you have no idea what passed."

**Later playable options, not just ending adjectives:** In chapter 4, the independent-audit action requires a document witness (`civic_tenant_notice_served` or `civic_tenant_direct_aid`) **and** a worker witness (`civic_apprenticeship_written` or a separately earned stage-4 worker repair). If either is missing, present a different playable choice to find that witness at a time cost, not a grayed-out miracle. In chapter 5, a `civic_private_exclusivity_signed` player may seek renegotiation with public disclosure and a lost contract benefit; never silently erase the earlier endorsement.

### Dawn convergence: authored causal resolution, not a single moral switch

At the last parade float, three possible skylines flicker in the bay fog. The rainbow shrimp's propeller coughs; an ordinary person calls for help from the water. Lenora offers the forecast controls, Rowan offers a cable cutter, and Dante asks who is checking the shore power. **First decision:** save people and stabilize communications using whichever tools the player actually preserved; failure leaves a scar but never kills the ability to finish the game. **Second decision:** publish the uncertainties and negotiate an auditable, locally accountable process, accept a narrower provisional bargain, or refuse to certify a forecast while making direct repairs. The optional metaphysical investigation may explain the images but is not a prerequisite to civic agency.

Resolve each promise in three states: **kept**, **broken with a witnessed repair**, or **unresolved**. Do not infer "kept" from a favorable dice roll on an unrelated scene. Epilogue selectors consume those named states, companion-specific earned actions and the existing ending resolver. They must never grant an unseen deed or invent a consultation. A dark fate can still preserve one concrete good; a hopeful fate must admit an actual cost.

**Two contrasted authoring traces (design examples, NOT executed seeds):**

- **Trace A, practical coalition:** protect the stage-2 accessible shelter and disclose the nesting-buffer tradeoff; secure stage-3 worker and tenant witnesses; warn the repeater crew in stage 4; at dawn choose independent audit. Expected later options: witness-backed audit and evacuation coordination. Cost: financing delayed, repair crew exhausted. Hope is *possible*, not guaranteed by the checklist.
- **Trace B, imperfect recovery:** endorse the rushed exclusive build; fail to secure wages; help a tenant directly; let the repeater remain intact but miss the records hearing; at dawn rescue people before signing a provisional agreement. Expected later options: renegotiation and direct aid, **not** the independent-audit shortcut. Cost: lost public trust and enforceable oversight still missing. Dark is *possible*, not compulsory because the opening lens or failed roll was "bad."
