# Ecotone: four companion optimal exploration paths and hopeful narrative covenant

**2026-10-19 PT · user-led direction · text-only design.** This document makes explicit Silas's requirement that **every companion has an optimal personal exploration path**, Cedar's logger-and-grower parents **remain together**, and the speculative-fantasy / sci-fi / horror adventure is **fundamentally hopeful** because the player's decisions can materially improve the future. Character paths below are authored **proposals**, not finalized encounter cards. There is no art or release approval. `Cedar` and `Mara` remain working names.

## Core covenant: hope with teeth

1. **Hope is the thesis, not the default difficulty.** A catastrophe, betrayal, dystopian county, terrifying phenomenon, or bitter personal setback may still be reachable. The player should always be able to recognize a *specific causal way their decisions mattered*, including near-misses where one ordinary act preserved someone.
2. **Each companion has an earned, legible optimal personal arc**, characterized by their own goals, competence, mistakes, and a relationship/community change. "Optimal" means most fully realized *for that character*, not maximized romance or global domination; every arc may retain an imperfect cost. Never award an ideal life simply for picking the matching festival drug lens.
3. **No one perfect political Humboldt:** Several civic futures can support hopeful companion arcs. Cedar's **specifically optimal path** should build a locally governed technoutopia where rural broadband, real skilled work and advanced habitat-monitoring coexist with redwood conservation. A communal/artistic future can also create real livelihoods. Each county-ending family still acknowledges tradeoffs. The player does not choose historical fact or erase rights via fantasy.
4. **Company isn't a gate:** Initial Sober→Mara, Tipsy→Kit, Stoned→Juniper, Tripping Balls→Cedar are first-encounter shortcuts only. **All four personal optimum arcs must remain achievable from all four starting perception states**, through later re-encounters, sober verification, missed-scene recovery, and alternate dialogue. Declining an initial companion must not irrevocably lock their hope.
5. **Honest agency:** At personal nodes, the player can listen, disagree respectfully, help practically, avoid or botch a choice. Companion ideals are primarily achieved through mutual trust and commitments, not one lucky 2d6 or a coercive relationship meter. No hidden "right beliefs" score. They act independently and may disagree with the protagonist.
6. **A good ending is not a prize for making someone suffer:** Avoid using illness, sobriety, family breakups, poverty, ethnic background or sacred knowledge as capricious levers. Real historic injustice is unchangeable by magic; present and future relationships can change through work.
7. **Endings remain optimistic overall even if an individual run is bleak:** At least one convincing hope or seed of repair, linked to an actual earlier decision, should be visible even in most dystopian epilogues. Don't soften horrific events into jokes; don't condemn Humboldt to doomed inevitability.

## Four optimal companion routes: initial creative direction

### Juniper: The Shared Canopy (Stoned first-meeting, all states)

**Need:** Her woodland nymph existence is real, but the ecosystem she loves is neither infinitely invulnerable nor hers to control. She can be protective to a fault and sometimes dismiss human needs.

**Three earned beats:** (a) verify ordinary habitat damage and learn why human families need local work, without believing a developer's false inevitabilities; (b) defend a living migration corridor and bring credible ecological evidence to a difficult human forum; (c) trust carefully chosen human allies, including some she initially judged, to steward what neither magic nor government can maintain alone.

**Hard decision:** surrender unilateral control of a fictional woodland crossing in exchange for genuinely shared stewardship with safeguards and lawful governance, without asserting nymph ownership over tribal lands or ritual places.

**Optimal result:** living forest corridors remain healthy, people continue to have a place, and Juniper can be honest with trusted companions without being exploited. **Tradeoff:** preservation requires periodic messy negotiation. A forest is not a museum.

**Non-optimal dignity:** she may withdraw, protect only a smaller grove, or lose trust and later rebuild it. Don't equate forest survival with disappearance of its actual wildlife.

**Possible flags:** `juniper_habitat_verified`, `juniper_human_allies_trusted`, `juniper_shared_canopy_kept`.

### Kit: The Living Return (Tipsy first-meeting, all states)

**Need:** She chose to return to Eureka generations after her fictional ancestors' expulsion in the documented **1885** anti-Chinese violence. She wants a creative life that belongs to her, without making grief her only biography or becoming a corporate marketing mascot.

**Three earned beats:** (a) take seriously both Kit's Old Town family memories and her joyous new kinetic-art ambitions; (b) prevent a fictional arts venue or communal workshop from being sold off as décor or from becoming inaccessible through rising rents; (c) work with artists, apprentices, neighbors and builders to stage a deliberately collaborative public performance.

**Hard decision:** sacrifice a chance at private celebrity/exclusive ownership of an impossible machine for a sustainable, accessible arts space that can pay participants fairly. Other self-respecting choices also exist.

**Optimal result:** Kit's work thrives, apprentices and audiences can participate, and she knows she can *choose* to build a future here. A living institution remains open in a decade, not just one moment of applause. **Tradeoff:** the best work of a community is more chaotic than her tightly controlled masterpiece.

**Non-optimal dignity:** she may leave for an opportunity elsewhere, build a smaller independent studio, or decline a compromised commission. This is not a judgement on people who must relocate.

**Possible flags:** `kit_history_heard`, `kit_workshop_kept_public`, `kit_living_return_shared`.

### Mara (working name): Lines That Last (Sober first-meeting, all states)

**Need:** She is a talented modern Indigenous tattoo artist with her own independent studio, a fictional casino-revenue-supported tribal small-business grant in her background, and personal sobriety based on precision. She has clients, bills, collaborators and aspirations; she is **not** a mystical key to Indigenous knowledge.

**Three earned beats:** (a) follow evidence of a plagiarized modern studio design and respect client confidentiality; (b) help defend her shop from fictional exploitative commercial terms while also weighing how creative businesses actually survive; (c) support a voluntary apprenticeship/professional network with consent, fair pay and shared credit, with community participation not dictated by the outsider protagonist.

**Hard decision:** trust emerging artists with work and authority she could control herself, without the player deciding what her nation should endorse or treating her identity as moral currency.

**Optimal result:** she keeps agency over her craft, her studio becomes durably viable, and apprentices create new work rather than merely copy her style. She retains meaningful ties to her community on her own terms. **Tradeoff:** mentorship consumes time; her personal artistry changes as she makes space for others.

**Non-optimal dignity:** she may opt for independence, switch studio space, protect a client at the cost of public recognition, or continue building after a costly compromise.

**Possible flags:** `mara_client_trust_kept`, `mara_studio_autonomy_secured`, `mara_lines_that_last`.

**Editorial gate:** before culture-specific published scenes, research a geographically coherent specific nation and seek appropriate contemporary Indigenous perspectives. No invented actual casino program, tribe-wide consensus, sacred tattoo designs or tribal consent checks.

### Cedar (working name): Two Ribbons, One Home (Tripping first-meeting, all states)

**Immutable present-day family canon:** **His parents are together. They stayed together through the era's bitter logging-versus-environmental tensions and are still a couple at the start of the game.** One was raised by timber workers, the other by growers. Their love is imperfect, funny, durable, and they can still have arguments about forests, job losses and Julia Butterfly Hill's **1997–99** tree-sit in Luna. Neither historically was necessarily an Earth First! activist; the green/yellow ribbons had complicated meanings. **Do not invent a separation or make their marriage conditional on a lucky roll, specific ideology, player drug choice, or completion of Cedar's route.** In nonoptimal futures, the wider economy and community may suffer, but do not casually break this relationship for drama.

**Need:** He has inherited two histories and jokes that everyone is running a conspiracy; he finds practical repairs easier than admitting that loving people who disagree gives him a responsibility.

**Three earned beats:** (a) ask *both* parents about their old green/yellow antenna ribbons, listen for what each feared and loved, then verify the publicly documented Luna history; (b) use his mechanical skill and a genuinely strange modern CB forecast to expose a fictional plan that would injure workers or damage habitat, with corroboration available sober; (c) help build a locally governed broadband-and-watershed-monitoring cooperative with viable skilled technical work, real wages, strict energy/land safeguards and community buy-in, while resisting both a reckless faction and a greedy developer.

**Hard decision:** stand up publicly to someone in his own social circle rather than hide behind a joke, while recognizing the legitimate fear behind a conflicting position. Let him disagree with his parents without making the player reconcile them.

**Optimal result:** his parents are **still together** in their home, still disagree about pieces of history, and have dinner at one table. Cedar helps create an accountable **technoutopia** where modern rural communication, repair trades, ecological monitoring, field crews and good local jobs coexist with forest preservation. He contributes materially to a place where forest, technology and livelihoods all have a future. The old green ribbon isn't transformed into an empty unity trophy; it can stay folded in a glove compartment beside the worn work gloves. **Tradeoff:** every compromise needs maintenance and sometimes bruises his pride.

**Non-optimal dignity:** his parents remain together, though the county's job prospects, social trust or habitat may be damaged; Cedar may be embarrassed, retreat from leadership, or make a repair later. Their marriage itself is **not** the hostage.

**Possible flags:** `cedar_both_stories_heard`, `cedar_modern_signal_checked`, `cedar_safe_rural_work`, `cedar_two_ribbons_home`.

**Sample optimal epilogue beat, ten years later (fiction):**

> The CB radio crackles once, predicting a catastrophe on a road that no longer exists. Cedar turns the volume down. His mother and father are arguing cheerfully about the best way to keep a creek bank from washing away the road, both entirely certain the other is missing something obvious. Through the kitchen window you can see a restored strip of forest and the crew trucks heading out to earn a day's pay. He takes off his boots and asks who's cooking.

## Path progression and authored scene contract

Treat companion arcs as **bounded four-beat authored threads**, not relationship-gauge grinding:
1. **Invitation:** an introduction or guaranteed later meet at a recognized regional stop.
2. **Contradiction:** the companion's goal collides with someone else's legitimate need. Present ≥2 meaningful player responses, with at least one observational/helping alternative.
3. **Commitment:** an informed, tangible act that costs time, social risk or resources; a natural 2d6 failure leads to a **repair scene**, not permanent random death of the optimum.
4. **Payoff:** a named community action and a 1/10/30-year epilogue variant with narrative callbacks.

**RNG guarantee:** Each companion needs **anchor slots** in the six-chapter journey. It cannot depend on randomly drawing one particular card from all 120 seeds to complete a chosen optimal arc. Re-encounters, retries through *different* practical approaches and deterministic route eligibility must exist. A focused run should be able to pursue **one** companion's full route from any opening perception; help on other routes is possible subject to meaningful time/decision consequences. Do not fake that all four optimal paths are automatically complete in one run.

**Ending separation:** A **county ending family** (10 distinct futures) is a separate dimension from companion path outcome (optimal / other dignified outcome / unresolved). Reaching Cedar's personal optimal arc does not require a particular "hippie paradise" or "techno-utopia". Some very bleak county outcomes may still cause local consequences that his parents endure, but the parents remain together. The future choice cannot magically change the actual 1885 expulsion or Luna history.

**Achievement/UI integration:** Add four personal thread outcome flags to a **journal/Companions view** (using the existing single-surface tabs; no page scroll), with spoiler-safe "unfinished" hints and authored completion summaries. **Do not** silently create four new global achievement badge definitions or queue badge art on this design pass; the global three-badge contract and ten-family Endings gallery remain unchanged until Silas asks for more.

**Proof / future tests:**
- For each of the 4 initial perception states × 4 companions × 8 archetypes, one test fixture must exhibit a possible progression through Invitation→Contradiction→Commitment→Payoff without mandatory drug use or ethnicity checks. Handle declined first invitation.
- Each of 4 personal optimal flags must be provably reachable through real deterministic seeded decisions, **not arbitrary injected flags**.
- Cedar's `parents_together` is invariant across **all** routes and future epilogues; only relational arguments, safety and economic outcomes vary. Distinguish this from a variable `cedar_two_ribbons_home` optimal-path completion.
- Failure in at least one key companion dice check has an authored recovery path.
- The gallery has **32** unique final-companion × original-opening-lens × earned-fate slots, while companion arcs and the ten county-future palette motifs are not extra collectibles. Achievement awards remain deduped.
- Keep all views single-surface on mobile/tablet/desktop; the journal/Companions tab is a bounded pane.
- Historical and culture-specific verification/cultural review gates remain intact; no art or publication authorization.


## New journey structure overrides earlier arbitrary lead selection (2026-10-19)

A lead is **never independently picked on the prelude screen**: first guide is automatically determined by Sober→Mara, Tipsy→Kit, Stoned→Juniper or Tripping→Cedar. The player then follows a **seeded series of randomized authored encounters**, meets the other companions at guaranteed crossover beats in shuffled order, and decides each time whether to **stay with the current guide or follow the newcomer**. A departed companion remains an active person in the fiction rather than a collected party member. The last guide genuinely followed determines the companion key of the 32 collectible endings. `opening_state` stays fixed; `current_state` may change. Statistics, check results and actual visit/meeting order meaningfully influence the personal arc and county future. Two runs with the same ending ID can have different callback scenes thanks to different companions previously followed; these are authored within-ending variations, not new unlock IDs. Every route must be able to develop fully even if the final companion was met at the third crossroad; add guaranteed catch-up beats. Cedar's parents remain together in all stories.
