## Hooks

### Arcata Marsh: "The Marsh That Eats Its Words" (Ch 1)
- **The place:** the marsh and wildlife sanctuary is the town's wastewater-treatment wetland and a famous birding spot. It is Ecotone's thesis made literal: a built system that became habitat.
- **Hook:** the ponds are digesting the festival's radio static along with everything else; the birds are singing it back a day late.
- **Companions:** Cedar (engineering) and Juniper (habitat) get a natural argument here, and their hybrid answer is the marsh itself.
- **Fact check:** confirm the marsh's history and dates before writing specifics.

### Bayshore Mall: "Anchor Stores" (Ch 3)
- **The place:** "the once great Bayshore mall." It opened in Eureka in the late 1980s with four anchor stores. Several anchors have closed since, and at least one visit found more than half the storefronts empty.
- **Hook:** after closing time, each dark storefront opens onto one of the county's possible futures: the co-op broadband office, the corporate fulfillment hub, the abandoned food court of a slow decline. This is where Ecotone's ten county motifs (ECOTONE-ENDINGS.md) become walkable: a dead-mall horror-comedy and a preview of the finale.
- **Care:** no current tenant or owner is the villain; the futures are fictional.

### Sequoia Park Zoo: "Bill's Last Painting" and the friends who aren't there (Ch 3)

**Silas, 2026-10-19 PT, verbatim:** *"Snerf (aka Snerfy) was a river otter back when they had a simpler pen setup. When I was a young kid, I'd run back and forth and snerfy would run with me. We'd stop at each side and share an excited moment when we'd meet. This feels like it could be a story shared by Kit. The groundhogs (actually think they were prairie dogs?) used to be in the center when you first entered the zoo. They had such personality, always popping up out of their holes."* And on Bill: *"Bill's story is complicated and bittersweet. He lived with a human for years, and then was transferred to the zoo. He was the oldest chimp in captivity before he died. He would pace back and forth blowing raspberries. He and I would occasionally just chill out together. He occassionally throw his own poo at troublemakers."*

- **Bill, documented:**
  - Arrived in July 1957, bought with money Eureka schoolchildren raised after he got too rambunctious for the Polack Bros. circus.
  - About 50 years at the zoo; his finger paintings sold in the gift shop.
  - In 2005, after vandals cut his cage, his keeper found him a block and a half away and walked him home.
  - Died June 26, 2007, at 61 or 62. He lived beside, not with, another chimp, Ziggy.
- **Bill, remembered (Silas):**
  - He lived with a human for years before the zoo. The sources found so far mention the circus; the home may have come before it or alongside it, so check before stating either as fact.
  - He was often called the oldest chimp in captivity. Phrase that as reported or remembered unless a source confirms it.
  - He paced and blew raspberries, sat companionably with a kid who came to chill with him, and threw poo at troublemakers.
- **Bill's card should be bittersweet, as Silas asked.** It holds joy and the hard truth together: a life lived among people, then in a cage, and a town that loved him. The raspberries and the well-aimed poo are affectionate, earned comedy; he is a character with opinions, never a punchline. The unsold finger painting showing a Humboldt that hasn't happened yet stays the gentle bit of magic. Let the companions wonder, without lecturing, about what we owed him.
  - **Choice: sit with Bill a while** (no roll). He paces, blows a raspberry at you, then sits down near the glass. (`sat_with_bill`)
  - **Choice: tell off the teenagers rattling the fence** (Rizz 7). Success: they leave. Failure: Bill handles it himself, with excellent aim.
- **Snerfy, as Kit's memory.** Kit grew up in Arcata, so this is her childhood: running the length of the old otter pen with Snerfy keeping pace on the other side of the fence, both of them stopping at the end to celebrate, then racing back. She tells it on the zoo card or a quiet campfire beat, and it is one of the moments that shows who she is under the performance. Silas has offered this memory for Kit; credit it in the project notes.
  - The otters now live in the newer Watershed Heroes habitat with an underwater viewing tunnel. Kit can notice how much the place has changed, fondly.
- **The prairie dogs.** Silas remembers them (first as groundhogs, then probably prairie dogs) in the middle of the zoo's entrance, always popping up out of their holes. Use them as a recurring sight gag, a crowd of tiny heads that pop up at exactly the wrong moments, and confirm the species before naming it in player-facing text.
- **Care:** these are real animals and real memories. Tender and funny, never cruel. Bill's card carries the honest bittersweetness; the otter and prairie-dog moments are joy.

### Luffenholtz and Baker Beach: "Dress Code" (Ch 2)
- **The place:** Luffenholtz is a rocky cove south of Trinidad, with tide pools and surfing. The clothing-optional beach is **Baker Beach**, half a mile north (confirmed by Silas).
- **Hook:** the tide is going out backward again, and the only witnesses are a very relaxed group who refuse to be embarrassed by anything, including time travel.
- **Care:** PG-13. Comedy without bodies on screen, and no future art showing nudity.

### Devil's Playground: "Behind the Mall" (Ch 3)
- **The place (Silas):** a heavily graffitied spot by the water behind the Bayshore Mall. People use drugs there, and student filmmakers shoot there now and then.
- **Hook:** a Cal Poly film crew is shooting a horror short in the graffiti at dusk, and the monster they built keeps getting better takes than the actors. Pairs with "Anchor Stores": the mall's front is the county's futures, and its back is where the town's artists and outcasts actually are.
- **Care:**
  - The people who spend time here are not props, punchlines or monsters. Anyone using drugs is a person with a name and a reason to be there, written with the same dignity rule as the opening lenses.
  - No directions that read as an invitation to an active drug-use spot.
  - The graffiti is real local art; invent the pieces rather than reproducing a real artist's work.

### Fickle Hill: "Bad Reception" (Ch 1 or 4)
- **The place:** the fog-bound ridge road climbing above Arcata.
- **Hook:** the one spot where Cedar's CB gets a perfectly clear signal from the future, and the fog won't let anyone drive back down until they've heard it.

### Heartwood: "The Laying On of Hands" (Ch 5)
- **The place:** Heartwood Institute was a holistic-health and massage school in Garberville. Its current status is unconfirmed, so write it in the past tense or as an invented retreat inspired by it, not as a claim about the present business.
- **Hook:** Southern Humboldt's healing-arts culture; a bodyworker who can feel which future your shoulders are bracing for.
- **History:** Cedar's withdrawn backstory (older grower turned licensed massage therapist) came from this world. It could live on as an NPC here.

### Ferndale: Victorian village and cemetery (Ch 5, finale)
- **The village:** a historic Victorian main street, and the finish town for the Kinetic Grand Championship (the real event lives in FACT-CHECK.md).
- **The cemetery:** the hillside graves above the village are natural gentle-ghost territory: the dead have opinions about the county's future.
- **Care:** no real named grave, family or person is made into a ghost. Invent the dead.

### Casinos (Ch 1, 3, 4)
- **The places:** the county's casinos are tribally owned enterprises (Blue Lake, Bear River, Cher-Ae Heights, Lucky Bear in Hoopa; verify each before naming).
- **Hooks:** the Night-Shift Casino Dealer background; Mara's grant-funded studio (character fiction, not a named program); a 3 a.m. card where the slot machines briefly pay out in futures.
- **Care:** casinos are portrayed as ordinary workplaces and community resources run by sovereign nations, never as cartoon vice or plot villains. Name a specific casino only once its details are checked.

### Hupa / Hoopa Valley (Ch 4, Backroads)
- **The place:** the Hoopa Valley Tribe's reservation on the Trinity River, along Highway 96. The Tribe is a sovereign nation, and the Hupa are a living community with their own history, government and present.
- **Care:**
  - Hupa ceremonies (including dances) are not game content.
  - Any Hupa character or place card is written with Hupa sources and reviewed before it ships, like Tuluwat.
  - Hupa, Wiyot and Yurok are not interchangeable.
- **Possible role:** a drive through the valley on Highway 96, where the protagonist is a guest, plus a scene about contemporary life that is not a mystery clue.
