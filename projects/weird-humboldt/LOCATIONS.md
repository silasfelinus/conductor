## Hooks

### Arcata Marsh: "The Marsh That Eats Its Words" (Ch 1)
- **The place:** the marsh and wildlife sanctuary is the town's wastewater-treatment wetland and a famous birding spot. It is Ecotone's thesis made literal: a built system that became habitat.
- **Hook:** the ponds are digesting the festival's radio static along with everything else; the birds are singing it back a day late.
- **Companions:** Cedar (engineering) and Juniper (habitat) get a natural argument here, and their hybrid answer is the marsh itself.
- **Fact check:** confirm the marsh's history and dates before writing specifics.

### Bayshore Mall: "Anchor Stores" (Ch 3)
- **The place:** "the once great Bayshore mall." It opened in Eureka in the late 1980s with four anchor stores. Several anchors have closed since, and at least one visit found more than half the storefronts empty.
- **Hook:** after closing time, each dark storefront opens onto one of the county's possible futures: the co-op broadband office, the corporate fulfillment hub, the abandoned food court of a slow decline. This is where Ecotone's ten county motifs (ECOTONE-ENDINGS.md) become walkable: a dead-mall horror-comedy and a preview of the finale.
- **Care:** no current tenant or owner is the villain; the futures are fictional.

### Sequoia Park Zoo: "Bill's Last Painting" (Ch 3)
- **Bill:** a chimpanzee retired from a circus. Eureka schoolchildren raised the money to bring him to the zoo in 1957, and he lived there for about 50 years. His finger paintings sold in the gift shop, he once wandered a block and a half after vandals cut his cage in 2005, and he died in June 2007.
- **Silas's other friends:** the groundhogs and Snerf the river otter are no longer there. Online sources don't record them, so they belong to Silas's memory and should be written that way: an affectionate local recollection, not a documented claim.
- **Hook:** in the gift shop's back room, an unsold Bill finger painting shows a Humboldt that hasn't happened yet. No dice, a little magic: the player can choose to remember the friends who aren't there any more.
- **Care:** treat this as the game's gentlest card. It should be tender and wonderful, with no jokes at the animals' expense and no grim captivity horror. It can carry an honest line about how zoos have changed, if Silas wants that.

### Luffenholtz and the clothing-optional beach: "Dress Code" (Ch 2)
- **The place:** Luffenholtz is a rocky cove south of Trinidad, with tide pools and surfing. Sources say the clothing-optional beach is **Baker Beach**, half a mile north; Silas to confirm which one he means.
- **Hook:** the tide is going out backward again, and the only witnesses are a very relaxed group who refuse to be embarrassed by anything, including time travel.
- **Care:** PG-13. Comedy without bodies on screen, and no future art showing nudity.

### Fickle Hill: "Bad Reception" (Ch 1 or 4)
- **The place:** the fog-bound ridge road climbing above Arcata.
- **Hook:** the one spot where Cedar's CB gets a perfectly clear signal from the future, and the fog won't let anyone drive back down until they've heard it.

### Heartwood: "The Laying On of Hands" (Ch 5)
- **The place:** Heartwood Institute was a holistic-health and massage school in Garberville. Its current status is unconfirmed, so write it in the past tense or as an invented retreat inspired by it, not as a claim about the present business.
- **Hook:** Southern Humboldt's healing-arts culture; a bodyworker who can feel which future your shoulders are bracing for.
- **History:** Cedar's withdrawn backstory (older grower turned licensed massage therapist) came from this world. It could live on as an NPC here.

### Ferndale: Victorian village and cemetery (Ch 5, finale)
- **The village:** a historic Victorian main street, and the finish town for the Kinetic Grand Championship (the real event lives in FACT-CHECK.md).
- **The cemetery:** the hillside graves above the village are natural gentle-ghost territory: the dead have opinions about the county's future.
- **Care:** no real named grave, family or person is made into a ghost. Invent the dead.

### Casinos (Ch 1, 3, 4)
- **The places:** the county's casinos are tribally owned enterprises (Blue Lake, Bear River, Cher-Ae Heights, Lucky Bear in Hoopa; verify each before naming).
- **Hooks:** the Night-Shift Casino Dealer background; Mara's grant-funded studio (character fiction, not a named program); a 3 a.m. card where the slot machines briefly pay out in futures.
- **Care:** casinos are portrayed as ordinary workplaces and community resources run by sovereign nations, never as cartoon vice or plot villains. Name a specific casino only once its details are checked.

### Hupa / Hoopa Valley (Ch 4, Backroads)
- **The place:** the Hoopa Valley Tribe's reservation on the Trinity River, along Highway 96. The Tribe is a sovereign nation, and the Hupa are a living community with their own history, government and present.
- **Care:**
  - Hupa ceremonies (including dances) are not game content.
  - Any Hupa character or place card is written with Hupa sources and reviewed before it ships, like Tuluwat.
  - Hupa, Wiyot and Yurok are not interchangeable.
- **Possible role:** a drive through the valley on Highway 96, where the protagonist is a guest, plus a scene about contemporary life that is not a mystery clue.
