# Ecotone: Kit's Living Stage companion arc
**Draft for creative review, 2026-10-20 PT.** Text only. No art or publication approval implied.

Kit is the Arcata-raised, second-generation Hmong American kinetic sculptor, juggler, and physical-theatre performer. Her ancestry informs her life, not her mechanical skills, political views, or relationship to the supernatural. This story is accessible from all seven voluntary opening lenses, including guaranteed later Stay/Follow meetings. It adds no extra ending slots.

## Scene 1: The stage that would not fold (invitation)

The giant puppet was meant to bow. Instead it acquired an extra elbow, swept the donation jar off the bench, and pointed at the landlord, who had only come for the coffee.

Kit caught the falling head. "We call that unscheduled audience participation."

The crew is moving a shared spectacle out of an endangered workshop. Its painted gear shows a repeating impossible symbol, independently observable in all perception states. Someone wants to call the sculpture "Kit's masterpiece"; the builder who welded its knees wants their name on the plaque.

- **Repair together** (Wits 9): success preserves the sculpture and earns `kit_collaborator_credit_kept`; failure offers a guaranteed slower salvage action with a time cost.
- **Clear a safe path for the neighbors** (no roll): record `kit_crew_heard`; Kit saves the machine on her own terms.
- **Record the impossible symbol** (Local Lore 9): keep corroborated mystery evidence; credit can still be recovered later.

Kit: "You can call it my masterpiece once you can tell me who welded the knees."

## Scene 2: The offer (loyalty crisis)

A developer offers a real rescue: rent, insurance, repair money, and billing Kit as the star of an entertainment district. But the agreement claims exclusive image rights, reserves unpaid public demonstrations, and lets the sponsor strip collaborators' names from publicity.

"I want the money," Kit says. "I'm not going to pretend I don't. I also want to recognize my friends on the poster."

- **Negotiate a binding counteroffer** (Rizz 8): success establishes paid credit and access. Failure discloses the objectionable clauses and preserves a non-RNG repair route.
- **Build independent community backing** (no roll, costs one festival interval): gather specific pledges, maintenance commitments, and a workable public schedule; earn `kit_workshop_kept_public` only once the commitments exist.
- **Support the exclusive residency**: Kit may consider outside career opportunities; she retains the authority to sign or decline, rather than becoming the player's reward.

The deal's attraction matters. Sponsorship is not automatically evil, and refusing it does not magically pay insurance.

## Scene 3: The living stage (resolution)

On a reclaimed loading dock, the puppet finally bows. Its shadow refuses. Children applaud the shadow; the build crew applauds the pulley that actually works. Kit insists on reading every collaborator's name before her own.

- **Credit and durable access both earned:** the artists establish a fairly paid, accessible cooperative with shared governance and room for Kit's individual commissions. Set `kit_living_stage_shared`.
- **Only one earned:** a smaller studio, contractually credited residency, or shared-use calendar represents real progress. A guaranteed late follow-up offers practical repair of the missing commitment.
- **Neither earned:** the collective disperses across garages and borrowed rooms. Kit can still choose honest professional success elsewhere; departure from Humboldt is not an ethnic, personal, or moral failure.
