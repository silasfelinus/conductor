# ECOTONE: reusable county-future scenario motifs and epilogues

**Superseded count (2026-10-19 PT):** The ten county-future ideas below are now **noncollectible world-state motifs**. The **32 official collectible ending IDs** (16 hopeful, 16 dark) are companion × opening-state × fate in [ECOTONE-32-ENDINGS.md](ECOTONE-32-ENDINGS.md). The motifs may flavor multiple actual epilogues. These speculative futures are fiction, not predictions for real Humboldt; project key remains `weird-humboldt`.

## Ten substantially different county-future motifs (not collectible endings)

| Ending family | Coalition / choice flags (examples, not locked rules) | Opportunity and beauty | Visible price / narrative friction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Redwood Commons / Hippie Paradise** | Habitat restoration + cooperative land + mutual-aid trust, low consolidation | Homegrown music, gardens, protected woods, spontaneous art and communal repair | Food and housing supply strained; informal gatekeepers sometimes decide who belongs; people needing specialized work leave |
| **The Silicon Estuary / Techno-utopia** | Community-controlled broadband + transparent infrastructure + public protections | Remote work, good schools, resilient networks, low-impact research and new craft jobs | More newcomers, changing rents, infrastructure's energy/resource footprint; regulatory maintenance matters |
| **The Global Terminal / Corporate Dystopia** | Private infrastructure ownership + weak oversight + displaced residents | Enormous logistical reach, visually futuristic downtown, a few highly paid professionals | Unaccountable surveillance, empty local storefronts, precarious warehouse labor, scarred habitat |
| **The Beautiful Museum** | Absolute preservation rules + insufficient adaptation and housing | Stunning buildings and green views; improbable festival traditions intact | Young families can't stay, services close, nostalgia becomes a form of exclusion |
| **The Free County / Anarchist Utopia** | Mutual aid + widely distributed resources + accountable decentralized decisions | Radical local autonomy, DIY energy and arts collectives | Fierce coordination problems and conflicts about shared infrastructure; an opportunity for collective repair |
| **The Barricaded County / Chaos Dystopia** | Violent faction escalation + destroyed critical systems + broken trust | Some habitats spared by accident, a few isolated communities resilient | Communication outages, failed emergency services, dangerous splinter groups and loss of community confidence |
| **The Worktown / Slow Decline** | No durable coalition + economic collapse + fragmented services | Old landscapes and neighborhood rituals survive in pockets | Boarded windows, housing shortage alongside depopulation and few paths for young people |
| **The Tidelands Accord / Cooperative Future** | Housing, livable jobs, coastal restoration, consent and distributed infrastructure agreements | Estuary habitat, working waterfront, locally owned technology and stable homes | Negotiation takes years; compromises feel frustrating; no one faction gets everything |
| **The Sanctuary of the Redwood Curtain** | High surreal permeability + stewardship alliances + respectful boundaries | Nymphs, cryptids and humans coexist; mysterious natural corridors protected | An uncanny border isolates some communities, changing access and public services; coexistence requires ongoing work |
| **The Split County / Impossible Dystopia** | Failed stabilizer + broken promises + uncorroborated faction control | Multiple Humboldts appear at once; a reader glimpses paradise just across a street | Loved ones live in incompatible versions; geography and ordinary care become unreliable |

**Distribution:** Every family gets multiple authored variations based on who is safe, whose promises survived, companion relationships, restoration flags and site-specific choices. One "hippie paradise" should not always mean low jobs, nor every technology ending inevitable oppression. For example, a corporate build can be meaningfully constrained by community benefits and enforcement, or a decentralized commune can mature into resilient utilities; don't force predetermined bad outcomes onto the player's chosen aesthetic.

### Personal companion epilogue threads

- **Mara (sober route optional, present in all):** her tattoo studio may stay locally owned, be squeezed out, move to a thriving cooperative district, or inspire apprentices. She is not appointed "representative of all tribes"; governance remains with specific nations and communities whose consent and priorities exist independently.
- **Kit, Hmong American:** her kinetic sculpture and fairly paid maker community might thrive or be absorbed into corporate branding. She has her **own contemporary Humboldt family story**, not an inherited role in the Chinese expulsion. The 1885 anti-Chinese removal and later return remain fixed factual history in an independent researched encounter.
- **Juniper:** survives with a thriving, complicated living forest, learns to share stewardship rather than insist on no change, or chooses to retreat with nonhuman neighbors; she always retains autonomy.
- **Cedar (working name):** son of a **Hispanic father** and his long-term partner, one from a timber-working family and the other from a cannabis-growing family **who are still together**. His four optimistic paths seek **technoutopia with local broadband, transparent field sensors, accountable computing and skilled rural jobs**, while four darker futures show privatized networks and dangerous building choices. He might connect safe skilled jobs and living forest restoration, fall for a fictional developer's promises, avert a reckless sabotage plan or withdraw into suspicion. **His parents' marriage endures in all outcomes**; his optimal route gives them and future workers a more hopeful county to share. His green-ribbon family memory matters; the actual 1997–99 Luna tree-sit is fixed history and not an alternate timeline toy.

### Companion-optimal futures are separate from county future families

The ten county future motifs persist as backdrop, while the actual collectible ending registry has **32**, while each companion has a distinct, effortful, achievable optimal personal storyline, regardless of initial Sober/Tipsy/Stoned/Tripping perspective. The player may earn one companion's best personal resolution while the county pursues different plausible trajectories; optimism comes from agents changing outcomes, not choosing a morality-perfect ideology. A dystopian future can retain an earned small human kindness or repair attempt without pretending the wider damage never happened.

**Cedar's family continuity is fixed:** his logger-family and grower-family parents remained a couple through the Timber Wars, are together at the festival and remain together in the decades-later family epilogue. This is **not a breakup/reunion minigame** and isn't altered by success/failure. His optimal path achieves safe new work, forest restoration and a ten-year scene of the couple still cheerfully disagreeing at one kitchen table. Less optimal resolutions can challenge rural livelihoods, safety and social trust, but do **not** change the marriage.

**Suggested other optimal callbacks:** Juniper trusts humans to co-steward a living forest with safeguards; Kit helps sustain a fairly paid public artistic venue where her creative life thrives; Mara owns a durable independent practice and mentors new artists by choice. None of these requires magically changing actual historical injustices or trampling tribal consent. Full proposed route details, flags and QA are in [COMPANION-OPTIMAL-PATHS.md](COMPANION-OPTIMAL-PATHS.md).

### One quiet scene for each ending

Every epilogue should revisit **a normal drive across the Arcata-Eureka safety corridor**. Describe the egrets, peregrine falcons, shorebirds, or what their absence means. In technology-forward futures, a healthy marsh can still be present; in supposedly green futures, damaged habitats or displaced neighbors may reveal unresolved costs. The protagonist remembers one person who made the weekend matter.

A final newspaper clipping, tattoo sketch, kinetic miniature or letter, authored from saved flags, reveals the long-term effect of at least one **small individual act** (helping a worker, sharing food, saving a nesting bird, getting a resident to a meeting, noticing a promise that wasn't kept). The epilogue should not be limited to grand council votes.

## Eligibility contract (draft)

- Conditions are a **set of boolean/persistent authored flags**, e.g. `secured_habitat_easement`, `community_broadband_pledge`, `worker_union_promises_kept`, `housing_relocation_fund`, `consortium_private_key_exposed`, `anti_development_sabotage_prevented`, `tribal_jurisdiction_respected`, `kit_machine_repurposed`.
- A decision can simultaneously set values of tension and unlock contradictory subplots; never award invisible "pro-tech" or "pro-hippie" alignment points.
- At least one **reachable hopeful ending** per initial sobriety/perception state and per archetype; no exclusive drug or ethnicity-based win condition.
- A refusal to solve the transmission mystery still yields a meaningful civic future based on choices and coalitions.
- Failures continue to authored next beats, can produce losses and bad futures, and never fake evidence that the user did not earn.
- Treaty, consultation and tribal land use require independently documented **consent** and don't get unlocked through a lucky Rizz roll; the player cannot seize jurisdiction by being popular.
- Multiple endings may share a macro-family but differ on labor protections, housing access, companionship and weird-reality stability. `120` still counts **canonical encounter cards**, not these endings or per-state overlays.

## Questions for creative review

1. Should a cooperative, pro-housing, pro-nature, pro-technology **Tidelands Accord** be possible, or does the supernatural ecotone demand a sacrifice even in the best coalition?
2. Which groups are attracted to the county's future seam, and which are trying to prevent outsiders from noticing?
3. Which key character made a serious mistake *before the game begins* that they can finally confess?
4. Which cost of change hits the player personally: rent, an apprenticeship, a threatened forest home, a failing dispensary, broken family history, or something else?
5. Does a happier outcome require cooperation **across** rivals, or may an unusually compassionate single faction stabilize a narrow world?
6. Is the final decision about the *mechanism* (how many timelines may exist) or the *community* (who controls the future), or both through different options?
