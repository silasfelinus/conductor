# Weird Humboldt: four companion routes and character bible (design v0.3)

**2026-10-19 PT · character exploration; text only · art remains on hold.**

This document **extends** [PRELUDE-STATE-CONTRACT.md](PRELUDE-STATE-CONTRACT.md), the authority for `opening_state`, `current_state`, `clarity`, the five skills **Rizz, Burly, Wits, Local Lore, Psyche**, seeded canonical events and four authored narrative lenses. It does not replace that contract or require a particular intoxication to access the mystery.

**Silas confirmed Juniper and Kit as companions, then redirected the other two into locally grounded characters.** The recommended distinct-companion structure remains a proposal, not an approval to commission art. Cedar and Mara are **working names**, while their professions/backgrounds are Silas-directed. All four characters exist in every playthrough; the festival choice determines only who the protagonist first travels with, never who actually exists.

## The shared rule

At an invented composite festival, an adult player explicitly opts for **Sober**, **Tipsy**, **Stoned** or **Tripping Balls** (clearly identified fictional enchanted brownie for the surreal option). Each path meets one particularly well-positioned companion, who can be accepted, declined or met later. Nothing is a surprise dose, a drug how-to, or a mandatory psychedelic key. If the character's current state changes, their personal history, accumulated clues, and relationships do **not** reset. Every route has meaningful checks, corroborated weirdness, and reachable hopeful outcomes.

## 1. Juniper — forest honey, possible woodland nymph (Stoned introduction)

**Confirmed concept.** A charming, slightly curvy adult Humboldt hippie with naturally hairy armpits, lived-in thrift-store clothes, an easy smile and a warm, teasing manner. She smokes cannabis, recognizes people without demanding explanations and feels unusually at home around redwoods. Her sensual confidence appeals across genders without ever turning into voyeuristic framing or an automatic romance. Her own goals and boundaries come first.

- **Public world:** friends at camps, growers' potlucks, garden collectives and forest gatherings. She can be funny, evasive, very practical and occasionally wrong about humans.
- **Private world:** a real forest nymph, not merely a drug hallucination. She has a complicated relationship with the forest, which doesn't always cooperate.
- **Arc:** protecting a living place versus telling her human friends everything; she can refuse to guide, argue, or pursue a mission without the player.
- **Shared mystery:** the strange repeating symbol appears in natural growth patterns. The forest itself isn't a Native spiritual tradition in disguise.
- **Other routes:** sober, tipsy and tripping players can encounter her magic and choose to help her later.

## 2. Kit — Chinese American kinetic sculptor, juggler and physical-theatre performer (Tipsy introduction)

**Confirmed companion, expanded background (Silas, 2026-10-19).** Kit is a Chinese American adult woman (she/her; gender-expansive stage presence), charismatic, theatrically quick and physically inventive; she makes spectacle out of a broken bike, a folding ladder and a bad idea. Her voice is playful, outrageous and occasionally vulnerable, without making her the default comic drunk.

**Recommended family chronology, fictional genealogy grounded in local history:** Kit's great-great-grandparents lived in Eureka's Chinatown and were **forced out in February 1885** with hundreds of other Chinese residents. Their descendants built lives elsewhere in California. Kit grew up hearing fragments of the family story, then **chose to return to Humboldt** as a student at **Humboldt State University in the late 2010s** (renamed Cal Poly Humboldt in January 2022), gravitating toward physical theatre and **Dell'Arte's Blue Lake arts community** before joining kinetic sculpture and juggling circles. Her exact school years and theatre training remain fictional biography rather than an assertion about a real student or institute records.

**Timeline, carefully distinguished:** Eureka's mass expulsion was **1885**. Chinese American return began in the **mid-1950s**, although some Chinese residents remained in the wider county through the exclusionary decades. Kit's ancestors are **not** asserted to be descendants or relatives of a named historical person. Do not invent an identifiable shop or personal document and attribute it to a real victim.

**Beyond ancestral trauma:** She loves comedy, mechanical oddities, volunteering on build crews, impossible performances and an excellent prank. She sometimes uses spectacle to dodge difficult conversations. Chinese ancestry is **not** a psychic talent, an exotic archetype or a reason to force her into exposition; she is a fully realized contemporary local.

- **Public world:** juggling circles, handmade sculptures, theatre workshops, volunteer build crews and grassroots festivals; community relationships are chosen and reciprocal, not automatic from heritage.
- **Private world:** their latest contraption incorporates a mechanism Kit never designed that pulses with the same anomalous frequency.
- **Arc:** choose between acclaim for an ambitious sculpture and sacrificing the machine to protect someone; separately, face the complicated pleasure of belonging in a place her family was once driven from, without having to represent every Chinese American in Humboldt.
- **Special entry:** a dancing, laughing festival crowd is an easy first connection for a tipsy player; non-drinkers can befriend Kit through repair work or stagecraft.
- **Relationship:** Kit jokes about Mara's precision and respects it fiercely in an emergency. Kit's Chinese American family history and Mara's contemporary tribal upbringing are **different**, not interchangeable stories of exclusion; their bond develops through working together as artists.

### Kit's optional personal encounter: the absent storefront (text-only draft)

As you pass an Old Town intersection, Kit takes a worn photocopy from her bag, looks at a block of buildings, and goes very quiet. Her family has an old account of a shop near this spot, from before their forced departure. She has read the public historical interpretation but hasn't stood here with someone she trusts before. Then a kid riding a homemade unicycle goes by and Kit immediately starts critiquing its steering. Both parts of her are real.

- **Ask about the family story** (Rizz 7): success earns a personal, voluntary disclosure; failure is a repairable conversational mistake, not a requirement to interrogate her.
- **Compare the block to public historical records** (Wits 9): corroborate documented geography; failure still permits finding a trustworthy source later.
- **Offer quiet company** (no roll, spend a little time): let her decide what to share; create a friendship callback rather than turning inherited pain into a clue currency.

A separate supernatural anomaly may affect a *modern* kinetic contraption, but **must not rewrite the true history, fabricate a secret occult cause of the expulsion, or turn actual victims into horror props**. The scene is fundamentally about memory, chosen return and an artist's living agency.

## 3. Cedar (working name) — the child of two ribbons (Tripping Balls introduction)

**Silas's revised character direction, 2026-10-19:** This is **no longer an older hippie, former grower, dispensary owner or massage therapist**. Those details are withdrawn. Cedar is a **placeholder name** for an early-to-mid-thirties rural Humboldt man with a more redneck/country presentation: beat-up work boots, an oil-stained jacket, tools in his pickup, lively and occasionally ridiculous CB-radio conspiracy theories, a practical mind, and an excellent memory for backroads. He jokes about cosmic forces but isn't stupid or a parody of working-class people. His current occupation is **open for creative selection** (machinery repair, arboriculture, trades, etc. are possibilities, not fixed canon).

**Parents: a Humboldt Romeo and Juliet.** One grew up in a logging/timber family, the other in a backwoods cannabis-growing family. They fell in love during the era of intense timber-worker-versus-environmental-activist tensions, and built a family whose loyalties were never neatly sorted. **Growers and Earth First! activists were not synonymous**, nor was every logger against conservation. The parents may each carry complicated love for the forest and fear for local livelihoods.

**The car-antenna ribbons:** Silas recalls a **green ribbon** on his family vehicle growing up. Green and yellow ribbons around the late-1980s/1990s timber conflicts were contentious *local* social signals, not a universal binary membership code. Calling somebody "Earth First!" could be pejorative in his community. The real **Earth First!** was a radical movement whose controversial tree-spiking advocacy must be handled as serious history; spiking endangered sawmill workers. Some Northern California Earth First! organizers publicly renounced the tactic in **April 1990**. Do not flatten this into a cute green-versus-yellow sports rivalry or give any fictional family a documented real person's guilt.

**Julia Butterfly Hill as the family's historical hinge:** Julia Butterfly Hill's **738-day tree-sit in Luna, from December 10, 1997 to December 18, 1999**, near Stafford brought national attention to old-growth logging. Hill descended after an agreement protecting Luna and a surrounding buffer. Sanctuary Forest still monitors the protected tree, which survived a **November 2000 chainsaw attack**. **Luna is not a public-access destination**. Cedar was a young child during the tree-sit. His parents remember the event, its costs and its consequences in different ways, which taught him to distrust anyone claiming one simple explanation.

- **Voice:** mischievously confident country banter, clever with engines, bad at admitting vulnerability, conspiracy-curious rather than credulous. His own facts should be verifiable independently of sobriety or a lucky Psyche roll.
- **Mystery:** His vintage CB receiver picks up a **new fictional broadcast from a possible future Humboldt**, which is *not* an alteration of Julia's actual history. Sometimes the transmission is wrong.
- **Arc:** a choice between cynicism and doing the work of keeping both livelihoods and habitats viable. A fictional reckless anti-development plot may test whether he protects endangered workers even when he opposes a corporation.
- **Tripping introduction:** the protagonist may be tripping, but Cedar need not be intoxicated. He is fixing festival equipment when the CB broadcasts a line from a future he doesn't recognize. Sober routes can verify and follow exactly the same phenomenon.
- **Relationships:** Juniper challenges his oversimplifications about forests, Mara spots holes in his theories, Kit values his mechanical help. Each can disagree with him without reducing him to a punchline.

### The green ribbon and Luna: prototype scene

Cedar retrieves a faded green ribbon, a strand of yellow thread and a cassette dated **1998** from a tin beneath the passenger seat. The cassette contains a fictional local family's recording of radio coverage about the woman living high in the redwood near Stafford. Cedar grew up hearing his parents disagree over that tree.

> “Everybody in my family remembers the same tree. None of 'em remembers the same argument.”

The player may **ask what each parent feared losing** (Rizz 7), **check documented accounts of the Luna vigil** (Wits 9), or **help fix the modern CB** (no roll; time cost). Only a *separate, present-day fictional transmission* depicts a future county that never existed. No conclusion requires Julia to have been supernatural; neither the tree-sit nor the conservation agreement changes.

**Editorial boundary:** never imply the player can visit Luna, invent participation or wrongdoing by Hill, absolve or accuse real people of unspecified tree spiking, or make Indigenous land dispossession an occult mechanism. Keep actual family conflict fictional, and treat workers' safety and the protected redwood as real stakes.

## 4. Mara — Indigenous Humboldt tattoo artist (Sober introduction)

**Silas-directed background; name provisional.** An adult Indigenous woman, born and raised in Humboldt County, **raised on her family's reservation**. She's heavily tattooed, meticulous, understated and dryly funny, with a busy independent studio. **Her tattoo business began thanks to a small-business grant funded by tribal casino revenues**, giving her the means to purchase equipment, establish a space and grow her practice. This is a **fictional character and fictionalized grant history**, not a factual assertion about any specific tribal casino program.

- **Specificity to resolve thoughtfully:** Her **tribal nation, reservation and family/community setting remain unset**. The writer must choose a geographically and historically coherent specific community, ground modern life in that community's own published sources, and seek suitable cultural review before publication of detailed culture-specific material. Do not conflate Wiyot, Yurok, Hoopa/Hupa, Karuk, Tolowa or other peoples, or infer that tribal casino funding works identically across nations.
- **Work ethic:** She personally avoids smoking cannabis because she finds it interferes with steady hands and fine, precise tattoo lines. This is her experience, **not** a medical claim or disdain for cannabis users. She can enjoy the gathering sober.
- **Independence:** Proud of building her business and deeply aware that support came through her community's economic resources. She has regular customers, bills, friends and obligations and needn't be grateful to outsiders for permission to be successful. The grant isn't her entire personality or a patronizing rescue story.
- **Humor and flaws:** low tolerance for performance without substance; a sharp eye for inconsistent stories; prone to relying on an observation she's already drawn rather than hearing the person explaining it.
- **Mystery arc:** A client requests a modern, ordinary custom design that Mara has never drawn before, yet that identical design appears in an old photograph, in a kinetic machine schematic and later in an impossible radio printout. **It is not a sacred tribal motif, ceremonial design or appropriated Indigenous symbolism.** She wants to find out who is copying her work and why, and must decide whether uncovering the truth exposes a client.
- **Sober-route reward:** grounded pattern recognition, reliable visual documentation, community relationships earned through real interactions. Her heritage gives her **no automatic psychic stat bonus or mystic 'tribal knowledge' ability**, and ancestry never locks other players out of understanding the symbol.
- **A possible line:** “I don't need to be high to know that tree just moved.” Warm humor underneath serious craft.
- **Placement in the wider cast:** If the player chooses the separate playable Tribal Community Organizer archetype, respect its different background and personality. Do not merge all Indigenous characters into a single representative or assume they share a tribe.

### Mara's first meeting: the sober festival path (prototype, not a completed card)

You walk past a table full of temporary tattoo stencils, handwritten price tags and bold new flash designs. The artist behind the table looks up from a sketch, ink winding down both forearms. She's drawing a bird with six wings, except you're fairly sure it had four when you looked a second ago. You point this out.

“There's a reason I don't smoke when I'm working,” she says, tapping the pencil on the paper. “I like my lines where I left them.”

Then she compares the newest drawing against an earlier page. Her humor vanishes for an instant. The extra wings are on **both** pages, but the older sketch is dated next week.

**Three early player choices:**
1. **Compare the dates** (Wits 7): preserve documentary evidence; failure reveals a more limited but independently verifiable inconsistency.
2. **Ask why she came to this event** (Rizz 7): learn about her studio, customers and personal stakes rather than prompting an expositional lecture on her ancestry; failure creates a socially awkward but recoverable introduction.
3. **Offer to help mind the table while she checks the original sketch** (no roll, give up time): earn trust through doing something useful; she can decline and continue her own work.

Mara may accept a shared route, decline, or arrange to meet later. **The signal mystery begins whether or not she joins.** The sketch discrepancy is an original fantastic event, not a suggestion that tribal designs or practices are occult clues.

## Shared arc: one physical reality, four viewpoints

All four have encountered the same impossible repeated sign or frequency, in independent human contexts:

- **Mara:** a modern flash design appears twice before she creates it.
- **Kit:** her kinetic machine diagram contains the same form; this modern anomaly is unrelated to her family's documented displacement.
- **Juniper:** the forest repeats a shape it should not make.
- **Cedar:** his present-day CB signal carries the same symbol, separate from real historic recordings.

The player can bring any pair together; their accounts disagree on meaning but agree on concrete details. Later introductions should change companionship and optional endings without negating earlier cards.

## Writing, testing and publication boundaries

- **Four introductions, one canonical event**: prelude changes how the event is perceived, not whether supernatural anomalies exist.
- **Save:** preserve opening/current state, Clarity, companion relationships, evidence provenance and choice/roll history. No reroll on refresh or retroactive state shifts.
- **Competence is earned and contextual:** Mara's knowledge comes from her specific life and skills; Juniper's magic from her fictional nature; Cedar's country repair skills and cross-family perspective; Kit's ingenuity from making things. No person is a decorative stereotype.
- **Review:** text-only authoring and mechanical tests may proceed; source/local review needed before any detailed culturally specific material or outward-facing release; **no artwork, ArtJobs, image commissions or publishing** without Silas's separately requested approval.
- **Names:** Juniper and Kit selected; Cedar and Mara remain working names, available for renaming.
