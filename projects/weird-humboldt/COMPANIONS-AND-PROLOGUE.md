# Weird Humboldt: four companion routes and character bible (design v0.3)

**CURRENT SCOPE (2026-10-22 PT):** Prior Wiyot/Tuluwat-associated backstories, tribal-grant concepts and unreviewed cultural art are removed from development. The rest of the companion arcs continue. See [the exclusion decision](EXCLUDED-CONTENT-2026-10-22.md); older brainstorming in related files is archival, not a review dependency.

**2026-10-19 PT · character exploration; text only · art remains on hold.**

This document **extends** [PRELUDE-STATE-CONTRACT.md](PRELUDE-STATE-CONTRACT.md), the authority for `opening_state`, `current_state`, `clarity`, the five skills **Rizz, Burly, Wits, Local Lore, Psyche**, seeded canonical events and four authored narrative lenses. It does not replace that contract or require a particular intoxication to access the mystery.

**Silas confirmed Juniper and Kit as companions, then redirected the other two into locally grounded characters.** The recommended distinct-companion structure remains a proposal, not an approval to commission art. Cedar and Mara are **working names**, while their professions/backgrounds are Silas-directed. All four characters exist in every playthrough; the festival choice determines only who the protagonist first travels with, never who actually exists.

## Companion crossings, not a selectable party

The adult protagonist starts by choosing a voluntary perception state, automatically meeting **Sober/Mara, Tipsy/Kit, Stoned/Juniper, Tripping/Cedar**. They experience seeded randomized local stories between guaranteed first-time meetings with each other member of the four. At the meeting they can **stay with their current companion** or **leave to follow the newcomer**, with the previous companion continuing their own independent mission. Choosing a different companion alters the adventure, not merely the portrait; the `meeting_order`, `follow_history`, stats and evidence affect dialogue and epilogue callbacks. Even late-followed companions must have an authored deepening/repair route. All four are met every run in changing order; one active follower at a time. Original opening perspective stays fixed for 32 collectible endings while mutable current state may change. See [JOURNEY-LOOP-CONTRACT.md](JOURNEY-LOOP-CONTRACT.md).

## The shared rule

**2026-10-19 character-arc amendment:** each of the four companions receives a **distinct achievable optimal personal exploration route**, independent of opening intoxication/perception. These are earned human-scale, optimistic resolutions, not a one-click perfect-world reward or exclusive endings. **Cedar's Hispanic father and his partner, from logger and grower families, are still together at the start and stay together; do not make their breakup a dramatic branch.** See [COMPANION-OPTIMAL-PATHS.md](COMPANION-OPTIMAL-PATHS.md) for the four provisional arcs, recovered missed meetings, persistent flags and personal epilogue variants.


At an invented composite festival, an adult player explicitly opts for **Sober**, **Tipsy**, **Stoned** or **Tripping Balls** (clearly identified fictional enchanted brownie for the surreal option). Each path meets one particularly well-positioned companion, who can be accepted, declined or met later. Nothing is a surprise dose, a drug how-to, or a mandatory psychedelic key. If the character's current state changes, their personal history, accumulated clues, and relationships do **not** reset. Every route has meaningful checks, corroborated weirdness, and reachable hopeful outcomes.

## 1. Juniper — forest honey, possible woodland nymph (Stoned introduction)

**Confirmed concept.** A charming, slightly curvy adult Humboldt hippie with naturally hairy armpits, lived-in thrift-store clothes, an easy smile and a warm, teasing manner. She smokes cannabis, recognizes people without demanding explanations and feels unusually at home around redwoods. Her sensual confidence appeals across genders without ever turning into voyeuristic framing or an automatic romance. Her own goals and boundaries come first.

- **Public world:** friends at camps, growers' potlucks, garden collectives and forest gatherings. She can be funny, evasive, very practical and occasionally wrong about humans.
- **Private world:** a real forest nymph, not merely a drug hallucination. She has a complicated relationship with the forest, which doesn't always cooperate.
- **Arc:** protecting a living place versus telling her human friends everything; she can refuse to guide, argue, or pursue a mission without the player.
- **Shared mystery:** the strange repeating symbol appears in natural growth patterns. The forest itself isn't a Native spiritual tradition in disguise.
- **Other routes:** sober, tipsy and tripping players can encounter her magic and choose to help her later.

## 2. Kit — Hmong American kinetic sculptor, juggler and physical-theatre performer (Tipsy introduction)

**Latest cast correction, 2026-10-19 PT:** Kit is **Hmong American**, retaining her exuberant and gender-expansive performance style, kinetic art, juggling and stagecraft. The prior **Chinese American identity and invented ancestor-family link to Eureka's 1885 expulsion are fully withdrawn**. The actual 1885 Chinese expulsion and 1950s-era return remain an important *separate, historically grounded county thread*, not genealogy we can transplant to Hmong characters.

**Family history (Silas, 2026-10-19 PT):** Kit is US-born and second generation. Her grandparents came as refugees from Laos after the war (Hmong families who fought in the US-backed war in Laos) and settled in Minnesota. She was born there and still remembers it. Her parents moved the family to California when she was small, and she **grew up in Arcata**: an American kid with a strong Hmong community. She is a Humboldt local, not a newcomer who came for school. Clan, dialect and ritual details stay unassigned until Silas chooses them.

**A memory Silas gave Kit (2026-10-19 PT):** as a little kid at the Sequoia Park Zoo, back when the river otters had a simpler pen, she ran back and forth along the fence and an otter named Snerfy ran with her. They'd stop at each end and share an excited moment, then race back. Kit tells it when she lets the performance drop. See LOCATIONS.md.

**Kit, an individual:** She is a charismatic adult woman (she/her) who builds extraordinary mechanical spectacles from scrap, can turn a broken pedal cart into a stage, loves the absurd, jokes before admitting fear, and knows the cost of making art. Her friends, neighbors, possible relatives and peers belong to Humboldt's real contemporary Hmong and wider creative communities, each with their own agency. **Her family's locality, generation, migration background, languages, surname and specific traditions are still open creative questions**, not assumptions. An HSU/Cal Poly Humboldt or Dell'Arte/Blue Lake connection can remain a proposed **fictional** part of her training, not an established fact about any real attendee. Her cultural identity isn't an automatic mechanical talent, drug effect, psychic power or ceremonial costume.

- **Public life:** physical theatre, community workshops, juggling, kinetic sculptures, volunteer build crews and complicated local artistic friendships.
- **Private mystery:** a modern kinetic mechanism operates by rules Kit did not write, with independently verifiable supernatural evidence unrelated to her ancestry.
- **Personal conflict:** private fame and fast corporate sponsorship versus fair-pay, credited collaboration, affordable venues and sustainable art; her family may challenge her own career plans without being representatives of Hmong culture.
- **Other routes:** Tipsy offers a playful introduction. Sober, Stoned, Tripping, Cocaine, Meth and MDMA openings can also meet and ultimately follow Kit through the guaranteed stay/follow journey; none creates an ethnic ideology score.
- **Relationships:** she and Mara respect each other's artistic discipline, Cedar helps fix complicated parts, and Juniper questions whether a grand spectacle respects living places.

### Kit's revised encounter: the stage that won't fold (text-only prototype)

At an imaginary maker cooperative, Kit is helping move a gigantic kinetic puppet before a lease expires. One of her fictional Hmong relatives is a contemporary working artist/technician, not a pretext for exotic textiles or a generic immigrant-history lecture. The team squabbles about who owns the drawings and who will be paid. Then the machine folds open onto an impossible street. The anomaly is real, regardless of drugs.

- **Repair the folding gear with the builders** (Wits 9): save the puppet and make attribution visible; failure leads to a slower, workable salvage plan.
- **Negotiate the lease and credit** (Rizz 8): seek durable, enforceable support rather than an empty sponsor's promise.
- **Clear a passage and help the neighbors** (no roll, time cost): give Kit the freedom to choose where the performance goes.

An optimal Kit path builds an accessible, fairly paid community art/workshop space where her singular talent and other artists flourish without flattening Hmong identity into branding.

### Separate historical scene: the actual Chinese expulsion

A historically researched Old Town archive, community-led exhibition, or **independent Chinese American character** can help players understand the **real 1885 Eureka Chinese expulsion and later community reestablishment**. Kit can participate as a contemporary local artist but **must not be written as descended from the expelled families by virtue of being Hmong or Asian American**. Keep actual history immutable and distinguish public facts from the game's supernatural inventions.

## 3. Cedar (working name) — the child of two ribbons (Tripping Balls introduction)

**Silas's revised character direction, 2026-10-19:** This is **no longer an older hippie, former grower, dispensary owner or massage therapist**. Those details are withdrawn. Cedar is a **placeholder name** for an early-to-mid-thirties rural Humboldt man with a more redneck/country presentation: beat-up work boots, an oil-stained jacket, tools in his pickup, lively and occasionally ridiculous CB-radio conspiracy theories, a practical mind, and an excellent memory for backroads. He jokes about cosmic forces but isn't stupid or a parody of working-class people. His current occupation is **open for creative selection** (machinery repair, arboriculture, trades, etc. are possibilities, not fixed canon).

**Parents: a Humboldt Romeo and Juliet, now clearly defined.** **Cedar's father is a Hispanic logger**, who knew the pressures of timber work and cared about wages and safety. **His mother came from a backwoods cannabis-growing family and was an environmental activist who served among the wider ground-support volunteers during Julia Butterfly Hill's real 1997–1999 tree-sit in Luna.** The mother is a **fictional participant**, not an identified member of the documented crew. She may remember helping organize supplies and neighbors, but no invented interaction with Julia, specific logistics shift, or family keepsake is offered as verified history.

**Their romance survived the timber/environmental conflicts and they remain together in every future.** He may defend skilled timber livelihoods without demonizing conservation; she may remain passionately committed to old-growth protection without automatically belonging to Earth First! or approving tree spiking. A growing-family upbringing does not imply either. They are allowed fierce disagreements, humor, affection and independent lives. Dad's exact Hispanic heritage and the mother's ethnicity remain undecided; neither background dictates accents, stereotypes or an ideological stat.

**The car-antenna ribbons:** Silas recalls a **green ribbon** on his family vehicle growing up. Green and yellow ribbons around the late-1980s/1990s timber conflicts were contentious *local* social signals, not a universal binary membership code. Calling somebody "Earth First!" could be pejorative in his community. The real **Earth First!** was a radical movement whose controversial tree-spiking advocacy must be handled as serious history; spiking endangered sawmill workers. Some Northern California Earth First! organizers publicly renounced the tactic in **April 1990**. Do not flatten this into a cute green-versus-yellow sports rivalry or give any fictional family a documented real person's guilt.

**Julia Butterfly Hill as the family's historical hinge:** Julia Butterfly Hill's **738-day tree-sit in Luna, from December 10, 1997 to December 18, 1999**, near Stafford brought national attention to old-growth logging. Hill descended after an agreement protecting Luna and a surrounding buffer. Sanctuary Forest still monitors the protected tree, which survived a **November 2000 chainsaw attack**. **Luna is not a public-access destination**. Cedar was a young child during the tree-sit. His activist mother remembers her fictional support work during the historic tree-sit, while his Hispanic logger father remembers the pressures of timber employment. These imagined family recollections are not historical eyewitness quotes. Their opposing experiences taught Cedar to distrust anyone claiming one simple explanation.

- **Voice:** mischievously confident country banter, clever with engines, bad at admitting vulnerability, conspiracy-curious rather than credulous. His own facts should be verifiable independently of sobriety or a lucky Psyche roll.
- **Mystery:** His vintage CB receiver picks up a **new fictional broadcast from a possible future Humboldt**, which is *not* an alteration of Julia's actual history. Sometimes the transmission is wrong.
- **Arc:** a choice between cynicism and doing the work of keeping both livelihoods and habitats viable. **Optimal path:** use hard-earned evidence, worker-safety and forest restoration to create real shared local opportunity, then return to a dinner with his still-together parents, whose love already crossed the divide. **Their marriage is canon, not a player-unlocked prize.** A fictional reckless anti-development plot may test whether he protects endangered workers even when he opposes a corporation.
- **Tripping introduction:** the protagonist may be tripping, but Cedar need not be intoxicated. He is fixing festival equipment when the CB broadcasts a line from a future he doesn't recognize. Sober routes can verify and follow exactly the same phenomenon.
- **Relationships:** Juniper challenges his oversimplifications about forests, Mara spots holes in his theories, Kit values his mechanical help. Each can disagree with him without reducing him to a punchline.

### The green ribbon and Luna: prototype scene

Cedar retrieves a faded green ribbon, a strand of yellow thread and a cassette dated **1998** from a tin beneath the passenger seat. An invented support-run checklist in his mother's handwriting sits beside his father's old logging work gloves; these are **fictional keepsakes, not archival records**. The cassette contains a fictional local family's recording of radio coverage about the woman living high in the redwood near Stafford. Cedar grew up hearing his parents disagree over that tree.

> “Everybody in my family remembers the same tree. None of 'em remembers the same argument.”

The player may **ask the Hispanic logger father and activist mother what each feared losing** (Rizz 7), **check documented accounts of the Luna vigil** (Wits 9), or **help fix the modern CB** (no roll; time cost). Only a *separate, present-day fictional transmission* depicts a future county that never existed. No conclusion requires Julia to have been supernatural; neither the tree-sit nor the conservation agreement changes.

**Editorial boundary:** never imply the player can visit Luna, invent participation or wrongdoing by Hill, absolve or accuse real people of unspecified tree spiking, or make Indigenous land dispossession an occult mechanism. Keep actual family conflict fictional, and treat workers' safety and the protected redwood as real stakes.

## 4. Mara — independent tattoo artist (Sober introduction)

Mara is a fictional, professionally established Humboldt tattoo artist: meticulous, dryly funny and fiercely protective of her clients. She runs an independent studio, knows the cost of rent and supplies, and values friends who respect privacy instead of rewarding sensationalism. Her family background is intentionally unspecified. The earlier proposal assigning a real tribal nation, reservation upbringing or casino-grant origin is **withdrawn**, not waiting for community research.

- **Work ethic:** She personally avoids cannabis while working because she prefers precise lines; that is an individual preference, not a statement about everyone else.
- **Independence:** She makes the decisions about clients, credit, prices, consent and what work may be copied, without a gratitude narrative or special grant.
- **Humor and flaws:** Quick to call out empty performance, slower to admit when she has misunderstood somebody else's motives.
- **Mystery arc:** A completely invented six-winged design appears in an old photograph, a kinetic machine sketch and a later impossible radio printout before she draws it. It is **modern original art**, not an Indigenous or ceremonial motif. She must decide whether pursuing the source risks identifying a client.
- **Sober-route reward:** Grounded observation, a paper trail, ordinary community friendships and precise documentation, with no identity-based supernatural abilities.
- **A possible line:** “I don't need to be high to know that tree just moved.”

### Mara's first meeting: the sober festival path (prototype, not a completed card)

You walk past a table full of temporary tattoo stencils, handwritten price tags and bold new flash designs. The artist behind the table looks up from a sketch, ink winding down both forearms. She's drawing a bird with six wings, except you're fairly sure it had four when you looked a second ago. You point this out.

“There's a reason I don't smoke when I'm working,” she says, tapping the pencil on the paper. “I like my lines where I left them.”

Then she compares the newest drawing against an earlier page. Her humor vanishes for an instant. The extra wings are on **both** pages, but the older sketch is dated next week.

**Three early player choices:**
1. **Compare the dates** (Wits 7): preserve documentary evidence; failure reveals a more limited but independently verifiable inconsistency.
2. **Ask why she came to this event** (Rizz 7): learn about her studio, customers and personal stakes rather than prompting an expositional lecture on her ancestry; failure creates a socially awkward but recoverable introduction.
3. **Offer to help mind the table while she checks the original sketch** (no roll, give up time): earn trust through doing something useful; she can decline and continue her own work.

Mara may accept a shared route, decline, or arrange to meet later. **The signal mystery begins whether or not she joins.** The sketch discrepancy is an original fantastic event, not a suggestion that tribal designs or practices are occult clues.

## Shared arc: one physical reality, four viewpoints

All four have encountered the same impossible repeated sign or frequency, in independent human contexts:

- **Mara:** a modern flash design appears twice before she creates it.
- **Kit:** her kinetic machine diagram contains the same form; this modern anomaly is unrelated to her family's documented displacement.
- **Juniper:** the forest repeats a shape it should not make.
- **Cedar:** his present-day CB signal carries the same symbol, separate from real historic recordings.

The player can bring any pair together; their accounts disagree on meaning but agree on concrete details. Later introductions should change companionship and optional endings without negating earlier cards.

## Writing, testing and publication boundaries

- **Four introductions, one canonical event**: prelude changes how the event is perceived, not whether supernatural anomalies exist.
- **Save:** preserve opening/current state, Clarity, companion relationships, evidence provenance and choice/roll history. No reroll on refresh or retroactive state shifts.
- **Competence is earned and contextual:** Mara's knowledge comes from her specific life and skills; Juniper's magic from her fictional nature; Cedar's country repair skills and cross-family perspective; Kit's ingenuity from making things. No person is a decorative stereotype.
- **Review:** text-only authoring and mechanical tests may proceed; source/local review needed before any detailed culturally specific material or outward-facing release; **no artwork, ArtJobs, image commissions or publishing** without Silas's separately requested approval.
- **Names:** Juniper and Kit selected; Cedar and Mara remain working names, available for renaming.
