## Evidence and limits

- A new CI test sampled **1,260 completed campaigns**: three event starts × seven voluntary opening lenses × twelve seeded runs × five choice policies. All **252 five-policy matched groups** diverged in complete choice history, saved consequence snapshot and final ending ID. There were 1,260 different whole-history signatures.
- The CI log reported distinct ending IDs per simulated style: **evidence hunter 44**, **caretaker 21**, **weirdness chaser 39**, **solitary observer 21**, **coalition builder 21**. These are *sampled coverage* counts; they do not prove some archetypes are less fun or locked out of endings.
- Existing Ecotone CI separately reported **8,400 seeded campaigns**, **84/84 ending IDs witnessed**, **17,492 failed skill rolls**, **1,186 earned callbacks**, and **9–13 recorded decisions** per route. This supports mechanical reachability, not a claimed 45–75-minute average reading experience.
- `verifyEcotonePlaystyles.test.ts` scores choices by different synthetic objectives. Its full-history uniqueness metric is strongly influenced by the policies' deliberately different decisions, and its ending diversity by the choice/companion/fate grammar. **None of those numbers prove dramatic uniqueness.** Do not adopt a "252/252 = 100% fun" interpretation.
- `engine.ts` offers later encounter cards using chapter pools, seed and eligibility (`requiresLead`, `requiresLens`, `requiresMeetCount`, `requiresFormerGuide`). Existing saved flags are used for asides and callbacks, but there is no general precondition for *specific civic evidence or repair flags* to unlock an alternative later decision. A policy can affect its ending meter without rerouting the dramatic middle.
- `content.ts` defines seven origin archetypes with promised special `perk` text (e.g., a one-per-chapter reporter sourcing question or student partial-success rewrite). The runtime uses their initial `skills` values, while the board shows the perk label. No spent-perk state or reducer action was found. This is a concrete UI/gameplay contract mismatch.
- `computeEnding` scores ordinary futures via Goodwill + Evidence + a narrow Nerve modifier; solo Quiet Way scores animal-first actions exclusively against its four-choice threshold. Meaningful civic choices can therefore matter in prose without changing the fate branch; this can be appropriate, but the dramatic cost and path must be checked.
- Prior editorial rounds already improved Green Ribbon and Quiet Way setbacks, bespoke responses from physically present companions, earned callbacks, and opt-in hangouts. Do **not** overwrite these gains to manufacture additional text.

## Recommended ordering

1. Ship the missing playable archetype perks and guard save compatibility.
2. Establish a better path/causality baseline while writing two genuinely reactive scene pairs per route; prioritize quality rather than adding more WH IDs.
3. Repair solo ecological earned-agency edge cases without making the hermit outcome impossible.
4. Use the new tests to challenge existing ending-signature and companion work; refine currently scheduled `t-013`, `t-021`, and `t-045` instead of duplicating ownership.
5. Conduct a human reading review only when enough truly divergent mid-game material exists to compare; artwork delivery remains with the current art owner.

## Review rubric for future passes

Rate each witnessed route **0–3** on: (1) hard choice with two credible values, (2) surprising but fair success, (3) memorable and non-punitive failure, (4) later playable payoff, (5) distinct companion/solo agency, (6) voice and pacing. Publish the *evidence excerpt and seed* alongside each score. Passing simulator assertions are prerequisites, not player enjoyment evidence. If a route fails `later playable payoff`, prefer a small callback-driven intervention over ten new cards.

**Do not claim new physical playtests, deployed art, creative approval, or public release.**
