# Emperor of Dust — Systems Design v0.2
*User direction, 2026-10-12 PT. This document supersedes conflicting early values in GAME-BIBLE.md; implementation and playtest tuning remain outstanding.*

## Non-negotiable model: Horde versus follower
The **Horde** is a numerical population of armed/organized supporters available to the emperor. Its headcount rises and falls through recruitment, casualties, deserters, alliances, defections and authored events. It is **not** a purse of Support Hoard favor tokens. Keep separate numerical state for Prosperity, Popularity, faction affinity and faction tensions. A **follower** is a persistent, named, character-driven **leader** who commands a contingent of the Horde on a mission. Troop casualties can occur without leader injury; leader injury can occur without deaths among the troops; a rare leader death can have consequences beyond the numbers lost.

Provisional tuning baseline, not approved final balance: begin with 60 Horde members (cap 250, expandable through story); a leader takes 5–20 available members on an assignment according to risk and circumstances; those members and the leader remain unavailable until the mission and return transit finish. Small tasks may require no troops, only one capable follower. A mission must display contingent size, projected troop risk, ETA, deadline, and expected occupation. The Horde never becomes an unbounded chance-bonus currency. Sending too few people raises danger or makes missions unavailable, while stripping the tower leaves defense and unrest vulnerable. Casualties, recruitment and desertion adjust headcount visibly in the night ledger.

## The 100 authored follower pool
Create **100 unique recruitable follower characters**, persistent IDs F001–F100. These are candidates in the recruitable world, **not 100 active commanders on Day 1**. Begin with five active leaders; let the player recruit, unlock, lose, alienate, rehabilitate and encounter others through the campaign. Avoid an artificial tiny roster cap if the player earns many commanders; balance dispatch action economy through affordability, availability and Horde headcount rather than forced arbitrary dismissal.

Each complete FollowerDef needs: ID; name; species and character design; pronouns; home district; recruitment conditions/Prosperity cost; one primary and optional secondary faction association; loyalty/trust values toward empire and factions; traits and flaw; personal stakes; bio and distinctive voice; friends/rivals and arc flags; stat ratings; optional specialization; injury duration and recovery condition; rare death hazard gates; availability, mission and ETA state; and **three required image assets**:
1. **Healthy roster portrait**: readable, distinct face/silhouette at thumbnail size; stable `followers/F001/portrait` identity.
2. **Injured roster portrait**: same recognizable character, distinct expression/pose/bandages or supported visible injury, stable `followers/F001/injured` identity. The roster uses this whenever that follower is injured or recovering; avoid generic overlays as the only depiction.
3. **Splash illustration**: larger dramatic environment/character scene on selection, recruiting and pivotal scenes; stable `followers/F001/splash` identity.
That is **300 delivered initial artworks** for the full 100-character pool. The initial art budget is 3 images per follower, with a **planned flexible ceiling of 1–20 images per follower over time** if specific character arcs justify additional emotional states or action/story portraits. Three is the required baseline for this game; one can be enough for a new side character outside this committed cast. Do not pre-generate 20 portraits for everyone. Future optional variants might be laughing, furious, afraid, triumphant, grieving, suspicious, worn, or specialized scene art. Use a stable character appearance/visual specification and, where supported, original portrait references to keep designs consistent when adding variants, rather than costly independent redesigns. Runtime UI selects by state with safe fallback: injured image if present, otherwise healthy portrait with an explicitly temporary injury indicator; missing splash falls back to healthy portrait until delivery. No broken images. Keep an artwork manifest per follower with `variant`, `assetId`, `jobId`, `promptVersion`, `renderStatus`, `imageUrl`, `referenceIds`, and provenance, tracking planned/queued/rendered/delivered states distinctly. A placeholder, prompt, or queued ArtJob is not a delivered image. Use the Kind Robots durable ArtJob pipeline, traceable art prompts and appropriate Zuzu world canon. Batch artwork with 10-character editorial/visual reviews before scaling to 100. Each character must have more than a name and stat roll: relationships and story choices drive the game.

### Challenge stats and composition
Three clear public core stats, rated **0–5**: **Might** (physical danger, defense, combat, endurance), **Diplomacy** (negotiation, persuasion, court politics, de-escalation), and **Ingenuity** (creative problem solving, invention, inquiry, improvisation). Specialty tags such as medicine, stealth, navigation, magic, logistics or lore give contextual bonuses or authored solutions, not additional confusing top-level bars. Some encounters permit multiple ways to succeed (combat OR negotiation OR ingenious workaround). Personal relationships, faction standing, exhaustion, Horde contingent strength and terrain add situational factors. Distinct combinations of 1–3 leaders may reveal character banter or extra options. Default maximum **three leaders per incident**, zero is an explicit neglect/delegate choice. No leader can be double-booked.

## Clock, straight-line map routes, and incident pacing
Each scripted day has an **in-world clock**, provisionally 07:00–23:00 with an overnight handoff. Some events arrive at authored timestamps or trigger after a previous dispatch; the player can inspect the roster and choose assignments as time progresses. The player must have enough notice to reason about deadlines, without constant real-time reaction pressure: pause/inspect is allowed; fast-forward until next arrival, report, completion, or scheduled event. No softlock when only one injured leader remains.

The **Emperor's Tower sits at the top center** of the map. Ground roads are intentionally **mostly straight radial lines** running from the tower to districts, outposts and landmarks; line junctions may create credible route choices later. The first map need not be a complex pathfinder. Each destination has a predetermined **one-way distance of 1–5 in-world hours**, plus separate configurable incident duration and **full return trip**. Dispatch ETA formula: `departure + outwardTravel + missionDuration + returnTravel`; leader and assigned Horde members only become reusable once home (except explicit local-stationing rules). Route modifications from road damage, patrols, weather, conflicts and items must be authored and visible; don't silently change arrival times. Make in-flight groups visible traveling out, at scene, and returning. The UI should show the earliest available leader and queue upcoming reports by clock time. An incident deadline can expire while leaders are on the road; often **waiting** is an intentional choice.

Balance target for prototyping: 2–5 meaningful incidents/day, staggered enough that 5 starting leaders can handle some but not all without tradeoffs. Vary short nearby and faraway response calls. Pace the day so the player is not forced into needless timer micromanagement; no mandatory time-based click reflexes. Use simulation across 100-day runs, with scarcity, injury cascades and faction wars.

## Injury is common; death is rare
The main personnel setback is **injury**, not permanent death. Assign wounds an explicit in-game recovery duration (initial proposal: minor 1–2 days, moderate 3–5 days, severe 6–10 days; healing specialists and decisions may shorten durations). Wounded leaders **cannot lead assignments for X days**. Rest, travel time, commitment and wounds are distinct states; healing does not imply instant reavailability. Recoveries should create return conversations, grudges and changed behavior.

Permanent follower death is **rare, exceptional, signposted and character-important**. Ordinary failed checks and routine deployments must **not** roll random instant death. Reserve fatalities for clearly telegraphed high-hazard missions, repeated extreme risk choices, sacrificial choices or specific dramatic story branches, with a disclosed or strongly implied consequence, plus aftermath, memorial and affected relationships. Initial balance target: in a competent typical full run, zero or one leader death; catastrophic play can lead to more. This is a **design target to test**, not a hidden fixed quota. A failed challenge normally means injury, troop losses, lost time, damaged trust or resources, not guaranteed death. Injury downtime is the strategic pressure; beloved characters survive often enough to develop arcs.

## Daily intelligence: two truths and a lie
Write **exactly 100 authored sets of morning reports** (one fixed base set per day), coordinated with DAY-PLAN.md. On each day Young Komodo briefs the court with **three discrete intelligence snippets**: **two grounded truths, one deliberately false**. For initial implementation the false report may merely be **wrong flavor** (a narrator/source's mistaken claim or a rumor about an unreliable witness) rather than a lethal gotcha. Its true/false disposition must be author-known and internally consistent for deterministic testing; sources and reliability matter. Avoid designing a lie that punishes the player with irreversible loss without adequate ways to investigate.

Young Komodo supplies plausible, fallible hints in an advisor voice: e.g., "Reports say raiders are gathering near the eastern ford, but the messenger has been wrong about uniforms before." His hints can recommend dispatches, faction opportunities, red flags and route constraints, but do not unilaterally reveal the answer by default. Later day reports reference player choices, faction status and named followers via **authored conditional variants**, while retaining a **fixed authored baseline for each of 100 days**. The lie position rotates and cannot be inferred from a fixed slot. The player may verify, disregard or act on reports; intelligence should reward attention over memorizing arbitrary trivia.

## Persistent relics / Items of Power
Items of Power unlock at **authored milestones and quest lines** and belong to the player's meta-progression collection, surviving the end or loss of a campaign. A **new run equips at most one or two** collected items (initial default one; optional second slot unlocked via progression). They are *active run modifiers*, not a character's disposable backpack and not unlimited stackable buffs. Preserve unlocked ownership across saves/new campaigns, with deterministic starting-loadout record; single-run actions cannot create permanent loss of an earned relic without explicit separately approved mechanic.

**Eye of the Elder Crow**: when equipped, the known false intelligence snippet in each day's three-report briefing is **visibly highlighted as false**. Do not reveal it if the Eye was not equipped for that run, and do not randomize truth labels after load; this is a powerful persistent reward with a meaningful opportunity cost (takes one active slot). Other proposed items for planning and balancing, not automatically awarded:
- **Komodo's Brass Hourglass**: once per day reduces **outbound** travel for one mission by one hour, minimum one hour, but the return still counts; unlock as a Day 10–20 route/clock quest outcome.
- **Bellkeeper's Seal**: one diplomatic incident each day may reduce conflict escalation by one step; obtained through a Hollow Bell peace arc, earliest Days 21–40.
- **The Mole King's Lantern**: reveals one safe alternate response on selected underground/rebel incidents, unlocked by earned Mole Revolutionary trust, earliest Days 41–60.
- **Ember Ward**: one selected leader per day gets one fewer injury recovery day after a nonfatal injury, not immunity to story consequences; found in a risky mission arc, earliest Days 61–80.
- **Crowstep Spurs**: a once-per-day discount of one return-travel hour (minimum one), obtained by negotiating with the Storm Crows late (Days 71–90).
Relic distribution should avoid reliably granting the Eye before the game teaches reading and investigating reports. Tie at least one relic to an early meaningful choice (proposed Eye Day 15–25 Crow/reliability investigation) and spread later pieces across factions. One run cannot reliably collect all relics; alternate actions can offer other rewards. Audit stacking of travel modifiers if a second active slot exists.

## Faction VIII: Mole Revolutionaries
The **Mole Revolutionaries** are an eighth major faction, a network of burrow-builders, tunnel organizers and underground political cells trying to **overthrow the Emperor**. They are **hostile by default**, but **can be won over through genuine change, credible concessions and earned cooperation**, not simply bribed with an affinity point. They have their own internal idealists, defectors and opportunists; a stable reconciliation path may grant tunnels, scouts, recruits or a negotiated constitutional settlement instead of total palace collapse.

They are **explicit enemies of the Mire Mothers / swamp witches**. Their tunnels intersect threatened wetland routes, and their history with missing companions or stolen underground sites provides clues to the witches' deeper malice without making every Mole revolutionary automatically truthful or good. This conflict affects bilateral faction tension, missions and the Day 100 endings, including distinct revolution/reconciliation consequences. Provide named Mole leaders with individual positions, rather than treating species as politics.

## Acceptance for future implementation
- Hundred **authored complete** follower records, **300 final delivered baseline images** (healthy portrait, injured portrait and splash each); modular expansion to **at most 20** images per follower when earned by story needs, not 20 upfront. Keep consistent character identity and track delivered art separately from prompts or queued ArtJobs.
- Each of Days 001–100 has three **prewritten morning reports**, exactly two true and one false, plus plausible Young Komodo hints, rotating lie placement, outcome-linked variants and explicit truth metadata.
- Travel out **1–5 hours**, incident duration, travel back and availability through a pausable/stoppable day clock; no double-booking leaders or Horde members, and mission timers can expire.
- Three clear stats plus specialty context and leader-to-Horde allocations; injuries usually produce **X unavailable days**, death rare/telegraphed.
- Horde headcount conservation with recruitment/casualties and clear explanation; independent Prosperity/Popularity/faction values, **no Support Hoard token mechanic**.
- Earned relic unlocks survive full-game resets; **one or two** equipped per run; Eye consistently marks the false report; acquisition times/branch conditions have explicit design.
- Mostly straight radial travel routes from the Emperor's Tower at **top center**.
- Mole Revolutionaries have hostility, win-over arc, and anti-witch faction conflict. Consequences remain compatible with unresolved Zuzu Book One lore.
- Simulated and human-playtested success rates, wounded-commander downtime, Horde depletion, active relic advantages, bad-end recovery and 100-day completion. The private Admin preview remains a prototype; public /play launch still needs a specific human approval.
