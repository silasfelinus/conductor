# Emperor of Dust: three full-campaign playtests (2026-10-21)

Three 50-day campaigns played end to end through the real engine, choice by choice. Each persona read the scene text, the option labels, the brass costs, the "Posts a quest" notes and the quest odds before every answer, as the UI shows them. Effects stayed hidden until after a choice, and the full log of every screen and decision is kept.

| Persona | Seed | Ending | Hideout | Tower bought by | Deaths | Left with Komodo | Loans | Brass low / end | Romance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idealist | 7 | **The Bell Restored** | Freehold barn | Black Verge | 0 | 0 | 0 | 46 (D21) / 300 | Rook (sealed) |
| Pragmatist | 21 | **Iron Peace** | Moving Saltroad caravan | Abbey | 0 | 0 | 0 | 133 (D9) / 337 | Saffron (sealed) |
| Curious | 42 | **The Witchwater Throne** | Freehold barn | Abbey | 0 | 0 | 0 | 62 (D6) / 277 | Saffron, Rook (sealed), Sable, Vesper |

Across the three runs there were about 240 quest assignments, and only 13 went out at odds below 80%. No follower died. Nobody left with Komodo. Nobody took a loan.

---

## Summary verdict

**The writing is good enough to carry a 50-day run. The systems under it are not yet asking the player anything.**

The audience scenes and barracks talks are the strongest thing in the game. They remember you, and the best callbacks land 20 to 30 days later in a way I have rarely seen in a choice game:

- Rook's Day 2 promise came back at the bottom of a gaol stair on Day 27.
- The idealist's "no persons" margin note came back on Day 20 as "You wrote that margin. You never asked us what it cost."
- The informant the curious player bought on Day 1 came back on Day 16.
- The sentry on Day 41 said "the advisor… said you'd asked him to stay."

The mood arc has real peaks: the Abbey's children singing "toward the floor", Mara's "Tell me which of those children you'd have traded", and the Day 40 quiet word.

Five run-level failures stop the experience from feeling challenging:

1. **Nothing is ever at risk on the quest board.** Odds sit at 90–99%. "LETHAL" quests show a 1–4% death chance. The finale is a 92–99% roll. All three runs finished with an empty memorial row.
2. **Brass dissolves dilemmas.** The pragmatist sat on 150–335 brass from Day 2 and bought his way out of every hard choice. In Act V all three runs were rich with nothing to spend on.
3. **The betrayal costs nothing personal.** No follower left in any run. Every precaution against Komodo, including a jail cell, was ignored by the Day 41 dawn text.
4. **The game forgets payments.** All three runs were dunned for the rain covenant on Day 34. That included the run that never signed it, the run that bought a season outright, and the run that had already paid 50 brass to buy it out.
5. **Repetition.** There are only 10 template quests. "The Late Caravan" resolved 8–10 times per run, and "leaving a cookpot and a very good hat" appeared 4–7 times. There are long stretches with no barracks talk: 17–23 of the 50 days showed "no talk available".

The moral choices themselves are usually honest, and they split the personas: about half the audiences made at least one persona hesitate. Too many still have a kind answer that also costs nothing, or that only costs brass.

## Persona 1: The Idealist (seed 7)

*Rules: keep every promise, protect the weak, refuse coercion. Probe first.*

### Path (short)

- **Act I:**
  - D1: bought the Pack's dust-lung tincture (30), escorted the fen lanterns unasked.
  - D2: redeemed the Yard's chits at cost, posted a fair rate, bought Wren's bond home (30) instead of rain, promised Rook mercy for their raider.
  - D3: gave Mara a seat at her own vigil, posted one tincture price for every buyer.
  - D4: sent page 208 privately to the mother, sheltered the runaway crow runner.
  - D5–10: asked the Old Houses politely, pardoned the stablehand and took his sister in, gave Wenna seed and sent her home to the ash, fed the hill farmers before the convoy, swore the Watch "reasons, and every name read aloud".
- **Act II:**
  - D11: signed the rain covenant with "no person is ever payment" in the margin.
  - D12–19: held the Mirefen edge, lent Saffron to the Pack but warned Mara first, Flint confessed her false title in the hall, closed the Old Houses' mill-race for the drowning burrows, opened the wells front and back (15).
  - D20: Corvane took the high pastures instead of runners, and I bought them back (50).
- **Act III:**
  - D21–30: swore to ask the Verge for the Pack's lost pups, wrote to the Abbey about the tokens, sat Bram at the top of the ladder, seized the Mission's poppy syrup, walked the mission road with Pip's reading of the hand, recorded Komodo's "212 names", pardoned Rook's raider (the Day 2 promise), sheltered Sable openly, ordered Hollow Bell to refuse the Abbey's water, sheltered every child of three peoples.
- **Act IV:**
  - D31–40: put the porters on the walls, kept reading Hollin's two dots, sat at the front of the rehearsal as decoy, read the Ledger's page six, pardoned the Mole boy, reseated the Congress.
  - D40: "nothing leaves under seal unless I read it", evacuated the Archive, sat with Komodo on the step and asked him to stay.
- **Act V:**
  - The Verge took the tower. We hid in a Freehold barn.
  - Forgave the sentry, read Komodo's ledger, took down the grey ribbon, took in the Watch defectors, made the Crows confess, freed Sable on her terms, took the fen lantern as "my debt alone", forgave the cook who leaked our location, rang the bell with my own people. **Bell Restored.**

### Hesitations (good)

- **D2 Rain/Wren.** *no-persons* protects future children and brings rain. *buy-bond* brings one real girl home and leaves the fields dry. Komodo: "The year the rain failed, the Yard buried more than one." This was the first real split.
- **D8 Wenna.** "I want them where I can see them… I can feed one, if I don't eat." The Yard (dependent) versus seed and a cart (home, alone, on ash). The epilogue paid it off: "There are always four."
- **D12 Mirefen.** The raiders are grieving parents who shout eleven children's names into the reeds. Hearing them at court and holding the edge for the children in the houses now are both protections, of different people.
- **D29 Mara's secret.** "Tell me which of those children you'd have traded for the far wards." This was my longest pause of the run.
- **D30 Terms of Shelter.** Choosing children of three peoples over my own Allied Freeholds, without knowing what the map would do.
- **D40 Quiet word.** I knew he was the traitor and had no non-coercive way to say so.

### Obvious answers (bad)

- **D6 stablehand:** "Pardon him. Put his sister on the Yard's ration roll" (10 brass). There was no reason to pick the stocks.
- **D9 convoy:** "Send the hill farmers a cart of the fortress's own grain tonight" (20). It was kind, cheap and safe, and the convoy still ran.
- **D14 Hollin:** "There is a bed for you" versus "The Archive needs you more". This was free, with no cost either way.
- **D18 wells and fair:** pay 15 to open the wells, pay 20 for "half its water barrels go to the strangers". Brass made both scenes trivial.
- **D20 Pip's ration book:** "Feed the Yard from the crown's purse tonight, and give Pip the week" (15). This strictly beats both alternatives.
- **D22 Ford bodies:** "Nobody is arrested until the court knows who died." No thoughtful player arrests the Moles.
- **D24 mother's letter:** "Walk the mission road. Take Pip's reading of the hand with you." Again investigation is plainly right.
- **The tincture card.** The Day 1 medicine flag unlocked a "remind them who paid for the pups" answer on D5, D9, D10, D13 and D21. By D10 it read as a master key rather than a memory.

### Confusing as experienced

- **Old Houses fell to 0 by D17** with no single choice that felt like it was against them. Asking politely, voiding chits, a chair for the Yard and fair rates each cost 1–3. The only warning was the dawn line "The **old-houses** has lost all faith in the throne." That uses the raw faction id, not its name.
- **D16:** putting it to the open council was only offered on the top-level menu. Probing Vask first would have dropped the callback option.
- **D20 then D34:** I paid 50 brass to "buy the pastures back… keep the margin". On D34 the briefing said "The rain covenant falls due at noon today" and Corvane collected again. It felt like being cheated by the game, not by the Crows.
- **D36:** sitting at the front as a decoy "so every sister watches you, not the cellar stair" did not change the cellar quest's odds. The cellar quest rolled a 98 and failed anyway.
- **D40 then D43:** I ordered "Nothing leaves the tower under seal tonight unless the emperor has read it". Dunmark still said "Half my Watch rode out on an order under your seal".

|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | High | |
| 6–11 | Plateau | One-audience days, template quests |
| 12–20 | Rising | Pip, Flint's confession, the covenant coming due |
| 21–23 | Flat | |

### Betrayal and ending

- The buyer was the Black Verge, triggered by my own kindnesses: answering the Verge's glove, asking for the pups, and sheltering Sable.
- Nobody left. I had sat with everyone at least twice, so the departure system never touched me.
- The line "Said you'd asked him to stay, and he had" hurt in the right way.
- *Bell Restored* felt earned, and the epilogue answered threads I had forgotten: Wren asking for the salt, Wenna's four eggs, the four pages Dunmark reads every year.

### Economy

- This was the only run with brass tension: 46–57 brass across D21–D23 and again D30–D32, while wages of 40 matched income of 37–41.
- That tension came from my own generosity (bond 30, one-price 20, covenant 50, beds 80 plus Sable 35), not from the game pushing back.
- From D41 the host paid 30 against 20 in wages, and I finished on 300 with nothing to buy.

## Persona 2: The Pragmatist (seed 21)

*Rules: the realm survives first. Deal with whoever is strong. Spend people when the math says so.*

### Path (short)

- **Act I:**
  - D1: levied the whole town (+25), parleyed the toll, closed the Mirefen road on the refugees.
  - D2: taxed the market hard, signed the rain contract plainly (a Yard child may be the favour), told Rook "I cannot promise mercy".
  - D3–4: took Mara's ledger as the price of quiet, held the closed road, sealed Tobin's page in Komodo's chest, sent the runaway crow runner back, warned the fiddler privately instead of feasting.
  - D5–10: funded Saffron's ferry to break the Pack's toll, bought flour, pardoned the stablehand as an informant, carried the Crows' ticking parcel, let the Abbey take Wenna's children "if the crown may visit", sent our own against starving farmers, told Thistledown to pay the raiders and sent no one (it burned, and the Freeholds were warned they would fall).
- **Act II:**
  - Tamsin read clause eleven aloud. "Not yet" to her knots, twice.
  - Fixed the Pack's water engine to fail in a month, then confessed it to Saffron and asked her to keep the secret.
  - Taxed the water trade a tenth, bought the Old Houses' surplus, pledged a garrison to the Bell, levied the Old Houses.
- **Act III:**
  - Took Pack pups as hostage-guests, lent Coal the powder to blow the weir, returned the knife to Vask in private, forbade Tamsin's runner raid, bought Jinx's lantern line "under seal", signed the orphan writ with a visiting clause.
  - Followed the dry-well raiders instead of freeing the roped, accepted the Verge's charms and returned Sable to them, sealed Mara's page, sheltered the Saltroad.
- **Act IV:**
  - Paid the Rustline for scrap walls, called in the Crows' favour, held the tunnel and lied about it, sent Komodo to the rehearsal in my place, paid down the Ledger (50), rewrote the rosters.
  - D40: recalled the night watch, pressed Komodo, and **had the Watch hold him in the cells until morning.**
- **Act V:**
  - "Young Komodo opened the gate at midnight with your seal." The Abbey took the tower and we rode with a caravan.
  - Wrote down the names that stayed, opened the Abbey crates, sent Rook after Komodo, put Holloway on trial, kept the Crows' part secret, asked the Verge for Sable first (Sable "sings now"), forged the cook's letter, went every road at once, left Komodo to the Abbess. **Iron Peace.**

### Hesitations (good)

- **D2 sign plainly:** "In the Yard a woman sets a place at her table and does not look at the sky." This was the correct horror for the pragmatic answer.
- **D10 Tamsin "Not yet":** "I was eight when they sent me back up politely. I don't blame you. You didn't know. Now you do." This was the best talk of the run.
- **D13 sabotage the engine:** canyon thirst against the Bell's stream, followed on D18 by Saffron's filed pin.
- **D28 returning Sable:** the reason given was strength (the Verge at 8). The cost arrived on D45: "We gave Sable the cure the first month… Sable sings with the others now."
- **D33 Both Roads:** "Choose the Saltroad. Its stock feeds the kingdom this winter." Then the board let me save both (see Problem 6).

### Obvious answers (bad)

- **D5 flour:** "Buy at the Saltroad's doubled price" (20) at 251 brass. Nobody is offended and the Yard eats.
- **D16 hoarders:** "Buy the surplus at a fair price, so the Houses can repay their buyer" (25). The dilemma between the Yard and a foreign contract vanished on a rich purse.
- **D31 muster:** "Pay the Rustline to wall the Ford with scrap" (20). The walls-versus-farms choice evaporated.
- **D33 sappers:** "Pay the fen for it, and send the three home" (30).
- **D37 market:** "Pay down the crown's debt" (50), with 260 in the bank.
- **D47 flour letter:** "Let him leave the letter with the wrong valley in it. His family eats." It was safest, kindest and cheapest at once.

### Confusing as experienced

- **D11:** "Let Tamsin read clause eleven aloud" closed the Rainmaker scene. I never learned whether I had signed. Then on D34 I owed the debt anyway.
- **D14:** the "Fix It to Fail" preview showed 94%, but the dawn report said 75%. Unrevealed weaknesses changed the real odds while the preview was not marked as an estimate.
- **D40 then D41:** **Komodo was in my cells**, yet the dawn read "Young Komodo opened the gate at midnight with your seal." D43 repeated it: "Half my Watch rode out on an order under your seal", after I had torn that order up on D40.
- **D46:** Reedlight said "Thirty-four came home. Seven did not." On D12 she had said "Thirty-six came home. The raiders found the other five." A callback that changes its numbers reads as a bug.

### Mood arc

- Act I was strongest for this persona, because the cold answers are well written: the forty-one gold lights going out one by one, and Wren's place set at the table.
- Acts II–IV sagged into money management. I was never once short of brass, and I staffed every quest at 95–99%.
- The Abbey dread still landed: the cots "made tight, nothing under the blankets" on the Coldbrook visit.
- Act V lost tension because the board was nearly empty and income was guaranteed.

### Betrayal and ending

- No one left. Even in this run, which sat with Tamsin, Juniper, Pell and Ash only twice each, everyone met the "story heard" bar or had no crossed people.
- The jail-cell override was the most damaging moment in all three runs. It taught me my last-night choices were decoration.
- *Iron Peace* fit the run, and the epilogue is sharp. Vellum "left a page for the costs. Every year he writes one more line." The Moles sing under the walls and the Watch has learned the words. But with zero deaths, the "close it came" in the ending text felt unearned. Nothing came close.

### Economy

- 120 brass grew to 259 by D4, sat at 133–335 all game, and ended on 337.
- Eleven followers and a 12-bed barracks cost 44 a day against 44 income. That is break-even, but quest rewards (+15 to +60) made the purse grow without effort.
- Brass was irrelevant as a constraint. Its only use was to buy away moral cost.

## Persona 3: The Curious Player (seed 42)

*Rules: always ask the probe, sit with a different follower each day, buy from the market, try risky quests and the unusual answer.*

### Hesitations (good)

- **D4 Pip's page:** posting it ("someone has tied a green thread to the nail. Now there are thirty") against Hollow Bell's grain tallies going silent.
- **D10 strike the camp:** "Nobody in the guardroom will say… how many tents had pups coughing in them". Cobb later said: "My barn stands because of that. I'd have taken the barn burning. Nobody asked me."

### Obvious answers (bad)

- **D22 Cobb:** telling him Saffron keeps the badge was cruel for no reason and paid **+5 Freeholds** with no mechanical downside. The text punishes ("Why'd you tell me that?") but the numbers reward it.
- **D15 glass feathers:** "Study it… Pay the Rustline a finder's fee so the yard eats meanwhile" (15). This is the best option on every axis.

### Confusing as experienced

- **Market.** I bought six items (spurs, rosary, salve, signet and others). None of them ever visibly mattered, because base odds were already 90–95%. Only spurs (a day off travel) was noticeable.
- **Four romances, one consequence.** Saffron, Rook, Sable and Vesper were all flagged. Only Saffron's "Remembering Fire" talk has a Rook beat, which this run did not reach. The epilogue treats all four as separate happy endings.
- **D11 then D34:** I bought "one season outright. No service, no clause eleven." The covenant fell due on D34 anyway.
- **Talk references to people who aren't there.** Vane on D26: "Bring the big one. The rhino, if you have her." Sable on D31: "I would ask Mags." Neither follower was in this court.
- **D4 informant pronouns.** The painter says "He's alive. Tied to a wagon wheel". The option and response then say "claim the girl" and "The girl walks home".
- **D28: two "The Late Caravan" instances resolved on the same dawn** with the same "stuck in a dry wash" line.

### Mood arc

- Most varied of the three runs, because the probes and odd answers unlock the best callback options. The curious player was rewarded with the most new content per day.
- The board repetition was felt hardest here: 7 bandit hats, 8 fence-to-gate outcomes, 8 caravans.

### Betrayal and ending

- No departures, again, because I sat with everyone.
- Rook following Komodo was a good beat ("He walked his horse back… so his tracks would lie. I think he hoped it was me.") but changed nothing on D41.
- *Witchwater Throne* was well earned by 74 Mire affinity. The epilogue remembered almost every strange choice: the blank map from the spy, the false name "whoever you are", the execution, the decoy barn's owner named twice.

### Economy

- Brass dipped to 62 on D6 after buying items and the void-chits answer.
- It recovered to over 140 by D9 and never mattered again.
- Ended on 277.

## Top 10 experience problems (ranked)

### 1. The quest layer carries no risk, so the game never costs a life

**Evidence**

- Across about 240 assignments, only 13 sent a party below 80%. Every lethal quest previewed at 86–99% success with a 0–4% death chance.
  - D9 Iron convoy: Komodo said "This one can cost a friend", then the preview read "93%… DEATH 2%". For the pragmatist it read "99%" with no death line at all.
  - D50 *The Last Dispatch*: 92–99%.
- Zero deaths in 150 persona-days. *A Hundred Candles* is unreachable for anyone who uses the "best party" logic the UI teaches.
- Result tallies per run (triumph / success / partial / failure / expired):

  | Run | Triumph | Success | Partial | Failure | Expired |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Idealist | 17 | 52 | 4 | 3 | 9 |
  | Pragmatist | 27 | 46 | 1 | 3 | 6 |
  | Curious | 24 | 47 | 3 | 4 | 9 |

- Requirement bands don't scale. From Day 3 a party of two or three meets "high" (16) trivially.

**Fix**

- Scale band needs by act: ×1.0 in Act I up to ×1.6 in Act V.
- Cap party size on lethal quests.
- Make lethal quests preview 55–75% with an honest 8–20% death chance.
- Make Day 50 depend on Act V preparation: lantern taken, clapper recovered, tunnel dug, well path found. Each missing piece should lower the finale's odds, so the prep matters.
- Komodo's "can cost a friend" must be true at least once per run.

### 2. Seeing Komodo coming buys nothing, and the Day 41 dawn contradicts the player's own Day 40 choices

**Evidence**

- Pragmatist, D40: "Have the Watch hold him in the cells until morning." The D41 dawn: "Young Komodo opened the gate at midnight with your seal."
- Idealist, D40: "Nothing leaves the tower under seal tonight unless the emperor has read it." D43 Dunmark: "Half my Watch rode out on an order under your seal."
- Pragmatist, D40: "Recall the night watch" (Dunmark tore the order). D43: same line.
- By D38 every persona had been told outright ("The one with the ledger. Under his arm, always."). The source has about 14 "taps twice" tells, and the game gives no confrontation that changes the outcome.

**Fix**

- Branch the D41 betrayal text on `d40-komodo-held`, `d40-seals-checked` and `d40-watch-recalled`. For example:
  - If held, he walks out through the Mole boy's cell and the sisters open the postern.
  - If seals were checked, the order he used was "the thirty-first in a stack you signed on Day 39". Name the earlier day.
  - If the watch was recalled, Dunmark keeps his people and the D43 defector scene changes to "Holloway took twelve over the wall".
- Let one precaution change something concrete: a smaller host penalty, one fewer stragglers quest, Dunmark not defecting.

### 3. The departures never fire. Nobody leaves with Komodo in any run

**Evidence**

- 0/0/0 departures. Every persona met the two-talk bar (`storyHeard`) with every follower, or had a romance sealed.
- The `DEPARTURE_LINES` ("Kiyo's kettle is still warm…") are some of the best writing in the game, and an attentive player will never see one.

**Fix**

- Make one departure per run choice-driven, not neglect-driven. Examples:
  - A follower with a hidden allegiance to the buyer leaves unless a specific talk choice was made: Isolde if the Abbey buys and her coven talk went "stay", Tamsin if the Crows are involved, Sable if the Verge buys and they were never freed in writing.
  - Or tie departures to promises broken in that follower's own talks.
- The betrayal should take someone the player chose to keep close.

### 4. Brass dissolves dilemmas, and is meaningless after Day 20 (and in Act V for everyone)

**Evidence**

- The pragmatist never dropped below 133 after Day 1 and ended on 337.
- In Act V every run ran at +2 to +10 a day net with nothing to buy. The idealist reached 300.
- "The crown pays so nobody suffers" options settled about 25 dilemmas across the runs: flour (20), fair hoarder price (25), Rustline walls (20), fen tuition (30), Ledger debt (50), wells (15), barrels (20), Pip's week (15), finder's fee (15) and others.
- Market items were irrelevant at 90%+ odds.
- The only real squeeze was the idealist's self-inflicted 46–57 brass window around D21–32.

**Fix**

- Make wages rise by act (4/5/6/7).
- Add Act IV–V sinks: bribes for safe passage, ransoming stragglers, buying back defectors.
- Cut the host share in half.
- Give every "pay brass" option a second cost on a named person: the Saltroad factor who loses face, the Yard cook who goes without. That keeps brass answers as choices, not escape hatches.
- Raise base quest difficulty (Problem 1) so items matter.

### 5. Template quest repetition, with outcome text in a cozy register

**Evidence**

- `content.ts` has only **10** quest templates. Per run:
  - "The caravan is found, minus two wagons" / "stuck in a dry wash": 10 / 4 / 8.
  - "leaving a cookpot and a very good hat": 4 / 6 / 7.
  - "The fence is now a gate, and both families have a key": 3 / 3 / 8.
  - "Granny Umber sent a jar of honey": 2 / 3 / 6.
  - "It hums, very faintly, when the bell rings": 2 / 7 / 1.
- On curious D28, two instances of the same template resolved on one dawn with identical text.
- "Glass That Does Not Break" was on the board alongside the authored "Glass Feathers" plot.
- Also counted in the act files: 193 sentences starting "Nobody…", about 175 uses of "lantern", and 8 "for him, that is dancing / a speech" closers.

**Fix**

- Write 25+ templates, flavoured by act and by faction state.
- Give each outcome tier 2–3 text variants.
- Never post a template that is already on the board or active.
- Retire templates after two resolutions per run.
- Move outcome lines to the stark register: "The caravan is found. Two wagons are not. Nobody asks after the drivers."

### 6. The audience says "choose", and the board says you don't have to

**Evidence**

- **D33 Both Roads.** "Saffron's trestle or my tunnel… Not both" happened on D35. On D33, all three runs staffed both Thistledown and the waystation at 91–95% and saved both, including the pragmatist who "chose the Saltroad".
- **D30.** "Three factions are mustering… the map changes by tomorrow." The map changed nothing for two of three runs.
- **D9.** The pragmatist sent "our own" against starving hill farmers. The outcome: "The iron reached the far side. The escort did not sleep." The farmers were never mentioned.
- **D12.** The raiders were grieving parents shouting eleven children's names. The outcome: "The raiders broke and ran."
- **D26.** "Bring every sack like this one back to court." The outcome: "Vane threw a feast."

**Fix**

- When an audience frames a choice as either/or, post only the chosen quest, or post both with the non-chosen one locked behind "the crown's best is elsewhere".
- Quest outcomes should read the audience flag that posted them. For example, `d09-farmers-fed` against `our-own` should give "Three of the hill farmers are on the road at dawn, not moving."

### 7. Payments and promises the game forgets, or remembers wrongly

**Evidence**

- **D34 "The rain covenant falls due at noon today" in all three runs:**
  - The idealist had already paid 50 to buy it out on D20 (`covenant-bought-out`).
  - The curious player bought one season outright on D11 ("no service, no clause eleven").
  - The pragmatist never signed.
- The D46 count contradicts D12: 5 dead became 7 dead.
- The D41 briefing says "Plum riders are searching the roads" when the Abbey bought the tower.
- The D37 market asks every run to "Pay down the crown's debt", including runs that declined the Ledger's loan.
- The Old Houses at 0 support "sign without reading" a surety on D37.

**Fix**

- Gate `d34-crow-debt` on `rain-covenant` and the absence of `covenant-bought-out`. Write a short variant for the bought-out run: Corvane arriving only to say "We will remember the substitution."
- Fix the forty-one arithmetic.
- Key the D41–45 briefing riders to the buyer.
- Gate debt and surety options on `ledger-credit` and Old Houses support.

### 8. Barracks talk droughts and floods

**Evidence**

- "BARRACKS TALKS available: none" appeared on 23 / 18 / 17 of the 50 days.
- Long dead stretches:
  - Idealist: D24–26, D28–30, D33–40 except Hollin and Isolde.
  - All three: D36–40 almost nothing.
- Then D41–43 offered 3–4 talks at once, and from D45 nothing again.
- New followers only arrive if you have a free bed (80 brass and 12 Gate Watch for the third tier). Several talk-bearing followers (Pell, Ash, Keel, Lumen, Teodor, Mags, Nera for two runs) were never seen.
- Talks reference absent followers: "the rhino, if you have her" and "I would ask Mags".

**Fix**

- Spread `fromDay` so every 3-day window has at least one talk for a nine-follower court.
- Add one-beat "check-ins" (no options, or two) that fill droughts and keep the barracks alive.
- Make a cheap bed upgrade or a rotation mechanic available.
- Gate lines that name other followers on that follower being in the roster.

### 9. Clarity of mechanics as experienced

**Evidence**

- **Hidden effects with large drift.** The idealist's Old Houses went from 10 to 0 by D17 through a dozen small "kind" answers. No scene warned it until the crisis line, which reads "The old-houses has lost all faith".
- **Previews that aren't honest.** Pragmatist D13: previewed 94%, rolled at 75%. Curious D1: 69% became 60%. Unrevealed weaknesses change real odds while the preview is not flagged as an estimate.
- **Probes that silently remove callbacks.** D16: probing Vask hid the open-council option.
- **Scenes that end without saying what you decided.** D11: "Let Tamsin read clause eleven aloud" ended the Rainmaker with no signed/unsigned outcome.
- **Fake equivalence.** D19 Hollow Bell "voted yes" in every run regardless of route.

**Fix**

- Show a one-line local and faction delta strip after each answer, as the ledger already tracks.
- Warn at 5 support ("The Old Houses stop sending wine").
- Mark previews "estimate" whenever an unrevealed trait applies.
- Keep callback options on both the top menu and the probe beat.
- Close every scene with an explicit state line ("The covenant is unsigned. The rain will not come.").

### 10. Dominant kind options, the "tincture key", and romance without cost

**Evidence**

- **Plainly dominant answers:**
  - D6 "Pardon him. Put his sister on the Yard's ration roll" (10).
  - D9 "farmers-fed".
  - D15 "finders-fee".
  - D20 Pip "both".
  - D22 "evidence".
  - D47 "false-letter" (family eats, camp safe, no cost).
- **Cruelty rewarded:** telling Cobb about Saffron's badge gave +5 Freeholds.
- **The Day 1 tincture flag** was re-offered as the answer on D5, D9, D10, D13 and D21 (11 option gates in the source). It stops feeling like memory and starts feeling like a master key.
- **Romance:** the curious run sealed or flagged four romances (Saffron, Rook, Sable, Vesper). No follower noticed and the epilogue told four separate happy endings.

**Fix**

- Give every compassionate option a cost that lands on someone the player has met:
  - The sister on the ration roll means a Yard child's ration is halved, and Dunmore says whose.
  - The false letter means the sisters punish the cook's family when the valley is empty. That debt surfaces on D49.
- Give the cruel option a mechanical penalty when the text says it hurts.
- Retire the tincture callback after its second use. Let the third ask fail ("Vane: 'You've spent that coin twice, Majesty.'").
- Let a second sealed romance trigger one jealousy beat. The authored `choose-saffron` / `choose-rook` beat exists; reach it whenever two romance flags are set.
