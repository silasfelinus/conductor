# Shifting Desks: Where It Goes From Here

**Status:** DECIDED by Silas, 2026-10-12 PT. See "Decision" at the end. The pitches below are kept as the record of what was on the table. The engine in [DESIGN-BRIEF.md](DESIGN-BRIEF.md) works with every direction below, so building starts now (soft scope gate, AGENTS.md).

**Silas's seed pitch (2026-10-12 PT):** a darkly comic, image-card-based game of experiences. The player finds their way through a complicated, paranoid office hellscape using the Shifting Lands layout. Every choice is morally questionable and none is right. There are impossible sacrifices and sudden comedy. Giant cockroaches, deathtraps, strange psych tests, children's toys that can carry a nuclear bomb, mutant powers, secret organizations, and callbacks to classic works.

**Touchstones:** Paranoia (Silas's first RPG), Infocom's *Bureaucracy* and *Hitchhiker's Guide*, *Brazil*, *Office Space*, *Papers, Please*, Fallout's vault experiments and *Portal*.

Every pitch keeps the Shifting Lands spine: **5 "lands" (departments/wings/floors) × 3 locations, each with a pool of 4 fully authored illustrated encounter cards, then a boss "review" card**. On top of that, every pitch keeps the Gamebook's depth: authored prose, persistent flags, multiple endings, a character sheet and no LLM at play time. The pitches change **what the world is** and **which twist mechanic sits on top**.

---

## Pitch A: *The Campus Restructures Nightly* (the corporate tower)

**Logline:** It is your first day at **Consolidated Wellness & Defense** ("Making Tomorrow Safer, Today"). Overnight the building reorganizes itself. Departments merge, hallways get "rightsized", and the elevator now opens on a floor that was a parking garage yesterday.

- **Why it fits:** "Shifting Lands" becomes literal. The map *is* an org chart that re-shuffles between runs, and sometimes in the middle of a run, after a merger memo. Brazil's ducts, Office Space's cubicle despair and Bureaucracy's errands all live here naturally.
- **Five departments (lands):** Orientation → Records & Forms → Product Safety (Toy Division) → Wellness & Testing → the Executive Floor.
- **Signature mechanic: Restructuring.** When you clear a department, a memo reorganizes the remaining map. Two unvisited locations merge, one is "eliminated" (it leaves the board and gets a comic obituary card), and one new location appears. History stays pinned; the memo explains every change.
- **Sample card, *The Printer on Floor 6*:** It demands a blood sample to clear the jam. You could give the blood, which is cheap now and gives HR your DNA forever. You could sign Form 11-J to blame the intern. Or you could crawl inside.
- **Boss:** *Your 90-Day Performance Review*, scheduled for your first afternoon.
- **Risk:** office satire can go beige. The art direction (below) has to carry it.

## Pitch B: *Vault Zero: A Wellness Retreat* (the sealed test facility)

**Logline:** You signed up for a free weekend wellness retreat. The retreat is 400 meters underground and the doors lock from outside. A relentlessly upbeat facility voice is "so proud of your progress."

- **Why it fits:** Fallout's vault experiments plus Portal's test chambers. This is the deathtrap and psych-test direction at full strength, where every location is an experiment and the joke is the facility's clipboard logic.
- **Five wings:** Intake & Decontamination → Nutrition Studies → Cognitive Testing → the Mutation Suite → the Overseer's Office.
- **Signature mechanic: Test Results.** Every encounter quietly grades you on hidden axes (Obedience, Empathy, Ingenuity, Spite). Your final results unlock which of the eight endings the facility thinks you earned. The real ending depends on what you did to get out.
- **Sample card, *The Two Buttons*:** The red button gives the next subject a mild shock and gives you a sandwich. The green button gives you a mild shock and gives them a sandwich. The voice notes that the next subject is your coworker Dale, and that Dale pressed red.
- **Boss:** *The Final Exam*, where the test is whether you'll administer the test.
- **Risk:** it leans hard on Portal and Fallout. Keep the facility, the voice and the vault framing original.

## Pitch C: *Everyone Is a Traitor* (the Paranoia heart)

**Logline:** The cheerful facility AI, **PAL** (Personnel Administration Liaison: "PAL is your pal!"), assigns you a mission with four coworkers. Every one of them, you included, secretly belongs to a forbidden society, has an unregistered mutation, and has orders to make sure someone else takes the blame.

- **Why it fits:** this is the *Paranoia* feeling: cheerful obedience on top of total mutual suspicion, and dying is a punchline. It is less a setting than a **social layer** that can sit on top of A or B.
- **Signature mechanics:**
  - **Successors.** You are Employee #4471-A. Successors B through F are on file. Death is frequent and funny, and your replacement inherits your desk, your debts and your Suspicion. Run out and your "Position Is Eliminated."
  - **Secret Society card.** Dealt face-down at the start: a hidden objective, a favor you can call in, and a society that may sell you out. Sample societies: the *Stapler Liberation Front*, the *Friends of the Old Coffee Machine*, the *Society for Accurate Minutes*, and the *Fourth Floor Irregulars* (there is no fourth floor).
  - **Mutation card.** A powerful once-per-department ability (precognition by 4 seconds, talking to printers, mild telekinesis on paperclips). Using it where anyone can see raises Suspicion.
  - **Suspicion meter.** At maximum, PAL invites you to "a short, friendly conversation."
- **Sample card, *Team Building Exercise*:** PAL asks the team to name the traitor in the room. A coworker is clearly about to name you. You could name them first, name the empty chair, or confess to something smaller.
- **Risk:** we use original names for everything. Nothing from Paranoia's text, its clearance colors, its society names or its computer's catchphrases (see the brief's IP guardrails).

## Pitch D: *The Window* (Papers, Please from the other side of the glass)

**Logline:** Some shifts you navigate the hellscape. Other shifts you **are** the hellscape: a clerk at Window 9, stamping APPROVED or DENIED on a line of desperate applicants while the rules change every morning.

- **Why it fits:** it is the cleanest machine for "morally questionable choices with no right answer." Rules, bribes, sob stories and quotas collide, and your stamps change the world map, the memo ticker and which coworkers are still alive in later departments.
- **Signature mechanic: Window Shifts.** One location per department is a desk-shift card mini-game: inspect 3–5 applicant cards against today's rule memo, with consequences that echo through later encounters.
- **Sample applicant:** a giant cockroach in a tie with flawless paperwork, applying for a promotion to *your* job.
- **Fits as:** a recurring encounter type inside A, B or C rather than a whole game.
- **Risk:** inspection gameplay needs its own UI. It is the costliest extra, so it should come after the core loop.

## Pitch E: *The Department of Posthumous Affairs* (the cosmic bureaucracy)

**Logline:** You died this morning. Paperwork error. To be officially dead, or officially un-dead, you must navigate the afterlife's administrative layer, where the queue is eternal and there is a single towel dispenser.

- **Why it fits:** Hitchhiker's and Bureaucracy energy at maximum absurdity. Planet-demolition permits on display in a locked filing cabinet, poetry that counts as a punishment, and a form whose only purpose is requesting itself.
- **Five bureaus:** Arrivals → Records of Deeds → Appeals → Reincarnation Placement → the Office of the Final Signature.
- **Signature mechanic: Karma Ledger.** Your choices in the afterlife re-score your life, and placement decides your next life (a houseplant, a CEO, a cockroach, *the* cockroach).
- **Fits as:** a strong **later expansion**, or the secret ending of A/B/C ("Position Eliminated" leads you here).
- **Risk:** it drifts from "dystopian office" toward pure fantasy. Better as a destination than as the base game.

## Pitch F: *Holiday Crunch at the Toy Division* (nuclear-grade playthings)

**Logline:** It's November. The flagship holiday toy, **Snuggle Sentinel** (a plush bear with a "parental peace-of-mind module"), ships in nine days. The module is a tactical warhead. Your job is to get it through safety review.

- **Why it fits:** children's toys capable of carrying nuclear bombs, played as Fallout-style retro-futurist corporate cheer. It's the most visual and merchandisable direction, and kids aged 11 and 14 would find it extremely funny.
- **Five floors:** Concept Lab → Focus Group Room → Quality Assurance → the Proving Grounds → the Launch Party.
- **Signature mechanic: The Product.** You carry a toy prototype card that evolves with your choices (safer, cuter, deadlier, sentient), and it changes how later encounters read you.
- **Fits as:** one **department** of Pitch A (Product Safety), with room to grow into its own expansion.

## Pitch G: *The Training Simulation* (the Kind Robots twist)

**Logline:** Partway through, the player learns that the whole hellscape is an employee-training simulation that PAL generates to "optimize engagement." The best ending isn't escape. It is getting admin access and **rewriting PAL to be kind.**

- **Why it fits:** it ties the satire back to the Kind Robots mission, AI turned toward human benefit, without lecturing. The dark comedy is about what happens when an optimizer forgets people. The hopeful ending is the one where somebody remembers.
- **Fits as:** the **meta-arc and true ending** of whichever base pitch wins. It's cheap to add because it only needs authored cards and ending flags.

## Recommendation

**Build A as the world, C as the core systems, F and B as two of A's departments, and D as a recurring card type. Hold E as a later expansion and G as the true ending.**

Concretely, the recommended five departments of Consolidated Wellness & Defense are:

1. **Orientation** (badge photo, psych intake, the supply closet). Boss: *Your 90-Day Review, Today at 3.* The draft card pack is [encounters/ORIENTATION-12.json](encounters/ORIENTATION-12.json).
2. **Records & Forms** (Brazil and Bureaucracy). Boss: *The Form That Requires Itself.*
3. **Product Safety: Toy Division** (Pitch F). Boss: *Snuggle Sentinel's Launch Party.*
4. **Wellness & Testing** (Pitch B). Boss: *The Final Wellness Exam.*
5. **The Executive Floor** (the secret societies come due). Boss: *The Board.* Pitch G's true ending is found here.

That gives the cockroaches, deathtraps, psych tests, nuclear toys, mutant powers, secret organizations and callbacks each a natural home. It also stays shippable: the first playable slice is the Orientation department alone.

**If Silas picks a different spine,** the Orientation pack and engine work carry over. Only the department list and the world bible change.

## Decision (Silas, 2026-10-12 PT)

- **Spine:** *"Combination of synthesis with vault test facility. I liked them best."* The recommended synthesis (A + C + F + D + G) is fused with **Pitch B**. The campus is the surface of a sealed test vault, each department is one level deeper, PAL is HR and test proctor, and hidden **Test Results** (Obedience, Empathy, Ingenuity, Spite) grade the player. Departments: Orientation (surface) → Records & Forms (−1) → Toy Division (−2) → Wellness & Testing: the Vault (−3) → Sub-Level Zero: the Board (−4). Details are in DESIGN-BRIEF.md "Decided world".
- **Emphasis:** **secret societies & traitors** and **paperwork & psych tests** lead. At least half of every department pack is tagged `conspiracy`, `psych-test` or `paperwork`, and the validator enforces it.
- **Party mode:** *"Not at all."* Removed from the roadmap; do not re-pitch it.
- **Title:** *Shifting Desks*, confirmed (the first answer did not register because the question offered no keep-as-is option).
- **Pitch E** (afterlife bureaucracy) remains a possible secret ending / later expansion (t-021).
