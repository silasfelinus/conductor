# Revision questions — for dialogue with Silas, not for fixing

Captured 2026-07-05 from Silas's own words, so the conversation has an anchor when he
wants to have it. **Nothing here is a task.** No agent proposes edits, drafts prose, or
"resolves" any of this on its own. The book is Silas's craftsmanship; these are the
questions he said he wants to talk through as the project evolves.

## Context, in his framing

Six years of work, released in a state he was — and still is — dissatisfied with. He
caught many errors himself between the first and second printings, without a
proofreader. The book has felt like an albatross: he loves it, but the unresolved
questions below have kept him from marketing it or recommending it to people. His hope
for this iteration: to feel proud of it again.

## The big one: the discarded character (magical negro pattern)

Silas introduced a Black character who fits the magical negro trope and is then
discarded. This has weighed on him more than anything else in the book. The three
directions he named:

1. **Different origin.** Give the character a researched origin from another culture,
   or promote one of the existing under-used characters into the role. Silas's own
   label for this option: "a bit cowardly."
2. **An iteration of Arlecchino.** Make the character an iteration of his father
   Arlecchino — folding him into the book's existing commedia dell'arte mythology.
   Silas called this "possibly a stronger one."
3. **Keep him, and face it on the page.** Keep the character, but only after the
   issue is addressed off-page and on: the character's Blackness and the magical
   negro role discussed openly with the main character (and the audience as
   surrogate).

He explicitly does not know which to choose. When he raises it, the useful posture is
honest dialogue — trade-offs of each, what each does to the book's mythology and to
readers — not a recommendation delivered as a verdict.

## The other named concerns

- **Third-act changes** he has deliberated on for a while (specifics to come from him).
- **Tone:** some parts feel too preachy; the heroes too noble; the origins forced.
- **Moments of male gaze** (minor but noted; the cultural-awareness editorial pass
  should catch specifics).
- **Geography hand-wave:** Venice sinking into the ocean, when Venice is not actually
  connected to the ocean (lagoon, not open sea).
- **Period drift:** the story takes place just before cell phones were everywhere,
  which now reads as a period piece. Sidestep, embrace, or address?

## Standing boundaries

- AI's role is editor-brain only: impressions, notes, catches, questions. Silas may
  adopt an insight, but the words stay his — "every word is my craftsmanship."
- The landing page carries the only AI-written words associated with the book (the
  disclosure paragraph itself), by his explicit design.
