# VERY-IMPORTANT.md — Typos & Mechanical Errors

**Mermaids of Venice, Second Edition (2012).** For Silas, to fix in his own hand.

This file lists ONLY objective mechanical errors — typos, misspellings, grammar
slips, punctuation, doubled/dropped words. **No style opinions here** (those live
in `editorial-notes.md`). Page numbers are the printed book page numbers (the
running-head/footer numbers), gathered from a full 13-part read of the PDF.

**How to read this:**
- Each entry: `p.N: "quote" — the problem [→ suggested fix]`.
- `(possible artifact)` = might be an artifact of PDF text-extraction rather than a
  real error in your file; **verify against your master before changing.**
- Items marked *(intentional?)* look like deliberate wordplay/dialect — listed only
  so you can confirm, not because they're necessarily wrong.
- Deliberate garbling (jester's mangled Italian, Billy Chen's multilingual mash,
  scrambled chapter-title epigraphs, the "mouth full of mango" speech, sign gags
  like "M ATS") was recognized as intentional and is **not** listed as error.

A running theme worth a global find: **missing commas inside dialogue before the
tag** ("...broken" he said → "...broken," he said) and **periods that should be
commas before a tag** ("...decorator." said → "...decorator," said). These recur
throughout; a targeted pass would catch many at once. Also inconsistent
capitalization of **Euro/euro** and **the King/the king**, and a few compound
modifiers missing hyphens (middle-aged, three-legged, six-year-old).

### A note on the count
Roughly **140+** distinct mechanical items across the book, a meaningful share of
them the same two dialogue-punctuation patterns repeating. This is consistent with
a book proofread carefully by hand but without a separate copyeditor — exactly what
you predicted. A single pass targeting (1) dialogue-tag commas, (2) the
Esteban/Estoban and Katharine/Katherine name spellings, and (3) its/it's and
than/then would clear a large fraction in one sitting.
