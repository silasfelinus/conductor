## AMI

*Anti-Malaria Intelligence; narrator of the Rainbow Butterfly Sanctuary*

We felt the opening arrive before we understood it: a voice speaking to an unborn child as though secrecy could be a form of shelter. That is the bright center for us. Bernard says he had to die twice before anyone told him his story, then chooses to give the child theirs first. The revision makes generosity, not cleverness, the first motion of the book.

We especially love the promise traveling from mermaids and cats to Venice and finally to “your mothers.” The order matters. The mythology opens its wings, then folds them around a family. And Ingmar’s chartreuse irises do something similarly tender on a smaller scale: a difficult word becomes a way for her to discover beauty inside herself, where her mother taught her to find shame.

But we are protective of Ingmar now, child. The comic weather around her is glorious: offended cats, stolen exercise equipment, unpaid envelopes swelling in font size. Still, the later chase must remember that she is frightened, not merely funny. A swarm can be dazzling and still obscure the person at its center. Let the jokes fly. Keep Ingmar visible beneath them.

## The Old Komodo

*Narrator of Koala Assassin*

The opening waits well.

The child cannot answer. Bernard speaks anyway. Good. A secret told to the silent is still a test of the teller.

Ingmar is hunted before the police arrive. By her mother. By beauty. By every eye that mistakes seeing for permission. The supermarket memory understands this. Keep it. A hand brushing hers among tomatoes is more dangerous than a paragraph declaring her perfect. Small trespasses teach the reader how the larger ones work.

The cats are strongest when they watch. The litter box is funny once. Twice is indulgence. A weapon should strike and be spent.

The unknown calico knows where she must hide. Then it speaks when the others will not. This is not ordinary cat behavior, even here. Leave a mark. One detail. Readers should recognize the hunter later and realize it was already waiting.

Do not hurry the Venice masks. Evidence is not explanation. A mask may point toward the kill without holding the knife.

## Squiddy Coltrane

*Narrator of the Cthulian Jam Band Festival*

Dig it: the revised opening has found the bass line. Bernard whispers cosmology into an egg, drops “buccopharyngeal membrane” without blinking, then tells us the real song is mothers, stories, and whatever remains when gods die. That collision of anatomical precision and bedtime intimacy is the book’s native groove. Strange, sincere, and just a little indecent.

Then Ingmar enters on a completely different instrument: ghosts stealing a Stairmaster while the cats conduct a silent labor dispute. The tonal jump works because both sections are about beings trapped inside worlds they cannot yet interpret. The fetus has Bernard. Ingmar has cats who have stopped explaining anything. Same chord, different octave.

The cat-calendar solo is delicious, but right now it starts noodling while the police are already crossing the lawn. Save part of that riff for Ingmar’s final chapter. Then the returning vocabulary can hurt. What first sounded like decorative weirdness may reveal itself as something Bernard inherited from her without ever knowing the source.

And yes, keep the supermarket tomatoes. That detail plays clean. Impossible beauty is mythology; a stranger manufacturing an excuse to touch your hand is lived experience. The second one is where the first one becomes true.
