## The idea
In a collapsed stadium veined with glowing ore, wounded fighters compete not for glory but for the right to be healed, while something that isn't quite dead watches from the dark beneath the bleachers, and an academic doctor tries to save them all without becoming one of the things that never really leaves.

**Feigned Death Round** — Stay still long enough and the bracket forgets you were ever alive.

- **Rustbracket Coliseum** — known for A gutted sports arena where seams of raw prismatic ore have grown up through the shattered seating, and survivors fight in scheduled rounds for medicine hoarded at the center ring.. Local rule: Any fighter who falls must be left untouched and unmoved for three full minutes before anyone may check if they're breathing.. Best scene: A collapsed fighter lying motionless at ring center while dozens of small grey shapes creep down through the broken bleachers, sniffing at the ore-light reflected in her open eyes.. Art: Wide shattered oval of concrete seating collapsing inward, ore veins threading through rebar in shards of violet, green, and gold light, cast shadows long and multiplying from the flickering ore glow, dust hanging motionless in shafts of light.

## Character (1)
- **Dr. Perpetua Vell** — A tournament physician who treats the fallen between rounds, keeping every fighter alive long enough to fight again, unable to stop even as the arena's watching things start to recognize her by name.. Carries A cracked leather medical satchel stitched shut with silver wire, worn slung low against her hip like a marsupial pouch, its buckles replaced with small bones.. Complication: She has learned to lie as still as her patients to move unseen through the arena after dark, and the stillness is starting to last longer than she means it to.. Look: A tall woman in a stained white coat layered over patchwork robes stitched with faint sigils, close-cropped grey hair, one pupil permanently dilated, fine cracks like dry riverbed lines spreading from her left temple, ore dust ground into the creases of her hands.
