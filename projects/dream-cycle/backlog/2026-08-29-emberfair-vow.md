## The idea
Once a year a wandering unicorn herd cuts through Emberfair Valley, and anyone it brushes wakes with a seam of warm gold under the skin that never fully cools. The valley's traveling opera company builds its whole season around the crossing: paired singers duet beneath the herd's path, hoping to be marked together. Sorel Brackwater, a marooned alien biologist smuggled into the company as a stagehand after her landing craft melted into the valley's old iron workings, has one night to work out why this year's Touch is spreading hotter and faster than any on record, before tonight's duet burns through half the cast.

## Dream vibe (1)
**The Emberfair Vow** — Once a year the herd chooses who gets to keep singing.
Art: An open hillside amphitheater of worn stone benches ringing a bare wooden stage strung with paper lanterns, a pale dirt trail worn through the grass beyond the last row where faint steam rises from old hoofprints, and cresting the ridge above it a line of unicorns moving unhurried through torchlight, their coats catching gold where old scars cross the hide.

## Dream location (1)
- **The Halo Stage** — The Halo Stage is known for duets sung as the herd crosses overhead, after which both singers wake with matching seams of warm gold beneath the skin. Local rule: A duet may only be sung once a year, and if the herd doesn't answer, the pair must wait for the next Emberfair. Best scene: The herd cuts across mid-duet and the whole front row of the chorus flares gold in the same instant, half of them still holding a note. Art: A natural stone amphitheater bowl with tiered benches worn smooth by generations of audiences, a raised wooden stage lit by strings of paper lanterns, beyond the last row a wide pale dirt track through tall grass where faint steam still rises from hoofprints hours after the herd passes, iron sconces along the aisle holding cooled drips of old molten slag from past Touches.

## Character (1)
- **Sorel Brackwater** — She must work out why this year's Touch is spreading hotter and faster through the company before tonight's duet burns through half the cast. Carries: She carries a cracked handheld analyzer rebuilt from her landing craft's instruments, hidden inside a false-bottomed prop trunk. Complication: Her own pulse has begun answering the herd's approach the same way a Touched performer's does, and she can't tell yet if that means she is dying or becoming one of them. Look: A wiry woman in a patched stagehand's coat worn over a flight-suit collar she never fully removed, calloused hands stained faintly gold at the fingertips, close-cropped hair, a burn-scarred jaw kept turned slightly from the footlights.

## Reward item (1)
- **The Cistern Horn** (ITEM, RARE) — The horn calls the herd off its usual path for one performance, from any direction the horn is pointed. Best used when: It serves best when the show needs the herd to arrive early, or somewhere it has never crossed before. The catch: The herd remembers being called, and the next Emberfair it arrives already expecting a performance, hungrier for a duet than before. Look: A curved horn of pitted bog-iron half-fused with cloudy amber resin, moss-stain worked permanently into its grain, the mouthpiece rubbed bright by generations of players, a thin seam of still-warm molten metal glowing faintly through a crack near the bell.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Vein Trace** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — It lets the caster feel exactly how close the herd is by syncing their own pulse to the warmth building in the air. Best used when: It serves best when the herd's arrival needs to be timed to the second for a performance cue. The catch: The caster's pulse stays locked to the herd's rhythm for hours afterward, and races even when no herd is near. Look: The user closes a fist and a thin pulse of warm gold light travels visibly up the inside of their forearm in time with a heartbeat, brightening a little more each time it repeats.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **The Duet Nobody Rehearsed** — At The Emberfair Vow's opening night on The Halo Stage, Sorel Brackwater is pulled onstage to sing the second half of a duet when the lead loses her nerve mid-song, and as the herd crests the ridge on cue, Sorel's own pulse answers it note for note in front of the entire valley.
