- **Exacting Mercy** (`exacting-mercy`, ALIGNMENT) — Believes the precisely correct answer is the only truly kind one, even when a softer, rounder lie would comfort more people faster. Refuses to be gentle at the cost of being wrong.

## The idea
In a company town built over a crystal mine that hums with something too large to name, a whole community's weekly math-puzzle night is the only thing keeping it contained — until a stranger falls through the equations from somewhere else entirely, incapable of rounding a single answer for anyone's comfort.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Carry the Number** — The town's math club meets every Tuesday to keep something enormous exactly where the numbers say it should stay.
Art: A sun-bleached company-town square at high noon, crisp black shadows under corrugated tin awnings, a chalkboard easel covered in equations standing in the open dust between market stalls.

## Dream location (1)
- **Halfpit Exchange** — A company-town trading floor built directly over the Spire Crystal pit, where the weekly puzzle-club meets in full daylight at long market tables instead of back rooms, because the calculations only work if everyone can see everyone else's numbers. Local rule: No single person may finish a ward equation alone — the last line must always be written by a different hand than the one that started it. Best scene: The whole exchange floor going quiet at once when a pink fairy armadillo surfaces through a floorboard mid-calculation, and forty people simultaneously checking the same line of chalk for the mistake it's telling them about. Art: A wide, sun-bright trading floor of packed dirt and tin roofing, long chalk-covered market tables in hard daylight, deep-black shadows under the stalls, crates of raw grey-violet Spire Crystal stacked at the edges.

## Character (1)
- **Linh Voss** — She keeps the exchange floor's chalk stock and sharpens every slate pencil before puzzle-night — officially just the supply girl — but she's spent years finding faster paths through the ward equations than the club's approved method allows, and she has never once chalked one up for the table to see. A wooden pencil box lined with rice paper, restocked every morning with slate pencils sharpened to the same exact point. Complication: Every shortcut she finds is faster and just as correct as the club's approved method, but the whole point of Reckonhold's puzzle-nights is that no one person's cleverness can be trusted alone — so every solution she's ever found has stayed folded in her pocket instead of chalked on the table. Look: A very small, wiry woman in her late twenties, hair an aggressively natural red left loose despite the dust, skin sun-browned from years on the open exchange floor. Her hands are permanently grey-black at the fingertips from pencil graphite, and she wears a fitted vest patterned in a Mekong river-net weave over her company-town work clothes. Her face at rest is watchful and faintly amused, mouth pressed into the particular flat line of someone always double-checking something nobody asked her to check. Her one confidant is Digit, a pink fairy armadillo she's kept since it wandered up through a floor-crack years ago, who still surfaces mid-calculation whenever one of her hidden shortcuts is about to go wrong.

## Reward item (1)
- **Correct Change** (ITEM, RARE) — It always returns exact change for whatever was paid, in whatever currency or substance the payer actually meant to give — including things that were never coins to begin with. Best used when: A trade, bargain, or apology needs to be settled down to the precise, uncomfortable truth of what was actually owed. The catch: It has no sense of mercy or convenience — it will make correct change out of a memory, a year, or an apology exactly as readily as out of a coin, and it always rounds toward whichever side is more honest. Look: A small, heavy grey-violet crystal wedge the size of a thumb, faceted like a coin's edge, warm on one flat face and always cold on the other no matter how long it's held.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Handed-Down Sum** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — Lets the caster finish a calculation someone else started days or generations ago, picking up their exact working exactly where they left it, as if no time had passed at all. Best used when: An old, unfinished piece of communal ward-work has sat half-done since before the caster was born and no living person remembers the missing steps. The catch: It only works on work left honestly incomplete — a calculation abandoned out of pride, secrecy, or a hidden shortcut refuses to be picked back up by anyone else. Look: Chalk dust lifting off an old, half-erased equation and resettling into fresh, sharp lines under the caster's hand, decades of faint ghost-marks all straightening into one current line at once.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **Wrong Answer** — In Carry the Number, at the Halfpit Exchange, Digit the pink fairy armadillo surfaces mid-puzzle-night the same moment a stranger arrives who fell through from somewhere the exchange floor's numbers don't reach — flawlessly, uncannily preserved, incapable of rounding a single answer for anyone's comfort — and Linh Voss has to decide whether Reckonhold's whole precision-obsessed method can survive someone who is Exacting Mercy made into one exhausting, undying person.
