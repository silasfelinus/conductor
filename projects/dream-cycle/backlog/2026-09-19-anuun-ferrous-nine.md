## New Facets (invented for this dream)
- **Tuning Warden** (`tuning-warden`, OCCUPATION) — Someone whose job is to keep an entire collective instrument, mechanism, or community in tune by adjusting everyone else, never themselves. The whole stays balanced only for as long as the warden's own pitch never moves.
- **Duet-Bound** (`duet-bound`, SPECIES) — A species born as two separate juveniles who must physically fuse into one shared adult body to survive their own metamorphosis. Two minds remain inside afterward, but only one body ever answers to being addressed.

## The idea
A tuning warden who has spent a lifetime adjusting everyone else's pitch is finally offered the one thing wardens never get: a turn to be adjusted themselves.

## Dream vibe (1)
**The Choir Only Grows** — At the Ninth Crescent Opera House, joining the choir costs you your solo voice forever.
Art: A vast sunlit opera rotunda strung with brass gears and thousands of hollow resonance-pipes, a crowd in bright banners and confetti gathered under open skylights, motion and colour everywhere.

## Dream location (1)
- **Ninth Crescent Opera House** — The Ninth Crescent Opera House trains tuning wardens to keep its thousand hollow choristers singing in one unbroken chord. Local rule: No warden may retune their own section once the noon bell has already rung. Best scene: A thousand banner-draped choristers flood the rotunda floor at noon, and every gear in the ceiling turns to face them at once. Art: Towering brass rotunda walls lined with thousands of hollow resonance pipes catching noon sun, confetti and banner-cloth drifting across a crowded gear-strewn floor, everything gleaming and in motion.

## Character (1)
- **Anuun Ferrous-Nine** — They keep the whole choir in tune by adjusting every other voice except their own, year after year. A hand-cranked tuning fork worn smooth at the grip rides slung across their back on a frayed strap, its dozen resonance rods each humming a faint different note. Complication: Today the noon bell will ask them to fuse their own pitch into the choir at last, and no one has ever taught a warden how to retune themself. Look: Short and dense through the shoulders, solidly middle-aged with deep laugh-creases worn permanently into weathered brown skin. Scalp shaved smooth and oiled to a soft shine, a scatter of small ritual scars along one temple. Sealskin-trimmed cuffs and a beaded harness-vest, stitched in an Arctic coastal pattern, hang oddly over an opera warden's brass-buttoned formal coat -- two wardrobes worn at once, neither fully surrendered. Eyes narrowed in permanent, good-humoured concentration, head already tilted to catch a wrong note before it lands.

## Reward item (1)
- **Split Fermata Bell** (ITEM, RARE) — It rings twice from one strike, a half-beat apart, as if two bells were sharing the same casing. Best used when: Two duet-bound performers have to hit the exact same note at the exact same instant, or the fusion fails. The catch: Once the bell has rung for a pair, it will never again ring true for either of them alone. Look: A small brass bell split cleanly down the middle by an old, fused seam, the two halves swinging a fraction out of alignment with each other, both edges polished bright from handling.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Calling the Full Choir** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — It lets a single voice pull every hollow chorister within earshot into one enormous, synchronized chord. Best used when: A ceremony needs more voices than any one performer could ever gather or rehearse in time. The catch: Every chorister who answers the call keeps a sliver of your own voice afterward and never fully gives it back. Look: A single held note visibly ripples outward across a crowded hall, hundreds of hollow choristers turning toward the sound in the same instant, banners overhead trembling from the collective volume.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **One Voice Finally Joins the Chord** — In The Choir Only Grows at the Ninth Crescent Opera House, Anuun Ferrous-Nine steps into the noon crowd and finally lets their own pitch fuse into the thousand-voice chord they have spent a lifetime tuning for everyone else.
