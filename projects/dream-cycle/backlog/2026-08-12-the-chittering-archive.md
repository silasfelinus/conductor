## The idea
When the storm season floods a mythpunk megacity, its rooftop crews keep the flood barriers powered by performing — verses shouted into hand-woven silk cable become current, and the block that goes quiet is the block the water takes.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Monsoon Static** — The storm only spares the block that out-sings it.
Art: Rain-lashed corrugated rooftops strung with hand-woven radiant-silk power line glowing amber, a jury-rigged broadcast mast wrapped in patterned indigo cloth, black floodwater below throwing back sodium orange light and drifting foam.

## Dream location (1)
- **The Roofline Circuit** — Rooftop stages here are wired straight into the flood barriers, turning every performance into the current that keeps the city's lamps burning. Local rule: The loudest verse gets the power, and silence anywhere on the line invites the flood. Best scene: The surge climbs the stairwell while the mast crew hauls glowing silk line back up hand over hand, racing the rising black water to keep the barrier lamps alive. Art: A chain of tin and concrete rooftops joined by sagging luminous silk cable, sandbagged stage platforms lashed with rope, small chinchilla-borne relay drones perched along the wires, green-black stormlight and horizontal rain.

## Character (1)
- **Adaeze Bankole** — She must hold the roofline stage all night so the barrier lamps never drop. A battered throat-mic rig spliced into a chinchilla-carried relay pack hangs from her collar, wired straight into the line. Complication: Her voice is failing, and every verse she spends costs her the next one. Look: A warm, broad-shouldered performer in a soaked indigo wax-print jacket, a copper throat-mic collar biting into her neck, wet braids taped flat, one forearm sleeved in cracked cyber-plating that glows faintly at the seams.

## Reward item (1)
- **Stormsong Reed** (ITEM, RARE) — It gives a spent voice one more full verse. Best used when: A spent voice reaches for the reed when the crowd has gone quiet and the barrier lamps are dimming. The catch: The borrowed verse is the last thing its previous owner ever said. Look: A hand-length reed of woven radiant silk stiffened with amber resin, its mouthpiece worn glassy, a single gold thread pulsing along its length.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Call-and-Return** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — It turns a crowd's answering shout back into usable current. Best used when: The cable has gone dark between verses, and the lamps have not yet failed. The catch: The crowd feels every jolt its user draws through them. Look: Amber sound-rings blooming outward from an open mouth and snapping back as bright filaments that crawl along the wet silk cable overhead.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **The Surge Set** — In Monsoon Static, up on The Roofline Circuit, Adaeze Bankole trades verses with a rival crew across the flooded bog district through the night's highest surge while chinchilla relay drones drag the silk line back onto the mast — because the moment the roofline goes quiet, the barrier lamps die and the water takes the block.
