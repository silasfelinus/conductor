## The idea
Once every ninety nights a sleeping glacier-whale surfaces beneath a comet, and a market unfolds across its barnacled back — sellers trading bottled starlight, secrets, and small debts instead of coin. People come to buy something they can't get anywhere else, and to find out exactly what they owe the world in return. Cozy, salt-cold, and a little bittersweet.

## Vibe / genre dream
**Borrowed-Light Bittersweet** — GENRE. A trade economy built on debts instead of money; warm lantern-crowds over cold dark water, everyone a little in arrears to someone.

## Locations (2)
- **The Comet Market** — known for ninety stalls lashed across a whale's broad back, lit by jars of bottled comet-light instead of lamps. Local rule: Nothing is bought here — everything is owed, and the ledger remembers. Best scene: the first hour after surfacing, when the stalls are still unfolding and the whale's breathing rocks every table. Art: night market of patched tents and rope-lashed stalls across a vast barnacled whale-back, jars of cold blue-white comet-light strung overhead, a bright comet low on the horizon, dark glassy sea, cozy strange scale.
- **The Static Lighthouse** — known for a beached, decommissioned lighthouse rewired into a one-man pirate radio station that broadcasts the whale's next surfacing. Local rule: The signal only tells the truth at midnight — every other hour, it's allowed to lie a little. Best scene: a stormy broadcast night, static crackling between real coordinates and comforting fictions. Art: weathered lighthouse interior strung with salvaged radio parts and cable, warm tube-amp glow against cold blue window-light, rain streaking the glass, charts pinned everywhere, lonely and warm at once.

## Characters (3)
- **Whisk Tallow** — the market's crier and ledger-keeper; wants to find the one buyer who can finally pay off the whale's debt so it can stop surfacing and rest. Carries a lantern of bottled comet-light and a ledger with more crossed-out names than clean ones. Complication: doesn't yet know her late mentor invented the whale's debt from nothing, just to keep the market — and her — from folding. Look: patched oilskin coat sewn with barnacle buttons, tally-marks tattooed down one forearm, a crier's brass hailer slung on her hip.
- **Corvid Ames** — the lighthouse's broadcaster; relays the whale's coordinates to keep the market's sellers fed, but quietly delays the true signal to keep a smaller, loyal crowd coming back to him first. Carries a hand-cranked radio welded together from three different shipwrecks. Complication: is fairly sure that if the whale ever found permanent shore, nobody would need his voice again. Look: sun-bleached radioman's coat, headphones fused into an old diving helmet, fingers permanently ink-stained from charts.
- **Nix Farrow** — a child stowaway collecting one secret from every stall, convinced a completed 'book of debts' will free the whale for real. Carries a notebook bound in fish-leather, half full of secrets that shouldn't be able to come true — but are starting to. Complication: the entries are changing the world to match them, and Nix has no idea it's happening. Look: patchwork poncho stitched from old stall banners, ink-stained fingers, a borrowed jar of comet-light kept dim on purpose.

## Rewards (2 — one skill, one item)
- **Debt-Reading** (SKILL, RARE) — the ability to see exactly what someone — or something — owes the world, and to whom. Best used when bartering at the Comet Market or untangling an old grudge that won't die quietly. The catch: once you can read debts, you can't stop seeing your own.
- **Bottled Comet-Light** (ITEM, UNCOMMON) — a jar of true starlight that lights any dark room for one full night and reveals ink hidden in shadow. Best used when exploring the lighthouse's locked archive or reading a page of Nix's notebook nobody's supposed to see. The catch: it dims a little more each time it's used, and no one left alive knows how to refill one.

## Scenarios (1-2)
- **The Ninety-First Night** — The whale surfaces a full night early, throwing off Corvid's carefully-delayed broadcast and stranding half the market's regular sellers who won't arrive in time — and Whisk has to decide who gets fed on short notice.
- **Reading the Debt Book** — Nix asks the player to help verify one entry in the fish-leather notebook — a debt owed not by any person, but by the market itself, straight from Whisk's ledger.

## Narrator
**Bosun Pell** as narrator bot: warm, gravelly, a little wistful — an old radio host who's watched ninety markets come and go. Personality: nostalgic, fiercely protective of the whale, quick with a trade-joke to defuse a hard moment. Appears as: a barnacle-crusted ship's figurehead brought to life, one eye replaced by a steady lantern. Best for: welcoming newcomers to the market's odd economy, explaining the whale's history, gently steering conversations away from questions that would break someone's heart. Expressions: NEUTRAL plus WARM, WISTFUL, ALARMED (an early surfacing), and DELIGHTED (a good trade). Topics/threads: how the debt economy actually works; the whale's long history; what happens if the debt is ever really paid off.
