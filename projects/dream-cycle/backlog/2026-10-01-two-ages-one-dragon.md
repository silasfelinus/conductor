## The idea
A dragon-bodied auditor whose growth got split between two migrations must choose: finish becoming herself the slow way, or accept the same force-grown shortcut the fraud she is investigating already used.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Tideglass Season** — Every equinox the solar canals fill with migrating sea dragons, and the office permitting their passage would rather nobody notice how many come back changed.
Art: A wide solar-glass canal at dawn, tiered green rooftops reflected in rippling water, a sea dragon's spine breaking the surface mid-migration, hard clean daylight, no soft glow.

## Dream location (1)
- **Driftpile House** — Standing on hand-driven Shadow Oak pilings, grown into the canal bed rather than poured or bolted; the whole frame darkens and swells before a storm and dries back to itself within a day, its color changing with its temper. Local rule: No permit may be stamped while the pilings are creaking, because the creak means a dragon is passing directly beneath the floor, and a permit closed out mid-creak is void by a custom nobody has ever bothered to write down. Best scene: A clerk freezes mid-stamp as the whole timber frame groans and tilts underfoot, brass desk-lamps swinging, while a sea dragon's shadow slides beneath the glass floor panels like weather passing through the building. Art: A timber office interior grown around living Shadow Oak pilings, dark wood swollen and glossy near the waterline, glass floor panels showing green canal water below, hard midday light, no soft glow.

## Character (1)
- **Adia Ngoro** — She investigates falsified growth permits the way she settles every dispute at Driftpile House: by pulling both sides to the same fire pit and refusing to let anyone leave until the argument is cooked all the way through, not merely charred on the outside. A dented iron cookfire grate, pulled from a burned pirate galley she once crewed before the migration spat her out at Driftpile House, slung across one shoulder on a chain worn bright with handling. Complication: Her temper runs on the barometer, not on provocation: calm and almost gentle in high pressure, sharp enough to scorch nearby brass fittings as a storm builds, with no memory afterward of what she said or did, so the clerks have learned to check the glass before bringing her a dispute. Underneath that, half her body is still finishing a growth the other half completed years ago, and the same people forging accelerated permits have just offered to finish her the fast way too. Look: A sea dragon thick through the middle, barrel-bodied where most of her kind run lean. Her crest spines, normally reef-red down only one side for males and pale down the other for females among her kind, run aggressively red on both sides, a marking her cohort still struggles to read at a glance. One flank is ancient-dark and gouged with overlapping old scarring; the other still shows the smooth pale-green sheen of a creature half her apparent age, the two halves meeting in a visible seam down her spine. Cattle-bell charms, brass and herd-worn smooth, are threaded on a braided leather cord looped twice around one foreleg, alongside a strip of indigo caravan-cloth knotted at a wrist-joint, both mended more than replaced. At rest her jaw sits open just enough to show the first knuckle of teeth, an easy, ready-to-argue look that reads as a grin right up until it doesn't.

Best used when: A shipment, a permit, or a body arrives suspiciously perfect and nobody can explain how it got that way so fast. The catch: It cannot tell you what a thing grew from, only that it grew, so a beautifully cultivated poison passes as easily as a vegetable.
