## The idea
At Sable & Bone, the last course of every seven-course tasting menu is a living ingredient that must be persuaded, not simply cooked. Wendell Hobb, a bearfolk dishwasher accidentally promoted to Court Taster-Diplomat after the real diplomat quit mid-shift, now spends every dinner service coaxing things that arrived from outside the world into agreeing to become dessert.

**The Tasting Menu Eats Back** — Seven courses in, the food starts talking back.

## Character (1)
- **Wendell Hobb** — get through one full dinner service without losing an argument, so nobody notices the real Court Taster-Diplomat isn't coming back. Carries a dishwater-stained tasting spoon and a dog-eared list of house promises he half-understands. Complication: he must honor every ingredient's spoken wish out loud and to the letter, or the whole kitchen's dishes turn cold and sullen. Look: A heavyset bearfolk man in a stained white apron over a borrowed diplomat's sash, thick clawed hands cradling a spoon too small for them, small round glasses pushed low on a broad muzzle, tired eyes and an anxious, accommodating smile.

## Reward item (1)
- **The Pulse-Metal Ladle** (ITEM, RARE) — settles any ingredient's objection on the spot, holding it in agreeable stillness for exactly one serving. Best used when an ingredient balks at being eaten and starts pleading its case mid-course. The catch: the ladle keeps a perfect memory of the last argument and repeats it back, out loud, at the worst possible moment. Look: A soup ladle forged from dull silver-blue pulse metal, its bowl faintly pulsing with a slow heartbeat-like glow, the handle warm to the touch and etched with fine concentric rings that brighten with each pulse.

## Reward skill (1)

Best used when facing a totally unknown ingredient with no precedent in the house promises. The catch: only works while the diplomat is being completely honest; one polite lie and the ingredient takes back what it offered.
