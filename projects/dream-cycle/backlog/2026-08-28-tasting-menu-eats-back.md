## The idea
At Sable & Bone, the last course of every seven-course tasting menu is a living ingredient that must be persuaded, not simply cooked. Wendell Hobb, a bearfolk dishwasher accidentally promoted to Court Taster-Diplomat after the real diplomat quit mid-shift, now spends every dinner service coaxing things that arrived from outside the world into agreeing to become dessert.

## Dream vibe (1)
**The Tasting Menu Eats Back** — Seven courses in, the food starts talking back.
Art: A candlelit fine-dining hall mid-service, white tablecloths and gleaming pulse-metal cutlery, while the seventh plate at the head table holds something faintly luminous and many-eyed that is still politely arranging itself into a presentable shape; a broad bearfolk figure in a sommelier's apron leans down to listen to it.

## Dream location (1)
- **The Petitioner's Larder** — Every ingredient stored here must be persuaded to give itself up, since none of them will simply hold still for the knife. Local rule: No one may taste anything before it has finished speaking its mind. Best scene: The larder door swings open onto shelving that keeps receding past where the room's walls should be, revealing a line of small glowing things waiting nose to tail down a hallway of stars. Art: A restaurant pantry whose back wall opens onto a starfield instead of brick, wooden shelves lined with jars and crates that visibly breathe or shift, a queue of small tentacled, softly glowing ingredients waiting one behind another on the floor, warm kitchen light spilling into the dark beyond the doorway.

## Character (1)
- **Wendell Hobb** — He must get through one full dinner service without losing an argument, so nobody notices the real Court Taster-Diplomat isn't coming back. He carries a dishwater-stained tasting spoon and a dog-eared list of house promises he half-understands. Complication: He must honor every ingredient's spoken wish out loud and to the letter, or the whole kitchen's dishes turn cold and sullen. Look: A heavyset bearfolk man in a stained white apron over a borrowed diplomat's sash, thick clawed hands cradling a spoon too small for them, small round glasses pushed low on a broad muzzle, tired eyes and an anxious, accommodating smile.

## Reward item (1)
- **The Pulse-Metal Ladle** (ITEM, RARE) — It settles any ingredient's objection on the spot, holding it in agreeable stillness for exactly one serving. Best used when: An ingredient is balking at being eaten and pleading its case mid-course. The catch: The ladle keeps a perfect memory of the last argument and repeats it back, out loud, at the worst possible moment. Look: A soup ladle forged from dull silver-blue pulse metal, its bowl faintly pulsing with a slow heartbeat-like glow, the handle warm to the touch and etched with fine concentric rings that brighten with each pulse.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Xenoculinary Treaty** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — It reads any unfamiliar ingredient's wishes at a glance, translating what it wants into plain kitchen language. Best used when: A totally unknown ingredient arrives, and the house has no precedent on record for what it might want. The catch: It only works while the diplomat is being completely honest, and one polite lie makes the ingredient take back what it offered. Look: A faint lattice of pale light briefly traces itself across the surface of an ingredient being read, like handwriting appearing on fogged glass, before fading once its wishes are understood.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **The Course That Wants to Stay** — At The Petitioner's Larder behind Sable & Bone, Wendell Hobb is halfway through The Tasting Menu Eats Back's seventh course when the evening's ingredient — a small drifting knot of starlight meant to become dessert — announces it has decided not to leave the mortal world once it's plated, and the whole dining room is waiting on him to talk it back into what it already promised.
