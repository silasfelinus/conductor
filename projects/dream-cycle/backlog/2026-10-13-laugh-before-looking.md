## New Facets (invented for this dream)
- **Claqueur** (`claqueur`, OCCUPATION) — Someone paid to begin the applause, weeping, or dancing that a crowd then believes is its own. The work moves whole rooms; the worker rarely gets to find out what they actually feel.
- **Borrowed Conscience** (`borrowed-conscience`, ALIGNMENT) — A moral compass that takes its bearing from whoever stands nearest. It makes for easy company and allies who cannot be counted on once the room changes.

## The idea
At a high-fantasy spring feast on a whisperwood dam, kingfishers dive into a bottomless reservoir and every strange thing they bring up is greeted with ritual laughter on cue, so a young diver starts wondering who the valley is laughing for.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Laughing the Catch Home** — The whole valley laughs at what rises from the deep, and nobody remembers why it must.
Art: Faux-fresco feast panorama in bold noon daylight: lime plaster brushed in ochre, lapis and vermilion with deliberately faked age cracks and flaking gilt, a vast laughing crowd on a pale wooden dam crest, striped awnings, long banners snapping in the wind, trestle tables heaped with bread, fruit and oddly shaped fish, swarms of blue-and-orange kingfishers diving in formation, crisp hard shadows.

## Dream location (1)
- **Kingsdrop Dam Feast** — Its whisperwood dam holds back a reservoir so deep that the fish brought up at the spring feast are kinds nobody downstream has ever named. Local rule: Every catch set down on a feast table must be greeted with laughter, and paid claqueurs in the frescoed galleries start it before anyone has had a good look. Best scene: A diver surfaces holding something that is plainly not a fish, and the entire dam-crest crowd bursts out laughing because the galleries already have. Art: A colossal curved dam of silver-grey whisperwood planks under hard noon sun, its crest packed with feast trestles, striped awnings, vermilion pennants and a dense laughing crowd, galleries along the dam face done in flaking faux-fresco ochre and lapis with rows of grinning figures, swarms of kingfishers plunging into a reservoir that turns from turquoise to ink-black a few metres down, wet planks dark where the spray hits, crisp shadows.

## Character (1)
- **Wayra Kel-Adar** — They are the last kingfisher who still dives the reservoir and comes back up, and they want to know what the deep is getting in exchange for its fish. A brass feast rattle, riveted like a small engine valve, hangs from an indigo leather cord beside a striped woven pouch of dried river salt. Complication: As the feast's hired claqueur, they are paid to start the laughter the moment they land, so every catch is laughed at before even they have looked at it. Look: A barely grown kingfisher, long and angular, all knife-straight beak and thin stilt legs, plumage a hard river-blue except for a crest of rust-red feathers that sticks up in every direction and will not lie flat, the bill banded black and sunset-orange in rings that match neither the drake nor the hen pattern. An Andean striped woven pouch and an indigo-dyed Sahelian leather cord cross the narrow chest, a riveted brass rattle at the breastbone, oil-stained brass dive goggles pushed up into the crest. Perched very still on a sunlit whisperwood rail above a laughing crowd, eyes level and unblinking, the wary, steady face of the only one who still comes back.

## Reward item (1)
- **Nodding Floodpole** (ITEM, RARE) — It murmurs, when dipped into any river, how high that water will stand at next spring's melt. Best used when: A village is arguing over whether to raise its walls or move its fields uphill. The catch: It speaks in the voice of whoever stands nearest, and it has never once disagreed with them. Look: A man-height pole of pale silver-grey whisperwood, finely ringed grain, notched every handspan with brushed water-marks in ochre and lapis, a brass ferrule at the foot gone green, the lower third dark and swollen from years of soaking, a frayed red feast ribbon tied below the top.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Overplastering** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — It paints a scene onto fresh plaster that everyone who sees it remembers as centuries old. Best used when: A ruined town needs to agree on what its old square looked like before the fire. The catch: Whoever paints it forgets what the wall showed before. Look: Wet lime plaster swept across a cracked, scorched wall in one broad arc, ochre, lapis and vermilion pigment blooming into a crowded harvest scene that ages as it dries, hairline cracks and flaking gilt racing across it, plaster dust hanging in hard noon light.
