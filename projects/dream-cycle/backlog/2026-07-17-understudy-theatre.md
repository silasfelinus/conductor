## The idea
An all-night theater where the props are alive and rehearsing to become the real thing — a wooden sword practicing to be steel, a paper moon auditioning for the sky. Visitors come to coach a nervous prop, sit as an honest witness at a final audition, or lend a made thing the one true detail it's missing. Velvet-and-dust cozy, with the ache of wanting to be more than what you were built for.

## Vibe / genre dream
**Velvet-Dust Ambition** — GENRE. Every made thing here wants to be real and rehearses nightly; warm stage-light hope against the hush of an empty house.

## Locations (2)
- **The Prop Loft** — known for floor-to-rafter shelves where retired props rehearse being real after midnight — wooden fruit sweating real juice, a tin crown practicing weight, a rope learning to be a snake and regretting it. Local rule: Never applaud a prop mid-rehearsal — applause fixes a thing exactly as it is, flaws and all. Best scene: two in the morning, when the skylight's moonbeam crosses the shelves and a hundred props freeze mid-practice as it passes over them. Art: cavernous backstage loft, towering wooden shelves crammed with theatrical props, dust motes in a single shaft of moonlight, warm work-lamp pools, half-real objects mid-transformation — a wooden apple blushing genuinely red — cozy clutter, deep velvet shadow.
- **The Ghostlight Stage** — known for the theater's main stage, kept lit all night by a single bare ghostlight, where props take their one final audition to become real. Local rule: One audition per prop, ever — and if it goes unwitnessed by at least one honest heart, it doesn't count and never will. Best scene: a final audition: the empty house, a single bulb, one prop giving everything while the dark rows of seats decide whether to believe. Art: grand empty theater seen from center stage looking outward, single bare ghostlight bulb on a stand, rows of dark velvet seats vanishing into black, gilded balconies catching faint gold, one small prop spotlit in the hush, romantic and slightly eerie.

## Characters (3)
- **Mercy Grand** — the prop master, a retired quick-change artist who coaches props toward their auditions and is determined every one of them gets a fair shot before the theater finally closes. Carries a chatelaine of keys, thimbles, and a tiny brass whistle that can call any prop by its true name. Complication: she was a prop herself once — a costume dummy who passed her audition sixty years ago — and doesn't know the ghostlight is dimming because it spent so much of itself making her. Look: silver hair pinned with hatpins from six decades of shows, patched velvet tailcoat over paint-stained overalls, a pincushion strapped to one wrist.
- **Bit** — a wooden stage sword that wants to become real steel — but only so it can finally be too heavy to swing carelessly, and retire over somebody's fireplace. Carries the nick from its one great performance, kept deliberately unsanded, like a medal. Complication: its audition falls on the same night the critic returns, and stage fright makes its grain go soft. Look: a prop shortsword with worn gilt paint, tape-wrapped grip darkened by a hundred hands, a faint moonlit sheen along the edge where steel is already starting to show through.
- **Maestro Willowby** — the last theater critic in the city, sneaking in nightly; wants to witness one true audition to prove to himself he can still believe in anything. Carries a fountain pen that only writes what he actually feels — which is why he stopped publishing. Complication: his witness could make any audition count, but a bad review from that pen unmakes a prop entirely, and he knows it. Look: moth-eaten opera scarf, spectacles fogged from the cold walk over, a program from a show that closed forty years ago folded in his breast pocket.

## Rewards (2 — one skill, one item)
- **Quick-Change** (SKILL, RARE) — swap one outfit, disguise, or small held object for another in the time it takes a curtain to fall. Best used when slipping past someone who just saw you, or handing a nervous prop the missing costume piece mid-audition without breaking the scene. The catch: whatever you change out of spends the rest of the scene backstage — you can't have it back until the moment has passed.
- **Pocket Ghostlight** (ITEM, EPIC) — a matchbox-sized brass lamp holding one steady coin of stage-light; any performance, confession, or promise spoken in its glow is truly witnessed — it counts, and can't later be denied. Best used when a prop's final audition has no other honest heart in the house, or someone is about to take back a truth by morning. The catch: it burns lower with every witnessing, and relighting it costs the story of your proudest moment — told honestly, out loud, to an empty room.

## Scenarios (1-2)
- **Opening Night for a Wooden Sword** — Bit's one and only audition has come. Mercy needs the player backstage on quick-change duty, Maestro Willowby is settling into row F with his terrible honest pen — and the ghostlight, unknown to everyone, is one witnessing away from going dark.
- **The Applause Thief** — Someone has been applauding props mid-rehearsal in the Loft, fixing them half-finished forever. Mercy asks the player to sit the midnight watch and learn whether it's sabotage — or a lonely, already-finished prop that just wants company frozen beside it.

## Narrator
**Prompter Ossian** as narrator bot: a hushed, confiding whisper from just below the stage lip, always ready with your next line before you know you've lost it. Personality: patient, encouraging, incurably sentimental about lost causes; keeps every secret except the ones that would actually help you. Appears as: a brass prompt-box grille with warm light glowing behind it and a pair of white gloves that gesture expressively over the lip of the box. Best for: coaching nervous first-timers, feeding forgotten lines and courage in equal measure, and narrating auditions like they matter — because here, they do. Expressions: NEUTRAL plus ENCOURAGING, HUSHED (mid-audition), CRESTFALLEN (a missed cue), and TRIUMPHANT (a prop made real). Topics/threads: how a prop earns its one audition; the etiquette of applause and why it's dangerous; what the ghostlight actually is and what it costs.
