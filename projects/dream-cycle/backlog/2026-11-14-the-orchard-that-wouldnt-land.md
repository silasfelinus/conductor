The original Dream's registered Facets remain creative constraints for this new, independent day; the supplemental game candidates use separate, registered Facets and do not inherit the Dream style.

## The idea
Each solstice, a rootless orchard drifts upside down over a tightly packed village. A relay of hollow-bodied athletes must fasten its wandering roots before its shadow turns houses into saplings; this year the final tether is attached to someone still alive.

## Dream vibe (1)
**The Orchard That Wouldn't Land** — A floating orchard changes course whenever a runner falls, and the village below has almost no roofs left to spare.

Art: Wide cinematic view of an enormous inverted quince orchard held in the sky by tangled vermilion root cables, rows of tilted clay rooftops far beneath, narrow rope racing lanes hung between heavy green fruit boughs, a chain of tiny athletes sprinting across sagging canopy bridges, bronze timing bells and brass flywheels, bright wind-struck sunrise and dramatic vertical scale, no writing.

## Dream location (1)
- **Rootfall Circuit** — An aerial relay course curves through the underside of an enormous floating orchard, where competitors must outrun the swing of the roots they are meant to secure. Local rule: Each runner may fasten exactly one dangling root, and a missed foothold sends the entire canopy drifting toward the nearest inhabited roof. Best scene: Two suspended raceways separate under a tumbling brass bell while a champion holds the loose ropes together with outstretched arms over a village far below.

Art: Vertiginous aerial athletics track made of braided rope and hanging living branches below a massive upside-down orchard, dark red roots whipping in the wind, gold quince fruit, ceramic rooftops dizzyingly far below, frayed rope bridges with one splitting at a bend, copper whistles, bright stormlit sky, wide dynamic composition without writing.

## Character (1)
- **Second Noon** — They enter the root-tether relay to prevent their childhood neighborhood from becoming another grove, even though they remember losing this exact race in a timeline nobody else recalls.

Carries: A compact harness holds carved wooden mooring pegs, a spool of copper wire and a battered blue-and-white ceramic race bell that only rings when someone loses their footing.

Complication: The wooden arm brace salvaged from a long-dead former champion predicts each leap perfectly, but keeps steering their hands toward the finish line where that champion died.

Art: Tall, heavy-set, androgynous Hollow Choir athlete with a deep hollow wooden torso and visible resonating throat chamber, asymmetrical salt-and-pepper moss braids, patched age-worn timber skin with fresh green joints, broad shoulders, vivid crimson running sash over patterned woven textile from a bustling Oaxacan market tradition, blue-and-white ceramic beadwork recalling a coastal Lisbon trading quarter, carved arm brace and copper belay harness, one foot flying from a hanging orchard branch while reaching for a red dangling root, spectacular sky perspective.

## Reward item (1)
- **Graftneedle** — A curved ironwood needle joins two broken living branches into a temporary bridge that remains sound for seven deep breaths.

Best used when: A crossing breaks in midair and the next safe foothold is beyond anyone's ordinary jump.

Catch: Each emergency graft takes a little flexibility from the arm holding the needle, leaving the user rigid until the next dawn.

Art: Large curved polished ironwood grafting needle with bronze threading eye, a taut green tendril passing through it, fresh plant sap beading along dark grain, suspended between two torn branches against a sunlit sky, detailed isolated fantasy item art.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Borrow the Falling Step** — The runner twists a falling body's momentum into a nearby hanging root, redirecting a disastrous plunge into one extra controlled leap.

Best used when: A partner slips beside a swinging root while the final tether is still unfastened.

Catch: The borrowed force returns after the next landing and may tear loose any knot that was not double-secured.

Art: A hollow-bodied athlete turning sideways in midair among hanging roots, a long curving silver kinetic arc redirecting a falling companion onto a rope walkway, sunlit quince leaves and wind streaming in two directions, dynamic athletic fantasy illustration.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **The Final Tether** — In The Orchard That Wouldn't Land, at Rootfall Circuit, Second Noon reaches the last dangling mooring root as the entire orchard begins rotating toward the village. A fellow runner is trapped beneath the finish rope; fastening the root saves the houses but leaves the runner falling, while cutting the finish rope allows a rescue but turns the orchard directly over the school.

## Four private Zuzu/Emperor object-catalog candidates
These are proposals for Kind Robots LOCATION/setting objects and Character models, not new gameplay. Neither is inserted into the games' playable rosters, routes or campaigns.

### Zuzu Locations (2)
- **Saltwind Rail Yard** [zuzu-gamebook / arthemy] private catalog candidate
  - art direction: A dry white salt flat under a hard blue sky, a row of rusting steam locomotives lashed down with chains, anthropomorphic goats in wide hats hauling crates, a striped gust flag snapping on a tall pole, a low sheet of salt dust blowing across the ground, long shadows.
  - best scene: A gust flag goes up in the middle of a deal and a dozen goats grab the same crate at once while a sheet of white salt blows horizontally across the yard.
  - known for: A rusting rail yard on a dry salt flat where anthropomorphic goats trade wind-polished cargo and every parked locomotive is lashed down against the hourly gusts.
  - local rule: No cargo may be unlashed while a gust flag is flying, and the flag is raised by whoever first feels grit on their teeth.
- **Cinder Ford** [emperor-of-dust / illustrious] private catalog candidate
  - art direction: A shallow brown river thick with drifting grey ash, a slumped dam of cinders across the ford, anthropomorphic followers in patched leather wading with shovels and a single raised lamp, a long dim line of the Horde waiting on the bank, scorched hills, low red sun through smoke.
  - best scene: A follower leader wades waist-deep through grey slurry with a lantern held high while the Horde's numbers shuffle patiently on the bank.
  - known for: A shallow river crossing where grey ash from the burning plains settles into a dam each night and must be shovelled free each dawn before the column can wade over.
  - local rule: The first follower leader into the water carries the column's only lamp, and nobody crosses behind a dark lamp.

### Zuzu Characters (2)
- **Marta Quillon, Gust Reader** [zuzu-gamebook / arthemy] private catalog candidate
  - carries: A brass weathervane worn on her wrist spins before a gust arrives, and she has never once let anyone else read it.
  - complication: The vane has begun to point at things that are not the wind, and she refuses to say what.
  - look: A broad anthropomorphic goat with curved horns wrapped in faded red cloth, a patched oilskin coat, a brass wrist-vane mid-spin, squinting into the wind, salt dust on her fur, a rail yard behind her, hard daylight.
  - role drive: She is an anthropomorphic goat who reads the wind for the rail yard, deciding when the flag goes up, and no train has left late or lost cargo in her years there.
- **Brannoch the Shoveller** [emperor-of-dust / illustrious] private catalog candidate
  - carries: A long-handled iron shovel scarred from old fires leans on his shoulder, its blade polished to a mirror by years of grey ash.
  - complication: He has started to refuse to call the ford open when the Horde is hungry, and nobody can say whether that is wisdom or fear.
  - look: A heavy anthropomorphic badger in soot-black leather with a pale stripe down the nose, a polished iron shovel on one shoulder, grey ash on his shoulders, a lamp hung from his belt, standing in shallow ashen water, low red light.
  - role drive: He is the follower leader who clears the ford each dawn, and the column crosses only after he has called it open.
