## The idea
A colony of fairies dropped from another world takes root across a city's colossal phantom-glass floodwall, and the residents who call them invasive discover the glowing nests are becoming part of the structure that keeps the drowned river out.

## Dream vibe (1)
**When the Floodwall Flowers** — The newcomers chose the wall, and the wall answered by growing light.
Art: A colossal translucent floodwall rising through a flooded city at dusk, thousands of tiny luminous fairy nests flowering across one vast pane while people and maintenance boats appear minute below.

## Dream location (1)
- **Phantom Span** — A kilometer-long phantom-glass barrier dividing the Floodline Precinct from a drowned river, now stippled with hanging fairy habitats that glow inside the material instead of on its surface. Local rule: Once a living addition carries structural load, it remains part of the floodwall until engineers, residents, and the river demonstrate a safer arrangement. Best scene: The entire district falls silent as one immense pane flexes in a storm surge and the fairy colony flexes with it, thousands of lit nests tightening into a single luminous lattice. Art: One towering sheet of smoky translucent glass fills most of the frame, dark floodwater pressed against its far side, a sparse constellation of tiny gold nests embedded across it, with miniature rooftops and figures at the base.

## Character (1)
- **Eda Serrin** — A stranded fairy from another world who has become the loudest defender of the colony's right to remain, even though she arrived months before the others and still does not understand why the floodwall welcomed them. A thimble-sized brass speaking horn wired into the shoulder speaker of an abandoned flood-control walker, letting her voice carry across several city blocks without making the machine move. Complication: Residents assume her early arrival makes her the colony's leader; the fairies themselves treat her as an interesting local and keep making collective decisions she learns about at the same time as the humans. Look: A palm-sized fairy in a dark rain cape standing on the shoulder of a forty-meter flood-control walker, translucent wings folded tight, the enormous machine mostly lost in open gray sky behind her.

## Reward item (1)
- **Ghostline Spindle** (ITEM, RARE) — Spins a temporary phantom-glass filament strong enough to suspend fabric or light across building-scale gaps without adding visible supports. Best used when: A Skyline Dresser needs to connect two immense surfaces while leaving streets, water, and flight paths open below. The catch: The filament vanishes after the display is complete, so structures that became dependent on it without notice lose support at the same moment. Look: A hand-sized black ceramic spindle wound with nearly invisible glass thread, each turn catching a thin violet edge-light.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Host the Strange** (SKILL, EPIC) — Reveals one environmental change that lets an introduced population coexist without pretending the host world will remain exactly as it was. Best used when: A new species has already altered a place and removal would cause as much damage as arrival. The catch: The workable future permanently alters both sides; it never restores a pristine before-state. Look: Pale geometric seams briefly glow across pavement, bark, glass, water, and skin, joining unlike surfaces into one slowly pulsing pattern.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **Let the Wall Decide** — In When the Floodwall Flowers, at Phantom Span, Eda Serrin stands among thousands of residents as the fairy colony and the floodwall begin responding to each other during a river surge; Skyline Dressers turn their city-scale rigging into a shared visual language while the precinct decides how to live with newcomers already woven into its defenses.
