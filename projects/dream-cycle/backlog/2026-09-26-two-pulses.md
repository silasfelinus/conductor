## New Facets (invented for this dream)
- **Merge Ward** (`merge-ward`, SETTING) — A civic district where a household rebuilds its own doorframes and stairwell double-wide the moment a second heartbeat is confirmed under one roof, and keeps the wider version even if that second heartbeat later stops.
- **Pulse Reader** (`pulse-reader`, ROLE) — The one trusted to press an ear or a palm against a merged body and count how many separate hearts are still beating inside it, out loud, so the number can be trusted. They hold no other opinion about a merge, only the count.

## The idea
A humanoid Pulse Reader who has outlived dozens of her own merges is the only one left in Two Pulses who can explain why a young resident's newest heartbeat sounds like it belongs to someone who died decades before the merging program even started.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Second Heartbeat** — Folding two people into one body is a Tuesday-afternoon errand here, and the only thing anyone actually argues about is what number the new pulse should be filed under.
Art: A person mid-stride on a crowded daylit street, one hand pressed flat to their own sternum where a second, visibly separate pulse flutters just under the skin, nobody around them looking twice.

## Dream location (1)
- **Two Pulses** — The oldest working Merge Ward block still standing, its whole row of houses doubled in width the same year the canal beside it still hauled factory freight. Local rule: A doorway stays double-wide for as long as either half of the pair who moved in under it is still walking around, merged or not; knock the wall back down early and the Ward makes you rebuild it out of your own pocket. Best scene: A Pulse Reader presses a palm flat to a resident's chest on their own doorstep, calls out a number nobody was expecting, and the whole street goes quiet enough to hear the second heartbeat everyone else can now hear too. Art: A canal-side row of narrow brick rowhouses, each abruptly doubled in width partway up with a visible mismatched brick seam, twin front doors under one shared roofline, laundry lines strung between doubled windows.

## Character (1)
- **Yuen Kowalczyk** — The Ward's longest-serving Pulse Reader, old enough to have watched the merging program go from a factory experiment to an ordinary civic service, and asked today to certify a pulse count that shouldn't be possible. A low-slung canvas belt hung with a chalk tally-slate and a flat cloth roll of lucky pebbles gathered from a dozen different regions, each one wax-sealed to a scrap of the port where it was found. Complication: Every pebble in the roll came from a place her current merge partner has never once set foot in, and she can no longer tell whether the collection is hers or a memory she folded in along the way. Look: Ageless in the specific way only true antiques are, skin evenly weathered no matter which merge last reshaped it, an athletic, unremarkable build currently settled somewhere between soft and lean depending which half of them is speaking. A crown of stiff black-and-grey braids piled improbably high and pinned with mismatched hardware, always a few pins from collapse. A steady, appraising half-smile that never quite resolves into a full one, like they are already listening past whoever is in front of them.

## Reward item (1)
- **Listening Cuff** (ITEM, UNCOMMON) — Wrapped snug around a wrist or a sternum, it weaves a slow, visible thread pattern that traces every separate heartbeat it can hear underneath, one colour per pulse. Best used when: A merge is too recent or too quiet to certify by ear alone and the count needs to be seen, not just heard. The catch: The cloth only weaves what it currently hears; unwrap it mid-reading and the half-finished pattern keeps knitting itself blind, guessing at a pulse that may already have stopped. Look: A wide band of pale, faintly glossy dreamcloth, one edge already dense with looped, multicoloured thread traces, the other edge still bare and waiting.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Pebble Reading** (SKILL, RARE) — Laying out a hand of lucky pebbles from different regions in the order they were found tells which of a merge's two hearts is going to give out first. Best used when: A Pulse Reader needs to warn a household before a second heartbeat fails quietly on its own. The catch: The reading only holds if every pebble in the hand came from a different place; reuse one region twice and the pattern lies flat and tells nothing at all. Look: A short row of small stones of visibly different colour and grain laid out on bare wood, one already turned face-down where the reading has already been given.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **Third Pulse** — In Two Pulses, where a Second Heartbeat under one roof is nobody's business but the household's own, longtime Pulse Reader Yuen Kowalczyk is asked to certify a newlywed pair's merge and instead hears a third, older pulse keeping perfect time beneath the two everyone expected.
