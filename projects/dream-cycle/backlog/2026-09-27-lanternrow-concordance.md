## The idea
Once a century a city's collected shelves roll out into the street for one sunlit day, and the crowd that gathers to answer its riddles accidentally closes the gap between volumes that were never meant to sit side by side.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Concordance Fair** — Once every century the collection opens every shelf at once, and the whole city turns out to answer what steps through.
Art: A sunlit plaza crowded with banner-strung bookstalls and rolling shelf-carts of pale moon-resin, paper caracal kites bobbing overhead, food-sellers and jugglers threading between towers of unshelved volumes.

## Dream location (1)
- **Lanternrow Shelfyard** — An open-air book fair that rolls its entire shelving stock into the street once a century, cart after cart of atlases, letters-collections, and volumes never meant to share a spine, all held apart by a single threshold-keeper's watch at the one gap between them. Local rule: No cart may be wheeled past the fountain until the riddle chalked across its flank has been answered aloud by a stranger who did not write it. Best scene: A cart of Lovecraftian atlases and a cart of court almanacs get rolled side by side by accident, and the gap between them briefly shows a corridor that belongs to neither building. Art: Rows of towering wheeled bookshelves cast in pale glowing moon-resin, packed plaza under bright noon sun, chalk riddles scrawled across every cart's flank, strings of unlit paper lanterns crossing overhead despite the daylight, crowds pressing between the carts.

## Character (1)
- **Amara Voss** — She keeps the yard's one true threshold — the gap between two carts that never quite closes on schedule — and is trying to get three centuries' worth of accumulated strangers-in-waiting properly sorted while the carts are still out. The fair used to fall reliably on the coldest cloudless morning each century; lately the resin carts arrive already half-soft, and nobody has said aloud that the schedule itself might be slipping. A ring of resin-cast keys, no two the same size, worn the length of one forearm; none of them open anything anyone has found yet. Complication: The riddle she is best known for inventing has never once been answered correctly, and she is starting to suspect she no longer remembers the answer herself. Look: A caracal-formed woman, ancient about the eyes and unnervingly long in the limb, ears tufted and impossibly tall. Her coat lies in overlapping bands of iridescent plumage where fur would sit on any ordinary cat, catching sunlight in shifting bronze and violet. She wears a trade-quarter coat, azulejo-blue diamonds hand-stitched along the hem, fastened with the blunt riveted buttons of a Ruhr Works tailor. She stands with her weight easy on both feet, chin lifted, mouth already curving into the start of a question before the last one has left it.

## Reward item (1)
- **Ninefold Lantern-Key** (ITEM, RARE) — Near any threshold, it tries the door that has gone unopened longest — often before you've decided you want it open. Best used when: A door, cart, or shelf has been sealed so long that nobody left remembers what was kept behind it. The catch: It does not wait to be asked. Carry it past the wrong threshold and it will try that one instead. Look: A stubby brass key cast around a core of pale moon-resin, nine notches cut unevenly along one edge, its bow worn smooth where generations of thumbs have rubbed it for luck.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Concordant Weave** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — It lets the caster treat any two texts as replies to each other, whether or not their authors ever crossed paths or shared a language. Best used when: Two records contradict each other and no one still living can say which one lied. The catch: The weave only ever reconciles — it will never rule which text is true, only how both might coexist. Look: Twin ribbons of pale resin-light unspooling from two open books at once, braiding into a single cord in the air above the caster's open palms.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **Nine Wrong Answers** — At Concordance Fair in Lanternrow Shelfyard, Amara Voss's oldest riddle finally gets an answer — nine of them, shouted out by nine strangers before she can complete the question. For one afternoon each answer briefly becomes the yard's real history, until the crowd rules on which, if any, gets to stay true.
