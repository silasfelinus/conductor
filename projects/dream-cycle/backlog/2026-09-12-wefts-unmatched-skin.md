## New Facets (invented for this dream)
- **Joy Scout** (`joy-scout`, ROLE) — A person who enters unfamiliar communal play first so others can watch curiosity happen without being volunteered themselves. The role makes room for awkwardness and refuses to turn bravery into authority.
- **Playable Habitat** (`playable-habitat`, SETTING) — A place whose everyday structures invite games, climbing, balancing, or improvisation as part of ordinary life. Repeated play gradually reshapes the space, so recreation becomes a form of local adaptation.

## The idea
Deep below a ruined city, a leech-folk neighborhood discovers a sealed living chamber while playing pulseball, a communal sport whose warm fungal ball follows body heat, and the strange new court turns a mystery into a joyful encounter with an organ their habitat forgot it had.

## Dream vibe (1)
**Pell's Pulseball** — Nobody remembers who invented the game, but every tunnel knows the sound of a good bounce.
Art: A crowded underground court grown from pale fungus and black volcanic stone, leech-folk players reaching for a warm glowing ball while fleshy wall membranes flex around them.

## Dream location (1)
- **Warmwall Court** — A broad cavern court where layered fungus mats and brimstone ribs have been polished smooth by generations of climbing, bouncing, and improvised games. Local rule: The newest playable surface belongs first to whoever finds a way to make everyone laugh on it. Best scene: A pulseball ricochets into a sealed wall, which dimples like skin and opens into a huge breathing chamber that none of the neighborhood elders remember. Art: A subterranean sports court of pale fungal cushions, sulfur-yellow mineral ribs, chalk marks, hand-worn climbing holds, and one vast membrane wall folding open like a slow eyelid.

## Character (1)
- **Pell** — They volunteer to be the first player through the newly opened chamber because curiosity should look like play before it looks like conquest. Pell carries a patched satchel of chalk, waxed cord, and six soft bubble-wands used for marking safe landing zones. Complication: The chamber reacts most strongly to Pell's body heat, so the neighborhood keeps treating them as an interpreter when Pell would rather remain just another player. Look: A compact leech-folk athlete with glossy dark skin, a sleeveless padded vest, chalk-whitened palms, bright rubber knee guards, and a ring of iridescent soap bubbles drifting around one wrist.

## Reward item (1)
- **Habitat Knuckle** (ITEM, RARE) — Pressed against a wall, floor, root, shell, or machine casing, it reveals the nearest place that surface was shaped by repeated play rather than work. Best used when: A community needs to understand how people actually inhabit a place instead of how it was designed to be used. The catch: It ignores official boundaries completely and may reveal beloved shortcuts through spaces someone considers private. Look: A fist-sized lump of sulfur-yellow stone polished flat on six faces, each face worn with a different pattern of finger grooves and scuffs.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Bubble Skip** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — The user can push off any thin flexible surface as though it were a taut bubble membrane, turning walls, leaves, cloth, and even giant fungal caps into temporary springboards. Best used when: Movement through a cramped or vertical place needs improvisation rather than force. The catch: Each surface works only once before relaxing back to normal, so retracing the exact route is impossible. Look: A boot touches a pale membrane wall and a clear rainbow-rimmed bubble briefly spreads beneath the sole before snapping the body sideways through the air.

## Scenario (1, authored last)
- **Nobody Keeps Score Down Here** — In Pell's Pulseball at Warmwall Court, Pell uses the Habitat Knuckle and Bubble Skip to help the neighborhood turn the opened living chamber into a new game space while learning which of its moving surfaces are invitations, reflexes, or signs that the habitat itself has begun playing back.
