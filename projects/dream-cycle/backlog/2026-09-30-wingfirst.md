## The idea
A single vast iceberg-island, home to a puffin colony and an ancient ship's engine long fused into its ice, only holds its course through a wraith-thick fog strait because the whole flock steers it as one at dusk — and the bird who always ends up at the front of that formation has to decide whether to let a dead friend's last frozen word go free.

## Dream location (1)
- **Icejaw** — A single iceberg-island the size of a headland, calved decades ago with an old ship's iron engine still fused whole into its underside, drifting an endless slow circuit through a narrow, permanently fog-choked strait. Local rule: No one flies solo through the strait after dusk — the fog only recognizes the shape of the whole flock moving together, and a lone flyer reads to it as prey instead. Best scene: At dusk the entire colony lifts off the ice at once and the fog itself seems to make room, opening a clear channel exactly the width and shape of the flock's massed wingspan, closing again the instant the last bird passes through. Art: A single monumental iceberg drifting in flat grey fog, its ice pocked with countless dark burrow-mouths, one rusted iron hull-plate breaking the surface at the waterline, a loose skein of small dark birds lifting off into the mist, everything else scaled tiny against the ice.

## Character (1)
- **Temir** — Every dusk flight puts them at the front of the formation though no one ever assigned it to them, so when a wraith drifts close enough to touch the flock, they're the one who ends up close enough to speak it back — and lately more of them are drifting close. A fist of Voicefrost worn on a plateau-forged wire loop against their chest, holding the last thing their flying-partner ever said, and a row of small bells strung caravan-fashion along one wingtip, chiming faintly with every wingbeat. Complication: Letting the frozen word go free means losing the last of their partner for good; keeping it frozen means flying at the front every dusk not knowing whether the fog is closing because of the strait or because of what they refuse to let go. Look: Built stockier than the rest of the colony, with a section of one wing-joint replaced years ago in dull, seamed Liquid Gold after an old injury -- a clean repair now, flat and settled, nothing still moving under it. Every other puffin's beak fades to a duller grey-orange by midwinter and brightens again each spring; theirs has held exactly one shade, unmoving, for longer than anyone still living remembers, which unsettles the younger birds more than the gold ever did. Crest feathers dyed and re-dyed a red so vivid it looks unnatural even though it grew in exactly that way, worn deliberately wild rather than preened flat, and their plumage carries both the bright breeding-display crest of a calling mate and the duller diving plumage of a hunter, refusing to settle into just one. Small bells strung caravan-fashion along one wingtip, forged plateau-style, catch what little light there is. Holds their head slightly tilted, always listening past whatever sits directly in front of them -- the patient, unblinking calm of something that measures out its kindness on purpose rather than feeling it all at once.

## Reward item (1)
- **Last Word** (ITEM, RARE) — Warmed slowly in cupped hands, it lets go of the one true thing said to make it, spoken again exactly as it was the first time. Best used when: Someone needs to hear a specific voice say a specific true thing one more time, and there is no other way left to hear it. The catch: It only ever plays that single frozen moment, and once thawed all the way through it never freezes again — the sound is spent, not stored. Look: A clear, fist-sized lump of ice with a single visible ripple frozen mid-air at its center, like a held breath caught between heartbeats, faint frost-ferns spidering out from the core, strung on a loop of dull plateau-forged wire gone slightly green.

## Reward skill (1)
- **Windline** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — Reading the exact place where moving air stops belonging to the living and starts belonging to whatever's drifting in it — a line most flyers never notice they cross. Best used when: Something in the fog needs to be turned back before it reaches solid, living things, rather than fought once it already has. The catch: Once you can see the windline, you can't unsee it — every ordinary gust afterward carries a flicker of what else might be riding it. Look: A visible seam in drifting fog, a hair's width where the mist moves faintly against its own current, marked only by how it drags at loose feathers passing through.
