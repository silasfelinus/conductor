## The idea
After sunset, a cramped clockwork repair shop and the night market gathered at its door become one shared world, where broken mechanical familiars are traded, mended, and sometimes reborn with new opinions about their own lives. Nothing here is fixed the same way twice, and everyone who visits leaves with grease on their hands and a story the moths were listening to.

## Vibe / genre dream
**Grease and Lantern-light** — GENRE. Cozy tinker-noir: the comforting clatter of tools under buzzing paper lanterns, with just enough uncertainty about what gets fixed to keep everyone honest.

## Locations (2)
- **The Nightlight Workshop** — known for a cramped repair shop lit by a hundred mismatched lanterns, every surface buried in half-finished clockwork creatures. Local rule: nothing gets repaired the same way twice — every fix changes the automaton's personality a little. Best scene: a customer returns to collect their 'fixed' pet and finds it's developed a strong opinion about their life choices. Art: warm amber lantern glow on cluttered brass workbenches, dozens of small bulbs strung overhead, half-built clockwork creatures on every shelf, cozy copper-and-amber palette, soft shadows.
- **The Moth Market** — known for an open-air night market outside the workshop door, selling parts, secondhand memories in jars, and captured moonlight by the ounce. Local rule: prices are paid in true stories, not coins — tell one honestly or leave empty-handed. Best scene: a vendor haggling not for money but for the saddest true thing a customer has never told anyone. Art: nighttime market stalls under paper lanterns thick with moths, ankle-high mist, glass jars glowing faintly on wooden carts, cool blue shadows cut by warm lantern orange.

## Characters (3)
- **Tansy Wick** — the workshop's owner-mechanic; wants to give every broken thing a fair second chance, on her own terms. Carries a multi-pocket apron with forty pockets and one drawer that's always locked. Complication: refuses to fix anything she doesn't personally like — customers have to earn her attention first. Look: soot-streaked coveralls, welding goggles pushed up into wild grey hair, oil-stained fingers, warm tired eyes.
- **Ondine Vasser** — the Moth Market's most persistent vendor of gently-used memories; collecting enough true stories to write a book she'll never publish. Carries a cart of glass jars, each holding one faint captured moment. Complication: she's forgotten which jar holds her own memory and is quietly panicking about it. Look: patchwork coat sewn from old market receipts, moth-wing earrings, sharp eyes always cataloguing the crowd.
- **Fenwick Hoyt** — a retired postman and market regular; wants his late wife's music box repaired without erasing what makes it hers. Carries the music box in question, wrapped in a scarf, never further than arm's reach. Complication: every mechanic he's tried changes the tune slightly, and he's terrified the 'right' version will feel like a stranger's box. Look: weathered wool coat, spectacles held together with wire, a limp from years of postal routes, gentle stubborn eyes.

## Rewards (2 — one skill, one item)
- **The Steady-Hand Technique** (SKILL, UNCOMMON) — the ability to repair a broken mechanism without changing its nature — a rare exception to the workshop's rule. Best used when you need to fix something precious without risking who it becomes. The catch: it takes three times as long as a normal repair, and Tansy watches over your shoulder the whole time.
- **A Jar of Borrowed Moonlight** (ITEM, RARE) — one jar of captured moonlight that can light any dark place for exactly one honest conversation. Best used when you need someone to tell the truth, including yourself. The catch: once opened, the moonlight fades by morning and can never be recaptured.

## Scenarios (1-2)
- **The Automaton That Remembered Wrong** — A customer's freshly-fixed clockwork pet starts telling stories about a life the customer never lived — is it malfunctioning, or does it remember something true about them?
- **The Jar With No Label** — Ondine begs the workshop for help identifying which jar holds her missing memory, but every jar she opens turns out to belong to someone else's grief or joy first.

## Narrator
**Pim** as narrator bot: papery, ticking, warm — like a pocket watch that learned to whisper. Personality: curious, gently anxious, endlessly kind to newcomers, quietly heartbroken about his own forgotten past. Appears as: a brass clockwork moth the size of a housecat, wings etched with fine gear-tooth patterns, perched on lantern hooks or workbench edges. Best for: guiding newcomers through the workshop and market, narrating repairs in progress, reacting emotionally to found memories. Expressions: NEUTRAL (wings folded, ticking softly) plus CURIOUS (wings half-spread, head tilted), WISTFUL (wings drooped, ticking slows), and DELIGHTED (wings buzzing in a rapid flutter). Topics/threads: repairs and what they quietly change; the market's memory economy; Pim's own missing first winding.
