## One-breath pitch

Midnight City's smarmiest agent, Odette Harker, built MONSTRIQUE, the monster-girl franchise that
has sold beach movies, disco specials, dolls, reality TV and an idol group for sixty years. Someone
stabbed her and her hired date in Room 205 of the Last Call Motor Lodge. Now her ghost wants
answers, and she's hired the city's most squeamish blogger. You're his assistant. You touch
everything.

## Tone: low morals, high craft

Leisure Suit Larry's leer, Monkey Island's logic, Thimbleweed Park's dependency charts. The rules
that keep it funny and not sour:

- **Innuendo beats explicitness.** Steamy scenes cut away on a comic beat (the ice machine clanks,
  a neon letter burns out, Norb knocks with his elbow). Scrapbook art is suggestive pin-up glamour,
  never explicit.
- **The joke punches at power and vanity**: agents, franchises, fandom, Norb's neuroses, Iris's own
  shamelessness. Never at a woman's worth, a body type or a monster's species.
- **Everyone is an adult in on the joke.** Consent is never the punchline. Stalking is never cute:
  Gavin gets consequences.
- **Violet is a professional, not a prop.** Her jokes are about the job (rates, costumes, clients,
  overtime in the afterlife), and she is the sharpest witness in the game.
- **Sex-positive and queer-positive.** Iris is bi and unbothered. Odette and Violet's date was
  fun until it wasn't. The Ooze Lounge is a gay disco that outlived the franchise that made it
  famous.
- **Low morals, earned:** Iris bribes, pockets, eavesdrops and lies to doormen. She does not hurt
  anyone who didn't have it coming. Odette has it coming, posthumously.
- **Norb rebuffs because of germs and the case, never because Iris is undesirable.** The running
  gag is his priorities, not her appeal.

## The investigators

- **Iris Quill** (player). Assistant, photographer, fixer and only pair of hands at *The Unwashed
  Truth*. Broke, bisexual, shameless, extremely good at her job. Her camera is her main tool. She
  flirts with anyone who'll stand still, especially her boss.
- **Norbert "Norb" Vale**, Midnight City's premiere blogger and amateur sleuth. Publisher of *The
  Unwashed Truth: the city's dirt, reported from a safe distance*. He's 4'11", wears latex gloves,
  thick round glasses and a rumpled suit, and has a nose like a doorknob. A brilliant deducer who
  refuses to touch any object or person. He's all about the case. His rebuffs are a catalog that
  escalates across the game, e.g. *"Iris, I have seen your phone under a blacklight."* The finale
  pays it off with an elbow bump, not a kiss.

## The client and her roommate

- **Odette Harker**, ghost and client. She ran modern Monstrique licensing and inherited the
  archives of every older recording, likeness and appearance contract. She didn't sign the 1960s
  paperwork; she just kept profiting from it. *"Ten percent of everything, darling, including your
  soul."* She was fifty-eight when she died, but her ghost looks thirty-five because ghosts appear
  as they see themselves. Violet notices this out loud. Odette insists on the investigation
  and pays in **the Harker Files**, her blackmail archive. Each file she releases opens a star's
  secret, which is how the motive web grows. Her secret is a lifelong unrequited crush on Draculina,
  which is why she hired a woman to dress as a vampire.
- **Violet Wisp**, ghost, professional companion. She was hired for one night in a cape and plastic
  fangs, and now she's stuck haunting Room 205 with her client for eternity. *"Worst. Overnight.
  Ever."* Odette died before paying her, and that unpaid fee is Violet's running favor quest. She
  observes the room (sounds, smells, the door, the ice bucket) while Odette remembers people and
  contracts. The two ghosts bicker like a divorced couple who never dated.
- **Both ghosts are bound to Room 205** until the killer is named. Every chapter loops back to the
  motel to report, show evidence and set the two accounts against each other. Room 205 is the hub.

## Motel regulars

- **Marta**, the night manager since 1965. Thick foundation, gloves and a scarf in every season.
  Helpful, dry, always "just refilling the ice". (Proposed culprit; see the case.)
- **Pip**, the maintenance man, who keeps a log of every clank the ice machine makes.
- **Gavin**, the creepy fan with a telephoto lens. Never the killer. His photos are evidence, and
  he gets reported anyway.

## Monstrique through the years: the has-beens and the still-has-its

FRANCHISE-HISTORY.md is the ledger: seven albums from 1964 to 2024, each with one star erased and a
replacement hired, a public story and a private one, and an original sleeve versus a doctored
reissue. Silas's brief asks the player to follow that path. The game turns each erasure into:

- **A place:** where the erased star landed (see Midnight City above).
- **A motive:** Odette inherited the archive and kept erasing her on every reissue. Each **Harker
  File** confirms one doctored credit.
- **A favour:** each star's personal problem, which feeds the gratitude system below.
- **A piece of the knife:** see the case.

The has-beens and the still-has-its, in ledger terms: Draculina, Roxie Lupin, Laguna and Cleo Pappatra
were all dropped and came back stronger, so they still have it. Francesca, Vespa, Blobzo and
Siobhan were dropped and built cult careers elsewhere, so they're has-beens only to the franchise.
**Albina / Allie Gator absolutely got in**: she was a billed founding act in the 1963 Monstrique
roadshow, quietly removed before the first iconic 1964 *Beach Hex* album. The seven public album-era
excisions stay intact; her erased roadshow contract is a secret **eighth/pre-album erasure**.
The hot-tub hopefuls are still auditioning; Albina is a genuine, devastated veteran, not one of them.

## Performer identities and the sewer star (2026-11-07 PT)

The franchise's apparent-age hierarchy is a deliberate visual inversion. **Draculina** looks about
20, the youngest-looking member, yet has been an adult for centuries and is a charter member. She
operates like a ruthless school-clique queen: compliments as weapons, reputation management,
favor-trading and social humiliations. Do not render her as a child or make her demeanor cartoonishly
incompetent. Her presentational innocence protects the **Chapter 6** revelation, not an early spoiler.

**Siobhan MacRae** is second-youngest *in appearance*, an adult Scottish werewolf performer with
wild hair/fur, big collar and a bubbly pop past in multi-species girl group **The Pack**. Her former
co-lead **Nyx Sable**, a sleek were-panther, is a competitive frenemy: Siobhan says Nyx stole her
solo, Nyx says Siobhan leaked their demo, and both benefited from an Odette-negotiated feud. Each
knows the other's contribution to the missing master-tape puzzle; reconciliation, a professional
truce and rivalry are all valid earned outcomes, not a forced romance.

**Cleo Pappatra** visibly reads as one of the oldest performers, dresses with historical authority,
and treats everyone else's trends as something she survived twice. **Albina / Allie Gator**, over
six feet of albino alligator in lipstick and an extravagant, aging starlet robe, was a **billed
founder** in Monstrique's pre-album 1963 roadshow. A sponsor demanded a more photogenic lead, so
management erased her and claimed she was never a member. Her sewer dressing room is a faded
Hollywood-star shrine: cracked spotlights, moth-eaten posters, silent-film reel, and recorded
applause. She performs to an empty chair. The joke is her operatic self-mythology, never her body.
A signed first-run roadshow program and edited 1964 reissue prove her exclusion, contributing to
Iris's historical rights case; no gratuitous puzzle asks the player to mock her or promise fame.
Detailed portrait/scene/scrapbook briefs are in **CHARACTER-LOOKBOOK.md**.

## The case: Draculina, revealed in the last act (Silas, 2026-11-07 PT)

Silas, 2026-11-07 PT: *"I like draculina for the villain. But in the last act."*

**The killer is Countess Draculina.** No player should be able to name her with confidence before
Chapter 6. Chapters 1–5 build a convincing wrong answer (Marta) and leave Draculina looking cleared.
Then the last act turns it over.

**Motive.** Odette loved Draculina all her life and managed her like a possession. She erased her
from the 1994 *Silver Bite* line-up, brought her back in 2006 on worse terms, and owned her
likeness. For the reboot she was quietly test-casting a younger, human "Draculina". On the night,
she hired Violet in a licensed Draculina costume (wig, cape, the accent) to play the woman she
could never have. Draculina found out through the costume shop, Hex & Giggles, which gossips.

**Method.** A vampire comes in as mist, under the door: *"the door never opened."* A vampire has no
reflection, and both women were facing the mirrored wall, so Odette saw only the knife move: *"the
knife floated, darling."* She needed no invitation, because Odette gave her a standing one in 1964:
*"Come up to my room anytime."* The weapon was on the nightstand: the long-lost fifth Hexblade, which
Odette had bought at auction as a "stake" for the role-play. Draculina killed Odette, and killed
Violet for wearing her face.

**The misdirect: Marta, "Little Miss Nobody".** The invisible 1964 drummer was erased before anyone
else (FRANCHISE-HISTORY's first, hidden erasure). The weapon was her own Hexblade, her foundation is
on the ice bucket, and she *was* in the room. She carried the ice up, spotted her stolen knife in
Odette's luggage, wiped off her makeup and stayed invisible to take it back. Then she watched the
murder from the corner, panicked and fled with the bucket she had touched. Invisible, she also
explains "the door never opened" and "the knife floated". By the end of Chapter 5 every clue points
at her. Accusing her is a real, playable ending (*Little Miss Scapegoat*).

**Chapter 6 turns it** with evidence the player has carried since earlier chapters:

1. **The mist on the stairs.** Gavin's time-stamped courtyard photos (Chapter 1) show a curl of fog
   climbing to 205 at the double clank. Iris dismissed it as a cheap lens.
2. **The alibi tape.** Draculina was "on camera at the mansion all night", which shows an empty chair
   and her voice; nobody questions that for a vampire. The audio is lifted from 1999's *Monstrique
   Manor*: a co-star who died in 2003 laughs in the background.
3. **Marta's testimony.** Once Iris can prove Marta couldn't have struck (her knife was taken from
   her hand; the ice log puts her on the stairs), Marta finally talks. She saw no one, smelled grave
   soil, and heard the accent.
4. **The costume order.** Hex & Giggles' order book shows the Draculina costume and a phone call
   asking who it was for, from the mansion's line.
5. **Violet, re-asked.** In costume, Violet was the only person in the room facing the *door*, not the
   mirror. With the right evidence she remembers the cold and the voice: *"She said, 'Nice wig.'"*

Fair-play rules: every one of these has a second route, and none needs a romance or favour.
Gratitude may only open a shortcut. Iris can flirt with Draculina all game, and the reveal lands
harder if the player liked her.

**Endings (target 5):**
- *Last Call*: full proof. Draculina is unmasked, and the ghosts are freed together.
- *Little Miss Somebody*: full proof plus every erased star's favour done. A corrected reissue credits
  everyone, Marta included. Bittersweet.
- *Little Miss Scapegoat*: accuse Marta. She's arrested, the Countess walks, and the ghosts stay stuck.
- *Headline Too Early*: thin proof. Norb's blog is sued into dust.
- *Ten Percent Forever*: take Odette's bribe to bury it. Rich, damned, and Violet never forgives you.

## The gratitude system ("a monster woman can be very grateful")

- **Flirt** is a verb. Anyone may be flirted with. Responses scale with that character's
  **gratitude**, and rejection is funny, never punishing.
- **Gratitude comes from favors**: solve a star's personal problem, usually an era-flavoured
  inventory puzzle. Gratitude unlocks, in order: a glamour card → a candid pin-up → a non-explicit
  "Thank You" scene that fades on a comic cutaway → an optional relationship epilogue.
- **Gratitude never gates a required clue.** A grateful star may offer a shortcut, a hint or a
  second route.
- **Norb is never a route.** He keeps a rebuff counter that escalates, and the elbow bump is his
  finale.
- **Favor seeds:** Draculina wants a photo, since she hasn't seen her face since 1890 and Iris's
  camera is silver-free. Roxie wants her stolen prop knife back from Gavin. Laguna's skin dries out
  in the studio, and the motel ice machine helps. Cleo Pappatra wants her 1986 billing restored on a
  reissue. Francesca wants credit for her props, so bring her the 1964 invoice. Blobzo wants one
  night solid (motel ice!). Vespa wants a date who can hold eye contact (Norb's spare glasses).
  Siobhan wants the Pack's masters. Allie wants an audition tape (Iris's camera). Violet wants her
  fee (Odette's purse, negotiated between two ghosts). Odette wants a kinder obituary on Norb's
  blog.

## Puzzle principles (Thimbleweed rules)

- Keep a **dependency chart per chapter** in the engine tests. Every required clue has two routes.
  No dead ends and no lost items.
- **"Norb sees, Iris does."** Norb's look/ask deductions reveal what Iris's hands should try next.
  His neuroses are puzzle tools: his hand sanitizer is flammable and dissolves slime, and a germ
  lecture clears any room.
- Combinations are absurd but fair, and every wrong combination gets a joke.
- The camera is the master key: photographs are evidence, favors, blackmail and alibis.

  reasonable thing for teens to get at her high school. And she rocked it."*), a **white streak** in her

  every character comments on (`iris-neck`, `iris-tattoo`, mature edition only). The boyfriend's identity
