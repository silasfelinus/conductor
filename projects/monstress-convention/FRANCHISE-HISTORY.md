# MONSTRIQUE: Seven Decades of Screams
_2026-11-06 PT · author-facing franchise canon · internal project slug remains monstress-convention_

## Franchise identity
**MONSTRIQUE** is the canonical name of the game and the female-monster movie, television, fashion and recording empire founded in 1963. The corporate brand is bigger than its changing performers. The Monstrique Convention is a distinct city location, not the starting motel. Old MONSTREXX branding is retired from the product; *Monstrexx* may appear only as an explicitly fictional failed/disputed vintage mark, never as the current title. **Monstrexxx** was a notorious, crass 2000s brand experiment, not the game name.

The franchise turns monsters into adult film actresses, idols, fashion models and personalities. A performer can be a founding member, guest star, understudy, solo artist, or a recurring hopeful without all of those being equivalent. Every decade has a distinct sonic palette, visual-production process and rights dispute. These are original fictional acts influenced by broad pop-history eras, not replicas of actual album sleeves or performers.

**Continuity:** Roxie Lupin is the original werewolf star; Siobhan is a separate younger Pack performer, not a 1960s band member. The pack contains wolf, fox, badger, hyena and other were-animal performers. Lagoon performer Laguna is more frog/salamander-like than human: amphibious muzzle/eyes, slick skin, external gill/frill structure and distinct creature posture. Dr. Vespa has genuine fly anatomy, humor and talent. Blobzo is a recognizable slime creature, not a glossy woman with a slime costume. Nefertari is the living mummy actress, never the motel murder victim.

## The acts and the losers' lounge
- **The Pack:** were-animal garage and idol offshoot. Siobhan's tartan, oversized collar, shaggy hair, youth and Scottish spark are part of her visual identity; Roxie may advise them but is not a member by default.
- **Monstrique: NEXT:** 2010s idol reboot. Its rehearsals, photocards and digital subscription contracts are mystery clues, not just collectibles.
- **Monstrexxx:** the 2000s marketing scandal. Its abandoned promotional contracts make embarrassing evidence with real money attached.
- **The Last Call hot tub:** unofficial audition and gossip lounge for aspirants not quite admitted to the franchise. Vespa and Blobzo may appear here after their expulsions, joined by other hopefuls. This must not imply that they never had real careers.
- **Albina / Allie Gator:** albino alligator woman beneath the motel, an unmistakably bipedal reptile with lipstick rather than a human supermodel with scales. She wants celebrity despite a frustrating lack of conventional charisma; nevertheless she has wit, competence and an independent story.

## Publishing history is evidence
The murder victim **Odette Harker** managed modern Monstrique licensing and inherited archives of older recording, likeness and appearance contracts. She did **not** personally sign paperwork in the 1960s. Her office holds reissued sleeves with erased performers, altered publishing credits and falsified endorsement signatures. The **seven erasures** form a deliberate collectible evidence chain, each involving a public press release, contemporaneous original sleeve, revised reissue, and contract correction. The discrepancy opens character-specific interviews and city leads, not automatic proof of murder.

- Original **Beach Hex** sleeve hides a scratched-out Francesca photograph; the remaster removes her production credit.
- **Velvet Crypt** original credits include a reversed Laguna songwriting claim.
- **Night Creatures** has a missing Nefertari royalty barcode beside a glossy Vespa launch decal.
- **Silver Bite** includes a torn Draculina tour-schedule insert bearing an ex-manager stamp.
- **Back From the Grave** features an 'indefinite hiatus' Vespa scandal clipping.
- **Blood Moon** idol photocards omit Blobzo but an early mock-up retains her name.
- **Six Decades of Screams** sold a premium Siobhan collector insert even after she was removed from the final line-up.

The player compares editions in a **Monstrique Scrapbook**, with distinct character pages, band/era pages, original/remastered variants, unlocked personal reflections, friendship/romance vignettes and earned non-explicit glamour art. Missing art retains a visible image slot, never silently vanishes. Adult content remains optional, cannot gate core clues, and requires the existing persisted mature-opt-in / non-CHILD access rule.

## Art continuity and approval
Seven square generated concept covers already exist in the creative chat (1964, 1974, 1986, 1994, 2006, 2017, 2024). They are *concept exploration*, not confirmed ArtJobs, delivered in-app assets or final character-accurate canon. Some designs reuse the same characters across incompatible lineups. **Review and correct the depicted cast against the ledger before project art intake.** The shared wordmark must retain family resemblance while typography, print aging, styling, photo treatment and stage technology genuinely change with each decade. Never treat a modern stylized vignette with fake paper scratches as sufficient period research.

The reusable creator interface remains an art-led playable stage first, with contextual editing for cast, rooms, hotspot edges, scene flags, topics, animation poses, prompt revisions and scrapbook unlocks. A static concept mockup is *inspiration*, not a feature-complete editor. Authoring, QA and development remain internal; public Play placement and publication require Silas's separate explicit approval.
