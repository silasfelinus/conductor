## 2026-08-01 | Reviewer (conductor Agent run) | interface-vision/t-003 | pattern

type: pattern

**Subject:** Split t-003 rather than attempting its riskiest half blind — the "flip app.vue's scroll ownership" instruction would have silently broken scrolling on 18+ pages if done without per-page verification the sandbox can't provide.

**Detail:**
- t-003's note directs flipping app.vue's `NuxtPage` wrapper from `overflow-y-auto` to `overflow-hidden` so "the page owns its scroll." Checked first: `grep -L overflow-y-auto components/pages/*.vue` returns 18 of 30 files with no scroll container of their own — they currently depend on the ancestor (the one the task says to stop scrolling) to reach any content taller than the viewport. Flipping it without fixing those 18 first would not "clip below the fold" (today's known bug) — it would make the content unreachable by any means, on every device, for every one of those pages.
- Landed only the two structurally-inert parts of t-003 (one fewer app.vue wrapper, content-host's blanket `:deep(*)` narrowed to `:deep(:first-child)`) in kind_robots PR #1255. Both verified by `vue-tsc`, `eslint`, `prettier`, and the newly-shipped `layout-contract` ratchet (0 new violations) — genuinely zero behavior change, just structure.
- Split the scroll-ownership flip into a new task, t-003b, explicitly scoped to include the per-page audit/backfill it needs, and noted it should probably merge with t-004's pass over the same files (one-header rule) rather than being a separate sweep.
- Also caught, unrelated to t-003 itself: `prettier --write app.vue` reformatted ~8 unrelated blank-line spots elsewhere in the file (pre-existing drift, confirmed via `git stash` that `prettier --check` already failed on `main` before my edit). Reverted and reapplied the structural edit by hand instead of running `--write` on the whole file, to keep the diff scoped to the task. Worth a dedicated `prettier --write .` pass as its own small task at some point, but not bundled into this one.

**Suggested action:** When a task note gives an explicit instruction that turns out to have a wide, hard-to-verify blast radius (especially in this sandbox with no local dev/DB), it's fine to land the safe subset and split the risky remainder into its own task rather than attempting it wholesale — same "scope" failure-triage category as an oversized task, just self-identified before attempting rather than after failing.
