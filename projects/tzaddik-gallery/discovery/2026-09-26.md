# Daily Tzaddik discovery docket — 2026-09-26

First manual seed for the future Daily Tzaddik pipeline. These are **suggestions
for Silas to vet**, not canonical members. Wikipedia is the default source;
every candidate still needs the normal ingest-time living/deceased verification,
image provenance check, and controversy review.

## Living suggestions

### José Andrés — Spain / United States — disaster food relief
**Pitch:** Chef and founder of World Central Kitchen, which built a model for
rapidly serving meals after disasters and during humanitarian emergencies.
Interesting Tzaddik case because the useful thing is intensely practical:
kitchens, logistics, local cooks, hot food, now.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Andr%C3%A9s

**Review note:** WCK works in active conflicts and disasters, so controversies
around access, neutrality, partnerships, worker safety, or specific deployments
must be distinguished from claims about Andrés personally.

### Leymah Gbowee — Liberia — peacebuilding
**Pitch:** Led the Women of Liberia Mass Action for Peace, uniting Christian and
Muslim women in nonviolent pressure that helped bring Liberia's civil war to an
end. A strong case for the gallery because the achievement is collective,
grassroots, and easy to learn from rather than merely admire.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leymah_Gbowee

**Review note:** Her later political advocacy and alliances should be described
factually without turning the profile into an endorsement of Liberian parties or
officeholders.

### Peter Tabichi — Kenya — education
**Pitch:** Kenyan science teacher and Franciscan friar known for working in an
under-resourced rural school, supporting students financially, building science
clubs, and helping students reach international competitions. He is exactly the
kind of less-American, quietly concrete candidate this project should surface.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Tabichi

**Review note:** Verify current role and the frequently repeated claim that he
donated 80% of his salary before presenting it as current practice.

### Nemonte Nenquimo — Ecuador — Indigenous stewardship
**Pitch:** Waorani leader and co-founder of Indigenous-led organizations working
to protect Amazon rainforest homelands and Indigenous governance. Her work makes
a useful Tzaddik case because it joins environmental protection with community
self-determination rather than treating the forest as scenery.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemonte_Nenquimo

**Review note:** Her lawsuits and advocacy are politically contested. Describe
the legal outcomes and stated positions of affected governments/industries
without deciding those political disputes for the reader.

### Shabana Basij-Rasikh — Afghanistan — girls' education
**Pitch:** Afghan educator who co-founded the School of Leadership, Afghanistan
(SOLA), the country's first boarding school for girls, and continued the school
after the Taliban's return to power. Her story turns "education matters" into a
specific institution built and kept alive under extreme pressure.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabana_Basij-Rasikh

**Review note:** Her work is inseparable from contested Afghan politics and
security conditions. Keep the profile centered on documented educational work
rather than broad geopolitical claims.

### Kailash Satyarthi — India — child labor and education
**Pitch:** Left engineering for a career fighting child labor and trafficking,
built Bachpan Bachao Andolan and international campaigns, and shared the 2014
Nobel Peace Prize for children's rights and education. The case is unusually
strong because it spans direct rescue, rehabilitation, organizing, and policy
change.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailash_Satyarthi

**Review note:** Large rescue-count and impact claims should be tied to the
organization or Nobel source that reports them rather than repeated as
unqualified site voice.

### Boyan Slat — Netherlands — ocean plastic cleanup
**Pitch:** Founded The Ocean Cleanup as a teenager and spent more than a decade
turning a speculative engineering proposal into deployed ocean and river
collection systems. A useful science/engineering Tzaddik candidate because the
story includes iteration, failed designs, measurement, and persistence.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyan_Slat

**Review note:** The Ocean Cleanup has drawn substantive criticism over
effectiveness, ecological tradeoffs, cost, and whether upstream prevention is
more important. Those objections belong prominently in the profile.

### Narayanan Krishnan — India — food and dignity for homeless people
**Pitch:** Former hotel chef who founded Akshaya Trust after leaving a culinary
career to feed homeless and mentally ill people in Madurai. His work is vivid,
local, and grounded in recurring care rather than one dramatic event.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayanan_Krishnan

**Review note:** This one has a real controversy requirement: a former resident
made a sexual-abuse allegation involving the trust; the Madras High Court later
exonerated the home while ordering monitoring. The detail page must represent
that sequence accurately rather than sanitizing or sensationalizing it.

### Azim Khamisa — Kenya / United States — restorative justice
**Pitch:** After his son Tariq was murdered, Khamisa publicly forgave the
teenager who killed him and founded the Tariq Khamisa Foundation with the goal
of preventing youth violence. Later, the man convicted of the killing joined
the foundation's board after release. It is a startling, teachable example of
restorative justice practiced personally rather than merely advocated.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azim_Khamisa

**Review note:** The site's pitch should not imply that forgiveness is an
obligation for victims. Present Khamisa's response as his choice and philosophy.

### Maggie Doyne — United States / Nepal — community education and care
**Pitch:** Began supporting children in Nepal as a teenager and co-founded
BlinkNow, which grew into a school, children's home, women's center, clinic, and
other community services in Surkhet. Worth considering because her work evolved
from individual charity into a long-running local institution.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Doyne

**Review note:** A deeper review should examine governance, local leadership,
and the familiar risks of outsider-centered humanitarian storytelling before
the profile treats the founder narrative as the whole story.

## Memorial suggestions

### Wangarĩ Maathai — Kenya — environment, democracy, women's organizing
**Pitch:** Founded the Green Belt Movement, linking tree planting with women's
livelihoods, environmental protection, and democratic participation, and became
the first African woman awarded the Nobel Peace Prize. She gives the Memorial
gallery a model where ecological work and civic life are inseparable.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wangar%C4%A9_Maathai

**Review note:** Her career included direct political opposition and elected
office. Keep factual civic history separate from any judgment about Kenyan
parties or governments.

### Abdul Sattar Edhi — Pakistan — emergency and social care
**Pitch:** Built the Edhi Foundation into a vast privately supported network of
ambulances, shelters, orphanages, rehabilitation services, and other care across
Pakistan. This is almost suspiciously on-brand for the premise: build the
infrastructure that catches people when everything else fails.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Sattar_Edhi

**Review note:** Verify large scale claims against current foundation or
independent sources. His ascetic public image should not substitute for ordinary
organizational scrutiny.

### Hawa Abdi — Somalia — medicine and refuge
**Pitch:** Somali physician and founder of the Dr. Hawa Abdi Foundation, who
kept providing medical care and refuge through decades of conflict. Her work
belongs in the educational mission because it shows institution-building under
conditions where institutions themselves were collapsing.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawa_Abdi

**Review note:** Conflict-zone numbers vary widely by source and year. Prefer
dated counts and specific services over heroic aggregate figures.

### Fazle Hasan Abed — Bangladesh — anti-poverty institution building
**Pitch:** Founded BRAC after Bangladesh's liberation war, growing a relief
effort into one of the world's largest development organizations across
education, health, microfinance, agriculture, and livelihoods. The Tzaddik case
is not one rescue story but designing systems that millions of people can use.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazle_Hasan_Abed

**Review note:** BRAC's scale makes it important to cover critiques of
microfinance and development-NGO power alongside its documented reach and
services.

### Chiune Sugihara — Japan / Lithuania — Holocaust rescue
**Pitch:** Japanese diplomat in Lithuania who issued transit visas that enabled
thousands of Jewish refugees to escape during World War II, despite serious
career risk. A direct fit for the gallery's interest in people who chose humane
action inside systems pressuring them the other way.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiune_Sugihara

**Review note:** Popular retellings vary on exact numbers and on how directly
orders were defied. Use the documented visa record and serious Holocaust
sources rather than mythologized versions.

### Irena Sendler — Poland — Holocaust rescue and social work
**Pitch:** Polish social worker and member of Żegota who participated in a
network smuggling Jewish children out of the Warsaw Ghetto, arranging false
identities and shelter, and protecting information about them under arrest and
torture. The story also usefully resists lone-hero framing because her work
depended on a wider network.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irena_Sendler

**Review note:** The often-repeated "2,500 children" figure and lone-rescuer
narrative should be presented carefully; scholarship emphasizes the broader
network of rescuers.

### Janusz Korczak — Poland — children's dignity
**Pitch:** Pediatrician, writer, and educator who developed unusually
child-centered ideas about rights and respect, ran a Warsaw orphanage, and
refused opportunities to abandon the children when they were deported to
Treblinka. His case combines a lifetime of institutional practice with the
terrible final act people usually remember.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janusz_Korczak

**Review note:** Do not reduce him to the manner of his death. The educational
work and children's-rights philosophy are the more useful reason to include him.

### Norman Borlaug — United States / Mexico / South Asia — food security
**Pitch:** Agronomist whose disease-resistant, high-yield wheat breeding and
agricultural programs became central to the Green Revolution and major increases
in food production. He is a good controversy-rich Tzaddik candidate because a
huge humanitarian impact case exists alongside serious environmental and
agricultural-system criticism.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Borlaug

**Review note:** The Green Revolution is debated for fertilizer and irrigation
dependence, ecological effects, inequality, and agricultural monoculture.
Those critiques are part of the story, not a footnote.

### Paul Farmer — United States / Haiti / Rwanda — health equity
**Pitch:** Physician and medical anthropologist who co-founded Partners In
Health and argued that excellent medical care for poor communities was a
practical obligation, not an unrealistic luxury. His projects in Haiti, Rwanda,
and elsewhere helped make "structural violence" a concrete health-system
question.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Farmer

**Review note:** The profile should include serious discussion of NGO power,
foreign-expert dynamics, and critiques of aid models, not just Farmer's personal
charisma.

### Jane Goodall — United Kingdom / Tanzania — primatology and conservation
**Pitch:** Transformed the study of chimpanzees through long-term field
observation at Gombe and spent decades turning scientific fame into conservation
and animal-welfare advocacy. Her recent death also makes her a useful test of
the system's rule that living status must be re-verified daily.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Goodall

**Review note:** Include scientific critiques of early anthropomorphic naming
and provisioning practices, and distinguish later conservation advocacy from
the evidence base of her original field research.
