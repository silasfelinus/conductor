# Living-36 candidate research pool

Built for tzaddik-gallery/t-010, feeding the t-011 canonical-36 selection gate
(FOR SILAS). This is a standing **research pool**, distinct from the daily
discovery docket (dated `discovery/<date>.md` files) — it lives in the same
`discovery/` directory (and uses the same heading/section format) specifically
so `excluded_names()`'s dedup scan picks it up automatically and no future
daily docket re-suggests one of these people. None of these are canonical
members; canonicalization only happens at t-011.

Deliberately biased toward international, non-Anglosphere, non-celebrity
cases per the project brief, and deduplicated against `seed-sets.yaml` and
the existing `2026-09-26.md`/`2026-09-28.md` dockets — no living name below
repeats one already docketed there. All are living as of this research pass;
living/deceased status, Wikipedia identity, and image provenance still need
the normal ingest-time refresh before any of these become an actual
`TzaddikCandidate` record, same as every other docket in this project.

Controlled tags below match `utils/tzaddikTags.ts`'s taxonomy (Politics, Pop
Culture, Humanitarian, Science & Medicine, Education, Environment, Civil
Rights & Justice, Peace & Diplomacy, Community & Mutual Aid, Arts & Culture,
Journalism & Truth, Courage & Rescue) so tag/geography concentration can be
inspected directly, per t-010's own instruction.

## Living suggestions

### Muhammad Yunus — Bangladesh — microfinance economics
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "living", "tags": ["Humanitarian", "Community & Mutual Aid"]} -->
**Pitch:** Founded Grameen Bank and pioneered microcredit lending to people,
mostly women, excluded from conventional banking; shared the 2006 Nobel Peace
Prize for this work.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Yunus

**Review note:** Removed from Grameen Bank's leadership in 2011 amid a
dispute with the Bangladeshi government; in 2024 became Chief Adviser of
Bangladesh's interim government, moving from humanitarian figure to active
head-of-government — a genuinely different kind of public role that any
profile must state plainly rather than soften.

### Maria Ressa — Philippines — journalism
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "living", "tags": ["Journalism & Truth", "Civil Rights & Justice"]} -->
**Pitch:** Co-founded Rappler and reported on disinformation and press
freedom under the Duterte government; shared the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ressa

**Review note:** Faced Philippine tax-fraud and cyber-libel charges widely
characterized internationally as politically motivated retaliation; state
both the charges and that characterization as attributed claims, not settled
fact.

### Dmitry Muratov — Russia — journalism
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "living", "tags": ["Journalism & Truth", "Peace & Diplomacy"]} -->
**Pitch:** Longtime editor of Novaya Gazeta, which lost six journalists
(including Anna Politkovskaya) to killings tied to their reporting; shared
the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize and auctioned his medal for Ukrainian refugee
relief.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Muratov

**Review note:** Novaya Gazeta suspended Russian print/online operations in
2022 after the invasion of Ukraine; his case sits directly inside an active
geopolitical conflict, so framing needs care to describe facts without
taking a side on the war itself.

### Shirin Ebadi — Iran — human rights law
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "living", "tags": ["Civil Rights & Justice", "Peace & Diplomacy"]} -->
**Pitch:** Iran's first female judge before the 1979 revolution barred women
from the bench; later a human rights lawyer representing dissidents,
children, and women, and the first Iranian and first Muslim woman to win the
Nobel Peace Prize (2003).

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirin_Ebadi

**Review note:** Has lived in exile since 2009 and is outspokenly critical of
the Iranian government, which disputes her legitimacy; politically charged
subject, keep language factual/attributed.

<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "living", "tags": ["Peace & Diplomacy", "Civil Rights & Justice"]} -->
**Pitch:** Coordinated the International Campaign to Ban Landmines, which

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jody_Williams

**Review note:** No significant controversy found; verify current (2026)

<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "living", "tags": ["Science & Medicine", "Community & Mutual Aid"]} -->
**Pitch:** Trained nurse-midwife and former Somaliland foreign minister who

maternal and infant mortality and train midwives.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_Adan_Ismail

**Review note:** Somaliland's disputed international statehood status

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isatou_Ceesay

**Review note:** No significant controversy found; public sourcing is
