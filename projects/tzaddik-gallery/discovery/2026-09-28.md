## Memorial suggestions

### Berta Cáceres — Honduras — Indigenous and environmental rights organizing
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Environment", "Civil Rights & Justice", "Courage & Rescue"]} -->
**Pitch:** Lenca activist who co-founded COPINH and led opposition to the Agua Zarca dam on land sacred to the Lenca people, winning the Goldman Environmental Prize in 2015 before being assassinated in her home in 2016.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berta_Cáceres

**Review note:** Her killing led to convictions including a dam-company executive; the case remains a documented example of violence against Indigenous and environmental defenders in Honduras and should be presented with that documented outcome, not left as an open mystery.

### Iqbal Masih — Pakistan — child-labor abolition activism
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Civil Rights & Justice", "Courage & Rescue"]} -->
**Pitch:** Escaped bonded labor in a carpet factory at age 10, then became an internationally known campaigner against child labor and bonded servitude before being killed in 1995 at age 12.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iqbal_Masih

**Review note:** The circumstances and motive of his killing remain disputed between accounts blaming the 'carpet mafia' and accounts describing an unrelated local dispute; both versions should be represented rather than presenting either as settled fact.

### Ken Saro-Wiwa — Nigeria — writing and Ogoni environmental-rights activism
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Environment", "Journalism & Truth", "Civil Rights & Justice"]} -->
**Pitch:** Writer and television producer who led the Movement for the Survival of the Ogoni People, campaigning against oil-industry pollution in the Niger Delta, before being executed by Nigeria's military government in 1995.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Saro-Wiwa

**Review note:** His trial was widely condemned internationally as a sham; Shell's role and later settlement of a related civil suit (without admitting liability) is a documented, contested part of the story that belongs in the profile.

### Vasili Arkhipov — Soviet Union / Russia — averting nuclear war
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Courage & Rescue", "Peace & Diplomacy"]} -->
**Pitch:** Soviet naval officer serving on a submarine during the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis whose refusal to authorize a nuclear torpedo launch, when the other two required officers voted to fire, is widely credited with helping avert nuclear war.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Arkhipov

**Review note:** Details of the incident were classified for decades and rely on accounts that emerged well after the fact; the profile should attribute specifics to the historians/participants who documented them rather than presenting a single cinematic account as certain.

### Chico Mendes — Brazil — rainforest and rubber-tapper rights organizing
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Environment", "Courage & Rescue", "Civil Rights & Justice"]} -->
**Pitch:** Rubber tapper who organized fellow seringueiros to defend the Amazon rainforest from clear-cutting through nonviolent human blockades ('empates') and helped establish the extractive-reserve model, before being assassinated by ranchers in 1988.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_Mendes

**Review note:** His killers (a rancher and his son) were convicted, escaped, and were later recaptured; that documented resolution should be included rather than leaving the case open-ended.

### Marie Curie — Poland / France — physics, chemistry, and radioactivity research
<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Science & Medicine"]} -->
**Pitch:** Physicist and chemist who discovered polonium and radium, developed mobile X-ray units used on WWI battlefields, and remains the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie

**Review note:** She died of aplastic anemia almost certainly caused by her own prolonged radiation exposure, a documented cost of pioneering work done before radiation's dangers were understood; worth including rather than treating her death as unrelated to the science.

<!-- tzaddik-meta: {"status": "deceased", "tags": ["Humanitarian", "Peace & Diplomacy"]} -->
**Pitch:** Businessman who witnessed the aftermath of the Battle of Solferino in 1859 and, appalled by the lack of care for wounded soldiers, led the effort that created the International Committee of the Red Cross and the Geneva Conventions, sharing the first Nobel Peace Prize in 1901.

**Wikipedia:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dunant

**Review note:** He died in poverty and relative obscurity after a business scandal derailed his earlier career; the profile should include that fall alongside the humanitarian legacy rather than presenting a purely triumphant arc.
