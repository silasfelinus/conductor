# Space Couch: The Crew's Impossible Odysseys

**Production tranche:** Conductor `game-of-captains/t-015`, Silas direction October 26, 2026 PT. This is a deliberate creative recalibration: crew errands and paperwork-only plot beats are **not** epic adventures. Our tonal mixture is towering tall tales, cosmic bureaucratic nightmares, point-and-click traps and inventory logic, sinister political mysticism, philosophical comedy, and plans that fail in the most entertaining imaginable way. Use original settings, characters, dialogue, moral dilemmas and physical set pieces rather than borrowing protected scenes or named franchises.

The executable source for this tranche is `kind_robots/utils/gameOfCaptains/crewEpics{A,B,C}.ts` (36 authored episodes), `crewFinalCalls.ts` (72 bespoke final decisions), `crewEpics.ts` (12 authored conditional epilogues, including public/protected/unfinished). The in-app crew-quest choice becomes a **two-stage episode**: choose an outrageous gambit, receive a reversal proving the mission is not what it seemed, choose how to deal with the newly revealed cost, and carry a unique relic and consequences into later stops and the galactic ending. Retain legacy `CrewId` values and the original v1 saved-game key. Existing counters and three-chapter chronology remain valid.

## Narrative design contract

**Spectacle first, ethics inside the spectacle.** Each scene should deliver a major, specific physical absurdity with theatrical scale; it must be playable without needing a long legal briefing. For example, show a moon clocking dead workers into a warship factory, then discover those wages have become life support for living children. The dilemma becomes stronger *because* the spectacle created the complication.

**Three-act journeys within each voyage.** Every officer's personal story has one episode per crew-quest chapter across three distinct locations. Stage one poses a daring, concrete plan. Stage two unveils a specific reversal. Stage three grants a named object/evidence item and world-specific aftermath. The same public versus consent-protecting resolution yields different personal epilogues; unfinished personal journeys remain unresolved rather than magically concluded.

**Captain and crew choices have consequences.** The captain may protect local privacy at the cost of supplies or make an urgent public intervention at the cost of trust and added nemesis attention. Mixing an initially loud plan with a discreet final resolution carries an additional complication. The outcome is stored as individual crew journey history with unique prize and initial gambit. It feeds villain counterplay and the earned finale. Crew cannot be lost merely for rejecting romance or declining a relationship.

**Mox's independent sentience is inviolate.** The point of Mox's adventure is deciding whether and how to remember, become a person, choose a birthday, and disagree with independently minded copies. The player cannot 'collect' Mox's copies as upgrade parts or erase their consent for XP.

**Three-person crew interactions remain a later expansion gate.** Current epics belong to each crew member, and companions contribute through existing mission synergies and romance paths. The 220-trio authored adventures and 440 trio endings are separate content quotas. Do not claim those counts fulfilled by this 36-episode tranche.

## Art production: not yet delivered

The intended minimum **scene-art coverage**, at full production quality, is one distinct illustrated opener and one major-reversal picture for each of the 36 stops, plus outcome variants and twelve crew endings. That suggests roughly 36+36+72+12 = **156 illustration slots**, pending practical deduplication and artistic direction. This estimate is an inventory, **not** 156 completed ArtJobs or an art queue submission.

For art prompts, show literal spatial relations and joke machinery: red couch within invisible bubble, uploaded ghost-shift cranes, a colossal golden sentient payslip, full-sized city in a cocktail olive, an android facing seventeen contradictory copies, a jailed musical note, a reactor launching itself on holiday, an autonomous suit of speaking border boots. Every comic prop has a reason to exist in the plot. Define the subject and emotional beat per frame, record verified ArtJob IDs and integrate returned assets into the game's manifest. No replacement SVG should be passed off as painted quest art.

## Adversarial acceptance gates

- All 12 arcs have exactly 3 mutually distinct locations, all 36 have unique scene titles, reversals, outcomes and named relics, and all 72 second-stage choices are specific to the actual crisis.
- From an ordinary fresh run, select an officer, clear the mission, initiate a personal quest, choose an initial gambit, experience the reversal, choose a final resolution, and read a genuinely bespoke aftermath. Prove save/restore between those two choices and no duplicate rewards.
- Across three chapters, each of the 12 officers must be able to finish an entire personal saga from an ordinary fresh game and earn one of two distinct full personal epilogues. A fragmented or unresolved arc yields a third, clearly incomplete epilogue.
- Resources cannot go negative; a player with no supplies retains a non-costly way to resolve the episode. Nemesis-sensitive evidence remains earned, not injected.
- Verify responsive mobile/tablet/desktop scroll and that all required narrative is actually visible. Generate verified art through the authorized pipeline in a separate delivery task.
- The larger 16-person cast promise, 30 mission families, 40 encounter families, full three acts, boss-specific endings, and complete scene artwork remain open production gates.

**Deliberate tone:** A cosmic tragedy is sometimes a moon trapped in a time clock. A cosmic joke is that the missing hours have been sold as a luxury retirement product. An adventure is when the player must decide whom to save, what to steal, and whether their crew can live with the answer.
