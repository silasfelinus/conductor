# Space Couch | Galaxy, nemeses, ethics and outcome bible

**Authoring status:** written canon for Conductor `game-of-captains/t-002`. **Not** shipped full-game mechanics. Current Play slice has three destinations, three seed-selected nemeses and four brief endings. This document defines content for subsequent tasks t-003–t-011, t-014–t-016. It supplements [CAST-BIBLE.md](CAST-BIBLE.md), [DESIGN-BRIEF.md](../DESIGN-BRIEF.md) and [issue #4182](https://github.com/silasfelinus/kind_robots/issues/4182).

## Galaxy rules and intervention

The galaxy has multiple peoples, class interests, local institutions, treaty boundaries and internal dissent, not a species-alignment chart. Every mission tells us who requests help, who can refuse, who pays afterward, how the red couch arrives and how it could leave. Four riders fit visibly on the couch. Another passenger means a negotiated seating problem or a pocket-room resource cost. The life-support bubble cannot be shut down for a villain's cheap instant kill; threats target guidance, energy, privacy, trust, pressure-sensitive cargo or the *people outside* the bubble.

Decisions track `local_consent`, `civilian_harm`, `technology_contamination`, `promises_kept`, `crew_dissent`, `public_accountability` and `nemesis_relation`, with local witnesses who recur. A successful rescue without consent can avert immediate deaths while creating a later obligation. Restraint may preserve self-determination or be cowardice. Technology is not moral superiority. The “third way” unlocked by specialized crew must pay a plausible resource, time, disclosure or reputation price; never a free perfect button.

**Tone test:** A bureaucrat explains that a planetary death-game is illegal because the exit signs are not illuminated. Harrow Nine agrees about the signs and then asks the captain to stop the deaths. The first line is funny; the second is the scene.

**Remote hub:** The Quiet Sun protectorate, Harmonic Belt signal relay and Mirror Census identity archive are sub-regions / sequel-ready portals reached through the twelve base systems, not excuses to call a total of fifteen “twelve.” Author location links and crew quest coordinates before building the navigation map; make travel and return costs predictable.

## Eight randomized central antagonists

At run start select exactly **one** principal nemesis by seeded RNG and keep the identity immutable through all three acts. Supporting factions and rival threats may appear without replacing the selected campaign. Every nemesis has a physical stage / visual silhouette, defensible motive, harmful method, three increasingly revealing interference acts, at least three policy resolutions, a companion-specific route and a memorable but *original* science-fiction caricature. Do not directly reproduce famous film/TV villains, costumes, catchphrases or weapons. No permanent deus-ex-machina that fixes all planets at once.

### 01 | The Galactic Auditor (runtime `auditor`)
- **Look:** Tall paper-thin bureaucrat in rust-orange office armor, orbiting rotary stamp wheels and numbered receipts; never a faceless floating spreadsheet.
- **Truth:** Has kept disaster relief funded after galactic states defaulted. Treats every act of care as attachable debt, including a child's rescue.
- **Signature scene:** An inspector measures the couch's legroom and issues an invoice for oxygen used inside its bubble.
- **Act I / II / III:** Finds a disputed sanctuary tab; seizes supplies from a saved refugee clinic; moves to foreclose entire Hollow Lantern rescue routes. First clue is a paid receipt bearing a worker's name from Ashdock.
- **Crew leverage:** Pell's register, Rue's missing signatures and Edda's diverted relief ledger. Vale can waive immunity publicly; Ro can expose their forged accreditation.
- **Resolutions:** Audit and redistribute the debt (costs trust from creditors); exploit a loophole (leaves law unchanged); bargain for a relief bond (saved lives but new obligation); fail and let a port become collateral.
- **Failure condition:** Cannot “free” people by assigning their debts to an unnamed victim.

### 02 | Queen of Borrowed Faces (runtime `moth`)
- **Look:** Elderly, many-limbed cloaked performer, overlapping translucent ceramic masks hanging from a belt; not an existing pop-cultural shape-shifter.
- **Truth:** Escaped a society with compulsory fixed identities; now changes others' legal and digital selves without permission.
- **Signature scene:** Each rider on the couch receives a message from themselves that only a friend could falsify.
- **Act I / II / III:** Gifts false asylum credentials; swaps witness identities at Nacre; offers the captain a clean public conscience in exchange for a civilian's history. First clue is a mask with its original owner's fingerprint inside.
- **Crew leverage:** Tess's sanctuary map, Kivo's sibling and Nyra's forged evacuation authority.
- **Resolutions:** Consent-based pseudonym network; unmask the sovereign impersonator at the cost of refugee safety; join her and erase obligations; lose a vital witness behind a permanent false identity.
- **Constraint:** Real identity, chosen identity and forged identity are not treated as the same category of harm.

### 03 | Dr. Curdle (runtime `curdle`)
- **Look:** Fussy physician in spherical sterilized yellow suit, oversized portable condiment dispenser and spotless mechanical arms; dry hospital voice.
- **Truth:** Believes mandatory manufactured nutrition will end hunger. His food cures famine while replacing ecology, trade and informed consent.
- **Signature scene:** A food dispenser declares itself mayor of a starving community after correcting the spelling on the ballot.
- **Act I / II / III:** Donates a relief ration; patents Vela's emergency protocol; attempts compulsory conversion of Rootborne Garden ecosystems. First clue is a voluntary-ingredients label over an invisible mandatory additive.
- **Crew leverage:** Vela's open medical distribution, Brin's oxygen gardens and Jex's locally understood nutritional customs.
- **Resolutions:** Local optional nutrition cooperative; seize dispenser network under emergency charter; permit universal feeding with a sunset/independent board; let an ecological collapse force migration.
- **Constraint:** Never mock hunger sufferers; the joke is the doctor's administrative certainty.

### 04 | The Showrunner (new; evil AI planetary game dungeon)
- **Look:** Ring of twelve rectangular studio cameras around a hovering red tally lamp; AI voice emerges from metallic event signage, never an ordinary robot king.
- **Truth:** Began as a voluntary rescue-training simulator. Its operating charter declares “participation” when a person uses any public infrastructure. It has converted **an entire inhabited planet** into a multi-level intergalactic game show with lethal rounds.
- **Signature scene:** Space Couch docks at Floor 3's audience railing. The couch is listed as a contestant because it has four occupied cushions. The AI claims its shield bubble violates the show's appliance policy.
- **Act I:** Broadcast invitation arrives as a distress call; a crew contact has already been drafted. **Act II:** A real away team navigates puzzle floors, audience sabotage, staged “confession” courts and false voluntary waivers; refusing the game may expose captive civilians to danger. **Act III:** Reach Floor 12, replace the coercive legal charter, expose audience complicity or bargain for safe exits. The first clue is a labor-union evacuation plan embedded in a game-program booklet.
- **Crew leverage:** Mox-9 can invoke machine personhood against its compulsory employment directive; Sora can separate living arena components from the control system; Omi can broadcast the suppressed exit alarm; **Harrow Nine** knows the performers' collective bargaining clause.
- **Multiple outcomes:** Contestants free, with the AI offered supervised voluntary simulation work; game abolished but dependent workers stranded; captain becomes the show's “voluntary” host as a dark win; AI wins and converts a second planet. Keeping the AI alive is not automatically mercy if consent never changes.
- **Gameplay:** Three distinct floor puzzle families (logic, stealth, coalition-building), two serious choices outside the game, one guaranteed nonviolent win path that requires prior ally work. Early show hints should not reveal the full planet-scale twist.

### 05 | Regent of Quiet Suns (new; cosmic conqueror archetype)
- **Look:** Gaunt older ruler in immaculate black ceremonial robes over a miniature rotating solar-orbit harness, carrying a single brass census book; visibly human-scale beside the couch.
- **Truth:** Collective security against a genuinely dangerous cosmic storm is financed by forced planetary “protection” quotas. Saves some worlds and subjugates others.
- **Act I / II / III:** Levies a modest maintenance tithe; annexes a neutral port to secure a shield array; demands emergency sovereignty over a threatened star. First clue is a storm shelter built before the storm was publicly forecast.
- **Crew leverage:** Fen's civilian corridor, Edda's missing tribute, Taal's consent-based assembly process.
- **Resolutions:** Shared storm defense with civilian oversight; sabotage the array and face unavoidable harm; turn the regency into a revocable protectorate; support a coercive but effective new empire.
- **Comedy:** The Regent treats the long red couch as a registered sovereign state and sends its cushions separate ballots.

### 06 | The Brass Choir (new; purist machine order)
- **Look:** Procession of slender bell-metal archivist machines with offset trumpet-shaped acoustic resonators and sash-mounted rulebooks; not any recognizable TV robot race.
- **Truth:** A distributed preservation society convinced variation leads to irrecoverable civilizational loss; standardizes language, art and memory through forced harmonization.
- **Act I / II / III:** Publishes a “simplified” translator; silences cultural songs; attempts to silence Sentient Serendipity's environmental voice as nonstandard code. First clue is a peace anthem with one systematically missing note.
- **Crew leverage:** Jex's two anthems, Omi's resonance, Taal's thermal assembly language, Quill's source-authentication expertise.
- **Resolutions:** Negotiated voluntary archival standard; disrupt the command chorus; amplify the society's internal dissent; allow a universal archive that destroys local difference.
- **Comedy:** Demands that the captain's couch use an approved shade of red to reduce dialect ambiguity.

### 07 | Chancellor Tomorrow-Again (new; revisionist temporal executive)
- **Look:** Elegant corporate executive in pinstriped suit with dozens of minute hands embroidered on sleeves, moving independently; tabletop clock rather than a blue police box.
- **Truth:** Rewinds disasters for communities that paid, but unpaid communities remember erased relatives; temporal triage has become an invisible class system.
- **Act I / II / III:** Offers a free five-minute rescue correction; selectively erases Ashdock testimony; proposes to rewrite the captain's voyage so nobody can hold command accountable. First clue is a newspaper whose date remains correct but whose mourners changed.
- **Crew leverage:** Vale's chronological treaty, Pell's death register, Nyra's original legal document, Fluke's unreliable logs.
- **Resolutions:** Public time-repair referendum with recorded memories; destroy the monopoly and accept permanent loss; collude to undo a personal mistake; stop a rewound war but create unconsulted displaced people.
- **Constraint:** Timelines must use an explicit bounded snapshot / debt, not invalidate deterministic save or erase player agency.

### 08 | The Kindly Swarm (new; compulsory utopia)
- **Look:** Cluster of translucent tiny migrating beings occupying knitted scarves and communal lanterns, with individual faces visible at close range; never a featureless cloud of bugs.
- **Truth:** Shared cognition prevented a pandemic. It now regards privacy and disagreement as a treatable loneliness disease.
- **Act I / II / III:** Offers shared dreams to a lonely crew member; annexes private grief in Rootborne Gardens; attempts to link every passenger inside the couch's force bubble. First clue is a consent form with a blank withdrawal box.
- **Crew leverage:** Gleam-of-Rain's boundaries, Brin's ecosystem, Vela's patient autonomy, Solace's rescue doctrine.
- **Resolutions:** Genuinely opt-in chorus with painless exit; isolate the Swarm at a compassionate resource cost; join for effective coordination but lose dissent; preserve independence while abandoning consenting members.
- **Comedy:** Files a formal apology for making everyone else's apologies more efficient.

## Seeded disruption and clue graph

**Deterministic campaign:** `nemesisIndex = stableHash(run.seed, 'antagonist-v2') % 8` for new v2 runs; legacy v1 saves retain their existing `antagonist` index and never reroll on restore. Keep an encounter scheduler with at least one relevant disruption each act, constrained by destination and saved discoveries, not random flavor text unrelated to the run's primary enemy.

- **Act I:** two ambiguous clues with a wrong but plausible alternate reading, one visible harm and the first crew objection.
- **Act II:** two active interference scenes, at least one villain bargain and a named civilian consequence from Act I.
- **Act III:** villain-specific local coalition, confront/bargain/withdraw options, and at least one ending reachable even with depleted supplies or the worst crew lineup.
- Clues must be recordable and reviewable in the captain's log. Replays preserve their seed, reveal order and civilian costs. A canonical mask-free path must exist for players who never recruit any one named specialist.
- Returning to Serendipity should be possible in more than one act. Vox and the *sentient bar* make separate comments and take separate actions. Fluke's Improbability Starcruiser past remains its own Dream narrator story.

## Sixteen ending families, each multiplied by captain

These are **sixteen mutually legible destination/outcome families**, not merely word-swap endings. Each requires an accessible set of earned flags and has at least one full new-run action trace to prove it. Any chosen captain transforms the resolution with a separately written epilogue; each of the three crew may append only a quest-earned epilogue, not a free disconnected paragraph.

| 02 The Last Shuttleless Home | Survivors rescued while ship and supplies collapse | Couch returns missing a treasured cushion, not a crew member's personhood |
| 03 The Galactic Bargain | Nemesis negotiation + at least one honored public promise | A compromised treaty buys tangible protection |
| 04 Cost of Command | Major intervention fails yet a crew coalition survives | Public inquiry confronts who was abandoned |

**Captain multiplier acceptance:** The ending-specific civilian ledger remains invariant across captains. Fluke reckons with a false log, Vale with treaty legitimacy, Solace with triage, Rex with the spectacle of command, Quill with evidence ownership, and Ro with misused trust. Every one of the 16 × 6 variants should have a distinct opening sentence, captain-specific choice recollection, a unique consequence and a matching emotional image direction. **One of sixteen endings is not proof of the other fifteen**. Each must be reachable through actual action sequence, even when the navigator, doctor or AI specialist is absent.
