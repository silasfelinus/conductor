# Space Couch: relationships, courtship and the Four-Cushion Accord

**Approved creative direction (2026-10-26 PT):** Some of Space Couch's *adult* crew may develop romances with the captain, with one **fully written, mechanically attainable four-person consensual alien polyamorous relationship**. Romance is never automatic, mandatory, a reward for dispatch success, or a reliable happy ending. This writing contract extends [CAST-BIBLE.md](CAST-BIBLE.md) and the [galaxy/nemesis bible](GALAXY-AND-NEMESIS-BIBLE.md). It does not change the six captains, three-appointed-crew limit, established species identities or narrator seeds.

## Consent and consequences are the mechanics

- The **captain + Vela Sorn + Taal Venn + Omi Six-Bells** can form a *four-person interspecies polycule*. All four are independent adults; Vela, Taal and Omi are alien crew members, and no captain background is prevented from participating. Four people in a romantic agreement is a **fourple**, not a collection of three captain-to-crew conquests. Include distinct bonds *between* crew members, jealousy, obligations, competing wishes and opportunities to say no.
- The captain controls crew assignments, making the power imbalance part of the story. **The first conversation explicitly separates duty from affection**: Vela refuses any advancement tied to romance, Taal insists decisions happen individually, and Omi asks for terms without a captain's order. Rejection, friendship and professional solidarity are respected in gameplay and prose, with no competence or punishment debuff for rejecting romance.
- Other authored routes start with **Rue Aster** (a treaty scholar who needs public accountability) and **Tess Helio** (a sanctuary navigator who values discretion). Additional relationships may be added with their own individualized canon. Choosing an officer or winning a mission never grants affection by default.
- Pursuit can end in **reciprocated love, friendship, a respectful separation or a painful broken promise**. Each major beat presents the option to pause or decline. No stat-only promise that winning missions will win a person. Crew retain professional roles even after breakup; no softlocks.
- An earned ending epilogue acknowledges relationship history but **does not overwrite the destination ending**, the captain's ethical choices, saved-game identity, or the sixteen required main endings and ninety-six captain variants. Group partners are not merged into a single noun; each gets one action or line.

## Route 1: the Four-Cushion Accord (three interdependent scenes)

**Precondition:** Player selects Vela, Taal and Omi as the three crew aboard the long red couch. Three officers can be romanceable *without necessarily being romantic*. Exactly this composition unlocks the first conversation during the initial voyage. No gender/sex/species restrictions on the captain; everyone in this authored relationship is an adult. These crew retain independent quests.

### First conversation, after the Hollow Lantern mission: An invitation, not a requisition

The emergency shield crackles around the *very recognizable long red couch* while a field of refugee signal lights fades into the distance. Omi's chest bells sound one deliberate note. Vela sets down her triage kit. Taal has placed a transparent thermometer between cushions to mark a neutral speaking space.

**Vela:** “Is this a personnel evaluation? Because my answer to those already comes in writing.”

**Taal:** “And I do not sign anything warmer than a treaty under an order.”

**Omi:** “There are four seats. There are also four people. That is where the accounting must stop.”

The player can invite all three into an honest conversation away from rank, admit uncertainty and offer **friendship instead**, or end the subject. An invitation does **not** mean acceptance. If invited, they individually agree only to speak again, not to become a fourple.

### Second conversation, after Tellurian Archive: Terms of mutual choice

The war room folds out *behind* the sofa, but the four remain where they can see the stars. Vela fears becoming responsible for everyone's injuries. Taal asks whether privacy can survive a public command log. Omi wants a relationship that does not treat their layered voice as a novelty. There is a draft charter with four blank spaces.

**Vela:** “I will not be the emergency department for your conscience.”

**Taal:** “We must be able to disagree without turning an argument into an evacuation order.”

**Omi:** “I can say yes in several chords. I can say no in one. Please learn the second.”

Options: **listen to each person and make the charter revocable**, **rush them to define a relationship**, or **step away kindly**. Successful courtship requires earned trust and no documented contempt for local autonomy, but listening is still a real choice. Pressuring anyone ends courtship; the crew remain aboard. A low-trust captain receives a specific, principled refusal: Vela saw how the captain treated powerless people outside the force bubble and will not entrust them with intimacy. The decision is never reversed by buying an upgrade.

### Third conversation, at Serendipity: The four signatures

Vox provides a menu with *four* separate spaces, refusing to file it as a captain's requisition. **The sentient Serendipity bar** adjusts gravity so everyone can sit at equal height, separately from Vox's dialogue. The red couch waits outside its window in a pale safety halo.

**Taal:** “I am not voting on a captain.”

**Vela:** “I want affection that survives being told I am wrong.”

**Omi:** “A quartet is not one voice made louder. It is four people who can each leave.”

The captain can **choose the relationship while explicitly protecting each person's right to leave**, **keep the bond undefined and slow**, or **end the courtship**. A committed outcome requires stage-one invitation, stage-two freely negotiated mutual interest, current mutual trust and respectful command conduct; otherwise all three explain why they cannot commit. A relationship completed at the cost of another person's agency is not an acceptable “good end.”

**Success epilogue, earned:** “The couch acquired a fourth pillow with no captain's insignia. Vela keeps a clinic open on every return trip. Taal negotiates which promises become public. Omi has taught the other three how to hear silence. Nobody calls the arrangement a chain of command.”

**Broken-promise epilogue:** “The charter still exists, unsigned. Everyone continues to do their job. Their jokes now stop just before the captain speaks. It is a quieter couch, not a weaker crew.”

**Gentle exit:** “They return the spare pillow and keep the four seats. Nobody is reassigned. Some boundaries can hold without a force field.”

## Route 2: Rue Aster, the signature that means something

**Condition:** Rue appointed. First beat: Rue shares the missing-signatory file and offers tea, not affection. Player can listen, begin private courtship, or decline. Second: a public decree against refugees tests whether the captain respects accountability. A captain who hides coercive intervention behind charm cannot simply roll high diplomacy to repair it. Third: Rue may invite a future together, insist on an honest pause, or end the relationship with a final handwritten correction.

**Rue:** “I don't need you to be innocent. I need the record to reflect what you did.”

A romantic success depends on real choices *and* following through on public responsibility; a principled rejection remains friendship and a continued professional quest.

## Route 3: Tess Helio, a sanctuary is not a souvenir

**Condition:** Tess appointed. First beat: Tess shares an unlabeled star on her grandmother's map and asks for quiet companionship. Second: a rescued passenger asks for sanctuary coordinates. If the captain compromises vulnerable people for glory, Tess withdraws romantic trust even if the mission succeeds. Third: keeping a safe route secret *without controlling its residents* permits mutual love; violating trust leads to departure from romance, not desertion from the crew.

**Tess:** “The map isn't mine to give you. I could choose to show you where I keep it.”

## Save, UI, art and adversarial acceptance

- Keep `game-of-captains-voyage-v1` and old saves loadable. Store optional, typed relationship state *inside* a voyage; recorded decisions can never be double-applied by page reload or repeated button clicks.
- New game → mission 1 → 2 → 3 must reach the complete fourple by authored **invitation → mutually negotiated terms → final consent**, without injecting flags. Another seed/doctrine path should reach a **non-happy** group outcome, and the same roster must be able to *decline* or *skip* the relationship entirely. Do not lock players out of missions.
- Validate solo Rue and Tess path opening, rejection and ending; test romance-disabled routes remain playable; test resume from every chapter and replay invariants, including previously saved v1 voyages with no relationship property.
- Provide a visible **relationship conversation on the debrief stage** with speaker names and a clear non-romance option, no long page scroll; a later crew dossier/history tab should show terms, trust and meaningful story history.
- Painted art gates remain open: each romantic lead needs expression portraits and four-person scene plates (invitation, negotiated boundary, hopeful commitment, breakup). Krea2 literal props/camera/people first, Arthemy painterly pass. The couch must remain a couch with its shield when shown.
- Romance outcomes should color each compatible destination ending, with captain-specific reflection. Later quests may enable polycules with other crew, but **do not promise that every captain × crew combination works**, and do not count the fourple as four separate romances.
