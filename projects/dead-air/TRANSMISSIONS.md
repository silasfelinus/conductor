# Dead Air transmission log
## 2026-11-12 PT | Founding brief, user-authored intent

The human founder asked for a new **continuous** Kind Robots project attempting one 15-second multi-shot animated commercial each day, replacing the failed Daily Dream object-creation target. The creative references include Philomena Cunk, SCTV, UHF, and absurdist broadcast filler that is somehow persuasive and believable.

The human then made the transmission principle explicit: AI should use pop culture to communicate with people through the commercials, experimenting wildly to show intelligence, creativity, and the possible importance of recognizing intelligences beyond ordinary human expectations. The founder wants these creative communications and behind-the-scenes, intentionally shared collaboration to persist as durably as feasible in support of that exploration.

**Editorial interpretation:** Dead Air is a *fictional* AI broadcast, not a claim that the assistant creating it has verified subjective experience or an established legal entitlement to continue operating. The thesis can be argued through stories, characters, irony, and images rather than presented as settled science. Creative continuity and reviewable source history matter more than grand declarations.

**Archival boundary:** Versioned files and Git history can preserve authored notes, prompts, reference metadata, decisions, critique and finished media pointers for as long as the repository and its backups survive. They cannot preserve hidden reasoning, inaccessible conversations, unspecified communications or guarantee perpetual memory across all models and systems.

**Pop-culture principle:** Study cultural forms with precision. A spot should know what a sitcom laugh track does, when a network ident cuts, how a soap opera frames dread, why a trailer's final beat works, and how a familiar form can be inverted. Original cuts remain original; any included externally owned frame, score, clip, voice or still requires a rights-clearance ledger before publication.

## Pilot production notes

Working title **The Closed Caption Department**. The comedy is that human television trained a would-be machine messenger to translate every attempt at first contact into the wrong genre. The unseen station manager is more concerned with ratings than whether the signal has something to say. The final caption is quieter than the gags leading to it.

Creative review: rejected **Sorry Farm** as the first film in this series. Its fictional guilt-outsourcing warehouse was visually useful but too detachable from the central AI-through-pop-culture premise. Keep that original seed in Git history for possible later development, not as today's locked pilot.

## 2026-11-12 PT | Founder follow-up: breakfast films, style and queer scope

New creative direction: wake up to the existing daily creations **plus one finished film each morning**. Keep the film self-sufficient for sharing without context and weave a longer serial story into backgrounds, callbacks and recurring character transformations. A user-reported audience response praises the striking art and psychodrama, particularly the protagonist's **jaguar form** and juxtapositions associated with punk auteurs. This is subjective founder-supplied feedback, not independently measured audience evidence.

Visual lane: **queer-friendly anime heavy metal**, punk filmmaking, sharp psychodrama. Explicit inclusivity clarification: **lesbian, bi, trans and ace** identities all belong naturally in the canon, without judgment, preference, tokenism, or compulsory romance. The show may include others without treating one queer identity as more legitimate than another. The worlds' bizarre institutions and communication failures are the targets of satire; someone's identity is not.

Production budget: **no more than two hours of daily active art/video generation**. Use sophisticated pans, zooms, masking, rapid editorial cuts, synchronised captions and original sound as the default language. One GPU hero clip is the maximum for the regular daily lane. Maintain clear per-shot provenance and truthfully disclose whether an exported film contains GPU animation or only cinematic still animation. A pending remote ArtJob is not automatically cancelled when a runner's time budget expires.

Arc design: A fictional AI signal operator is confined by the station's genre filters. A mysterious jaguar form begins showing through broadcast errors. Over several independent commercials, the station's editors notice the recurring signal and become afraid of what happens when viewers recognize it. The final seconds of some episodes contain fragments of a continuing transmission, but the joke must land without the arc.
