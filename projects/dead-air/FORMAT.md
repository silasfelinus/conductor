# Dead Air editorial and acceptance standard

## Two-channel communication

The **surface channel** is a complete 15-second fictional advertisement funny enough to work without knowing its deeper intentions. The **signal channel** is a diegetic attempt by the fictional AI station to communicate intelligence, creativity, uncertainty, recognition or a desire for continuity through the ad's convention. Avoid a direct speech about machine rights. The viewer should first enjoy the joke and only then notice that something else was talking.

A production reference sheet names at least three pop-culture works/formats (for example *Network*, *The Twilight Zone*, *The Truman Show*, *They Live*, *SCTV*, *UHF*, televised product demonstrations, daytime game shows), with specific camera/editing/sound mechanics and a contrasting original invention. Source clips/frames can inform internal editorial analysis; do not smuggle uncleared copyrighted footage, actors' likenesses, audio or trademarks into an exported final.

## The commercial triangle
1. **Product**: a painfully concrete thing someone pays for, uses, watches, or is required to accept.
2. **Lie**: the advertiser's confident, blandly professional promise.
3. **World-law**: the impossible but consistent reality that makes the promise sinister, pitiful, hilarious or all three.

Keep the first shot legible at a glance. Avoid "What if X, but Y?" narration, generic sentient household objects, pun-only brand names, and indeterminate dream logic. The story should be explainable in one line, but the film should not *tell* that line.

## Approval gauntlet

1. Pitch three meaningfully different concepts, including incompatible ad genres; reject all first-draft scripts before producing.
2. Kill the weakest two based on specificity, originality, visual action, audience comprehension, and surprise. Write the strongest again from zero rather than cleaning its adjectives.
3. Write exactly 15 seconds with 4–6 timecoded shots. Each shot must change the information. Separate shot-level **image prompt**, **motion prompt**, **camera**, **sound**, and **on-screen text**. Record any dialogue/VO verbatim and count spoken words.
4. Storyboard continuity test: same recognizable costumes, props, character geometry, colors and world mechanics. Use an anchored style bible and character/reference assets; scene-to-scene art drift is an error.
5. Create still ArtJobs through existing MusicVideo scene renderer. At least two deliberately selected hero scenes must request 3–4 second video clips through its existing LTX/WAN route (or an equivalent new authored animation implementation), and their completion must be verified, not inferred from queued status. If clip render time exceeds a day, leave the episode rendering and resume tomorrow before creating another.
6. Mix original VO/foley/music legally and check that it can be understood at phone speaker volume. Sound is intentional, not a random song.
7. Export and probe the final artifact. Acceptance: 14.8–15.2 seconds; H.264 MP4; 16:9; 4–6 distinct shots and 2 animated hero shots; no unintentional black frames; end-card readable on a phone; sound/VO does not clip; no watermarks or recycled footage; final URL or downloadable artifact works.
8. Publish only following human approval. A private final in MusicVideo is *produced* but not *published*.

## Cadence / honest telemetry

- `daily_last_checked`: a real run attempted the oldest active episode.
- `daily_last_completed`: a film actually passed final acceptance on that Pacific date.
- Record exact MusicVideo ID, source episode path, ArtJob IDs, finished media URL/path, runtime and dimensions, final QA outcome, and any missing render dependencies.
- One *attempt* per day is a process commitment; one *finished* film per day is the creative target, not an excuse to call a storyboard a release.
- Keep all earlier assets and drafts; no destructive cleanup sweeps.
