## Latest identity and night-patrol direction (2026-10-28 PT)

**This supersedes any three-named-form, 144-different-character or fixed 288-track requirement below.** There are **48 real people** (one per origin × affinity), **48 permanent hero codenames**, and three *different splash-page circumstances* per person: rookie/sidekick, hero/protagonist, opponent/heel. A role changes choices, ability use, relationships and consequences, not the person's name or fundamental face/silhouette. Granny Mayhem remains Granny Mayhem however she arrives in a campaign. 144 splash *scenes* share 48 canonical designs, with consistent original cast-body and clothing descriptions across image requests. Rendered-art completion still requires genuine ArtJob provenance.

**Night patrol must open with story beats:** the first two nights are solo with distinct internal monologues, no default roster friends. The third night has two simultaneous catastrophes, panic, and a friend who phones and volunteers. The player can split up to attempt both rescues or concentrate for the best result in one and explicitly fail/abandon the other. School is daytime, missions after class, and additional recruitable contacts increase the number of meaningful nightly choices at a controlled rate. Mission success, mixed result, failure and refusal must carry different authored text, plus hidden custom-matchup secrets and persistent callbacks. See source [#4402](https://github.com/silasfelinus/kind_robots/issues/4402), [#4403](https://github.com/silasfelinus/kind_robots/issues/4403) and implementing Kind Robots #4404.

## 1. Title, premise and dramatic question

**Opening and tone, October 26:** The entry screen is a cinematic console title, not an endless enrollment dashboard. Only **New Game / Yearbook / Settings / Tutorial** appear before New Game. Each origin and affinity picker is a separate full-height screen with a large original illustration, context and power risk. Reveal the 48 unique people as physical trading cards with student/sidekick, hero and villain forms, large portraits, biographies, six stat bars, abilities and costs. A first-time player starts as student/sidekick; previously earned hero/villain forms become playable New Game Plus choices recorded in a permanent Yearbook. **Shelter / Unmask / Claim** are three distinct starting philosophies with authored serial adventures across each week, remembered decisions and seed variation. Confirmation opens the tutorial. The target is the witty, dramatically messy ensemble energy of a 1990s superhero soap opera: rescue action, rivalries, relationships with agency, public scandals, betrayals, civic politics and occasional disturbing nanoscale body horror, not a cathedral-centered cyber-mystery. Keep adult relationships non-explicit and all character likenesses original.

**The last thirty days before graduation.** The graduating class at Comet City's elite, frequently dysfunctional superhero academy are all **adults aged 18 or older**. The player selects an original superpower family, their presentation and personal history; hero, villain and independent/rogue identities emerge from their actions. During the **day**, find friends, take classes, run errands, investigate, flirt, recruit, train and explore a connected campus/city. At **night**, work the patrol and mission board with trusted (or compromised) classmates, choose which rescue, crime or rogue opportunity to pursue, manage injuries and watch a run-specific mastermind's plan accelerate. Graduating and surviving prom are not guaranteed, but even an ignominious outcome should make a good story.

Tone: exuberant, witty, romantic, emotionally sincere superhero coming-of-age *for adult students*, with morally troublesome decisions, real consequences, and strange school rituals. Comedy does not trivialize casualties or consent; villain play must be compelling, not simply cruelty for points. **The city survives, changes ownership, is evacuated, is damaged, or is lost only through earned combinations of choices.** Every ending explains who was saved, who was abandoned, what happened to the villain, who stays with the player and what graduation now means.

A **seed-selected principal villain** (target **eight** distinct masterminds in the finished game) owns a *different physical plan to destroy or remake Comet City*, faction methods, milestone clues, henchpeople, and climax routes. Foreshadow from day 1. The player can stop, expose, redirect, join, supplant or outwit the mastermind; good, evil, rogue and hybrid faction dealings materially change available solutions. No plan may instantly win because of one hidden die roll or a missed early clue.

## 2. Systems and the full thirty-day game loop

**Calendar:** 30 playable days, not an indefinite grind. Narrative bands: **Days 1–7** school culture, petty trouble and first villains; **8–14** faction and romance foundations, early real night incidents; **15–21** stakes, betrayal, injuries, first mastermind evidence; **22–28** city under pressure, romance/sidekick commitment and faction schisms; **29–30** graduation, prom and antagonist showdown. Each day has a scheduled authored anchor or thematic beat plus **seeded location-specific draws**, with availability re-keyed by band, day/night, villain, reputation, selected companions and previous flags. Scripts never repeat unique revelations. Keep early filler skippable only when no mandatory decision or consequence is lost.

**Day phase:** open campus or town map → spend a limited number of meaningful time slots on classes, errands, shops, social scenes, investigations, NPC quests and infirmary actions → choose training or preparation as appropriate → dusk debrief. Randomized card pools per week complement, never replace, scripted daily beats. Encounters have useful dialogue, choices with costs and personal consequences, and follow-on versions when revisited.

**Night phase:** mission board reveals **hero**, **villain** and **rogue / shared** contracts. Hero missions rescue or protect, evil missions sabotage, acquire leverage or aid factions, and rogue/shared opportunities may be pursued regardless of alignment but react differently to methods. Assign **1–3 consenting, available followers** to a case; select multiple teams when roster permits. Distinguish Academy **training simulations** (initial readiness tests) from **real** night patrol/combat/crime (injury/civilian stakes). The player also makes at least one consequential direct night decision rather than playing solely as a dispatcher. Show role match, duration, uncertainty, resource requirements and deadline; good teams incur fatigue, personal disagreement and absence from other work. Failure, refusal and mixed success have bespoke future callbacks.

**Injuries:** follower marked `injured` on a real mission is unavailable through the following day's normal recovery window. Spending a **daytime action at the infirmary** may accelerate recovery, with medical costs, consent and other missed opportunities. No impossible roster softlocks; substitutions, negotiation, solo approaches or delayed missions remain viable. Academic/professional repercussions distinct from physical injuries.

**Powers:** six starting families, retained from the prototype: **kinetic**, **technopathy**, **empathy/illusion**, **elemental**, **spatial**, **biotech/morph**. Each offers unique investigative, conversational, rescue and covert choices with credible weaknesses and evolving unlocks; at least two unique successful *routes per family per narrative band*, not a generic +1. A non-powered alternative always exists when narratively plausible. Track distinct **public hero reputation**, **underworld status**, **rogue independence**, **faculty standing**, **civic harm**, **mastermind progress**, **alliances**, **grades**, **evidence**, **resources** and **prom readiness**; don't reduce every moral decision to one good/evil meter.

**Game state:** seeded deterministic encounter/case resolution, day + night + action slots, visited nodes, active scripted flags, faction contract ledger, promise history, follower skills/availability, relationship consent/compatibility, villain identity and independent clock, event/art IDs, discovered collectibles, exact choice transcript, versioned saves/loads and migration of existing `comet-city-academy-slice-v1` local saves. Preserve the seven-day prologue via safe migration or explicit `First Week` mode; never silently corrupt or discard it. No LLM dependency during play; repeatable test seeds.

## 3. Screens, geography, navigation and visual language

**Console-quality, art-first, one visible screen:** artwork is the large interactive stage; compact command icons along **one aligned persistent header/dock** (touch, keyboard, controller), clear focus, no duplicate shell header and no stacked scrolling web-form page. At 390 / 768 / 1440 widths the primary scene and action stay on-screen; secondary panels scroll inside their own bounded space. Use existing shell available height. Switch views instead of compressing images into illegible thumbnails.

**Two geographically consistent maps:** The **Campus map** shows the academy as the majority with a handful of **northwest** Comet City border blocks; the **Town map** shows most of Comet City with **southeast** campus landmarks. Both are projections of **one canonical coordinate graph**, with the **same crossover edges, building IDs, neighboring streets, and reversible navigation**. Do not generate two unrelated paintings and call them maps. There are at least **12 distinct campus nodes plus 12 town nodes** (24 authored total); every node has meaningful day/night activities, changing encounter eligibility and illustrated event history. Player may see patrol conditions, rumors, injured allies, romance availability and villain footprints on maps without advance spoilers.

**Named diegetic full-screen game views** (each has a considered full-size artwork treatment, strong foreground actions and icon/header navigation):
1. **Title / New Game / Continue**: cinematic campus/Comet City at dusk, choose power/avatar, see saved profiles.
2. **Main Hub**: current day/night, latest hero/villain broadcast, scene illustration, short decision summary and one prominent action.
3. **Campus / Town Map**: detailed connected geographic overlays, clickable hotspots, controlled camera/viewport.
4. **Encounter / Story Card**: large image and character portraits, narrative, power/faction choices, legible result presentation.
5. **Dispatch / Patrol Board**: illustrated call reports, hero/villain/shared alignment, time slots, portraits of selectable follower teams (1–3), honest odds, injured/unavailable state.
6. **Followers & Relationships**: painted *large* full-body character illustrations plus smaller emotion portraits, ability, gear, affinity, sidekick choice, injuries, romance/multi-partner status.
7. **Hero Page**: avatar, power evolution, hero/rogue/villain standings, reputation, records, partner(s), sidekick(s), gear and personal history.
8. **Scrapbook / Yearbook**: villain dossiers (spoiler-safe until encountered), important cinematic memories, faction history, romance milestones, all discovered good/evil/rogue endings and their alternate outcome plates; locked images show intentional silhouettes.
9. **Settings / Saves**: save/load slots and version information, card backs/gallery selection, sound, motion/text/contrast/input options, confirmations for destructive overwrites. These are subordinate game settings, not a generic admin app.
10. **Prom / Graduation / Finale**: cinematic stage with act-earned paths and unmistakable consequences.

**Visual design:** premium original illustrated comic/animation game with vivid inked silhouettes, lived-in school/social spaces, dynamic colored-light night rescues, accessible strong contrast. Mobile is not a shrunken desktop: stage and chief action get visual priority, dock collapses labels and secondary panels switch overlays. Artistic targets include wide scenes, maps, character splash and mood portraits, injury poses, mission reports, custom card backs, memorabilia, action/result/ending plates. The existing SVG panorama is **concept-stage art**, not a delivered painted final illustration.

## 4. Cast, romance, sidekicks, antagonists and event content

**Cast bible (2026-10-30):** the sixteen recruits, eight romanceable adults, compatibility rules and both runtime throuple arcs (The Listening Accord, The Night Shift) are canon in [RECRUIT-AND-ROMANCE-BIBLE.md](RECRUIT-AND-ROMANCE-BIBLE.md). Twelve recruits are in runtime and four are authored only.

**Roster:** at least **16 distinctive recruitable classmates, graduates, volunteers and staff** with personalities, biography, ethical outlook, role skill/weakness, private motivations, portrait/splash identity, powers, time availability, affinity trajectory, rivalry/loyalty history, dialogue and absence/injury reactions. Staff roles must be consistent with consent and power dynamics. Recruit by authored social/mission events, not magic roster population; some may defect or leave if mistreated. Include supporting non-follower NPCs and city residents who independently pursue agendas.

**Romance:** at least **eight substantial consenting adult romanceable NPCs** across genders, orientations, body types, backgrounds and visual aesthetics, with agency, character-specific boundaries, conflict, reciprocity, dates, breakups and genuine platonic resolutions. Romance and sidekick/follower categories overlap but are **independent**: someone can be a love interest, field partner, both or neither. Offer **at least two fully authored compatible triad/throuple routes** with all three participants making affirmative decisions and talking to each other, not simply two simultaneous romance flags. Single-partner, non-romantic, queer and non-monogamous routes receive comparable narrative respect. Consent cannot be bought with mission rewards, coerced by teacher/student power, or equated mechanically with alignment. Romantic compatibility affects epilogues, favors and dialogue but is never required to save the city.

**Example cast design slots to flesh out:** scholarship rescue engineer who values individual lives over metrics; glamorous student council fixer with murky allegiances; engineer whose simulations predict future attacks; earnest faculty rescuer burdened by a cover-up; skyway courier who fears intimacy more than danger; med-tech student whose healing favors strangers; student who wants a sidekick but dreads being a symbol; prom organizer whose party budget hides a weapon. Existing prototype IDs (`mina`, `sol`, `imani`, `ash`, `nix`, `kit`) must be reconciled in a canonical **cast bible** rather than renamed or discarded arbitrarily. Write their identities and ages deliberately, not by incidental artwork.

**Villain/case content:** eight masterminds with individual signature art, scheme, clues, minions, ethical contradictions and several ending interactions. At least **30 authored daily anchor events**, **60 unique week-gated random/location cards** with day/night and relationship variants, **36 dispatch/patrol case families**, **24 mapped meaningful sites**, and **12 causally different full-game endings** spanning heroic, evil, rogue and compromised outcomes, plus relationship/romance epilogues. These are *planning floors*, to be revised when adversarial tests prove more breadth needed. Never pad counts with flavor-only actions or generic swapped nouns.

**Art coverage contract per follower:** full-screen splash, consistent character turnaround/identity, large emotional scene states (at least neutral, joyous, angry, afraid, hurt), small matching emotion portraits, and explicit **injured** image state; rare serious injury/withdrawal variants only where authored. Every mission offer, relevant scene, decision consequence (success/mixed/failure/refusal) and ending gets its own prompt, provenance, rendered ArtImage/ArtJob ID and verified final URL. Count **requested / queued / rendered / delivered / verified** separately. Do not credit placeholders, broken URLs or queued jobs as finished.
