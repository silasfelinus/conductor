# Coat Dance — Section-by-Section Creative Treatment

Task: coat-dance/t-004 · Date: 2026-09-22 · Status: first pass, for Silas's creative approval

This treatment groups the 38-row beat map (`source/beatmap.md`) into 11 treatment
sections and proposes, for each, which of the six approved treatment modes
applies: **faithful reconstruction**, **animated transformation**, **impossible
perspective shift**, **silhouette/shadow passage**, **object-manipulation
flourish**, or **return to the human performer and coat**. Several sections
blend two modes where the beat map itself supports it. Every proposal is
grounded in a specific row/timestamp/measurement from the beat map — nothing
here is a generic mood guess.

Two constraints from `roadmap.yaml`'s `creative_constraints` shape every
section below: the coat stays a duet partner, not a prop, and no treatment
should flatten the piece's specific strangeness into generic AI spectacle. Two
structural facts from the beat map shape it too: the entire source is one
static wide proscenium shot with no cuts/pans/zooms, so any reframing is a
deliberate departure worth calling out per-section rather than assumed; and
the original audio is load-bearing (Silas, BRIEF.md Q1/Q2) and is not being
replaced — every treatment below is visual-only, timed to the existing sync
points the beat map already flags.

---

## Section A — Opening presentation (00:00–00:47, rows 1–2)

**Mode: Faithful reconstruction.**

The piece's quietest material — near-motionless formal stance (row 1, motion
mean 1.18, the lowest in the recording) into the single-arm rose presentation
(row 2). This is the performer introducing themselves and the coat before
anything strange happens; treat it straight. Candidate: a slow push-in or a
faint particle-atmosphere in the negative space around the still figure (per
row 1's own candidate note), and an isolated color-pop on the rose against an
otherwise desaturated frame (row 2's candidate) — restrained, not showy. This
sets the visual baseline everything after it gets to depart from.

## Section B — The blur-burst motif (00:47–01:34, rows 3–7)

**Mode: Silhouette/shadow passage,** bracketed by faithful stillness.

Rows 3 and 5 are the same motion-blur reading — a sharp, brief burst (motion
mean 4.04 and 4.86 against a ~1.3 baseline) that the source footage already
renders as an accidental double image. Per the beat map's own note, this
should become a deliberate ghosting/duplication treatment: a trailing
after-image or shadow-self that peels off the performer and snaps back,
applied identically to both rows so it reads as a recurring motif rather than
two unrelated glitches. Rows 4, 6, and 7 (7 being the single stillest segment
in the whole piece, motion mean 0.76) return to held stillness between bursts
— leave these untreated, so the contrast between "ghost bursts" and "held
breath" stays legible. Row 7 is also flagged in the beat map as the best
splice point in the first two minutes if the render pipeline needs a seam;
worth keeping in mind for t-006/t-007, not something this treatment itself
needs to resolve.

## Section C — Floor work: the coat set aside (01:34–02:22, rows 8–13)

**Mode: Faithful reconstruction** through the coat-off transition.

Row 9 is a real structural turn — the first clear coat-off moment, the
"duet partner" set aside for the first time — and should not be smoothed
into a plain cut; a brief hold or a subtle dip in visual treatment intensity
around row 9's boundary would mark it as a beat, not an edit seam. Rows
10–13 are grounded, physical floor work (crouching, gathering, kneeling,
the rose reappearing) at moderate energy. No AI treatment needed here beyond
matching the wide static framing — this stretch's job is to let the coat's
absence be felt before Section D brings it back as an object.

## Section D — The coat as object (02:22–02:40, rows 14–15)

**Mode: Object-manipulation flourish.**

Short bridge (row 14, coat draped back over one shoulder) into row 15
(partial wear, rising energy, motion mean 3.05) as the performer reclaims
the coat before Section E's flourish. Candidate: subtle independent motion
on the coat fabric itself — a slight animated drift or weight to the drape
that reads as the coat participating in its own re-wearing, not just being
put back on. Kept brief and understated; this section's job is momentum
into the hinge, not a set piece of its own.

## Section E — The flourish (02:40–02:50, row 16)

**Mode: Animated transformation / object-manipulation flourish (hero
moment).**

The piece's first big physical statement — rising to standing and throwing
the coat open into a full flared pose, motion mean 7.05 and peak 28.57, both
well above anything before this point. This is the hinge into the whole
second half and should be treated as one. Candidate: slow-motion emphasis on
the flare itself, with a particle or light-trail treatment following the
coat's edge as it opens — brief, one clean gesture, not a sustained effect
that would blur into Section F. This is also the point where camera
reframing (a push-in, a slight perspective shift toward the flare) would be
most justified if Silas wants one deliberate departure from the otherwise
constant wide shot — flagged here as optional, not assumed.

## Section F — The climax (02:50–03:21, rows 17–21)

**Mode: Impossible perspective shift, protecting rather than replacing the
real peak.**

Row 17 (02:50–02:58) is the measured motion-energy and near-audio peak of
the entire recording (motion mean 13.07, peak 50.97 — roughly 4× the piece's
own median) as the first projected photograph lights up and the performer
dances full-body against it. **This section must not be trimmed or slowed
for pacing** — the beat map is explicit that this is the real climax, not
just a visually busy frame, and losing it would be a structural, not
cosmetic, cut. Candidate treatment is motion-reactive visual intensity keyed
to the already-high real motion energy (brightness, projection-bleed, or a
perspective warp that intensifies with the performer's actual movement)
rather than any treatment that competes with or replaces the source energy.
Rows 18–20 sustain the wave at slightly lower energy against changing
photographs. Row 21 is the outlier worth calling out specifically: motion
drops sharply (1.68) while audio hits the single loudest point in the
recording (RMS 0.2047) — a deliberate "loud silence" the beat map flags as
real signal. Treatment: hold this beat visually still (no motion-reactive
effect firing) while the loudest audio plays underneath it, so the contrast
reads as intentional rather than getting smoothed into the surrounding
motion by an automated pass.

## Section G — Travel-photo sequence, first wave (03:21–04:03, rows 22–27)

**Mode: Animated transformation**, echoing whatever screen photograph is
live.

The performer alternates coat on/off through a sequence of changing
projected images (blue portrait, garden/patio, street/tree, a two-figure bed
scene) at sustained moderate-to-high energy, including a sharp burst in row
26 (peak 31.06) paired with an intimate reclining-figures image. Candidate:
let each cut's visual treatment take a light color/texture cue from the
photograph currently on screen, so the performer visually belongs to
whichever memory is projected behind them rather than reading as a constant
figure with a slideshow playing incidentally in the background.

## Section H — Travel-photo sequence, second wave and the image/body rhyme (04:03–04:40, rows 28–33)

**Mode: Impossible perspective shift, with one protected silhouette
moment.**

Continues the photo sequence (pyramid, moai statues, Mount Rushmore) at high
energy (row 28 peak 26.13, row 30 peak 21.24). Row 31 is the single most
specific beat in this whole stretch: the performer crouches low and bowed in
a pose that visibly echoes the moai statues' own posture in the photograph
behind them — a legible, deliberate image/body rhyme, not a coincidence.
**This is the one section where the silhouette/shadow mode should be used
literally rather than atmospherically**: a matched-outline treatment that
makes the performer/statue silhouette rhyme unmistakable, holding the
framing that makes it legible rather than reframing away from it. Row 32's
declarative pointing gesture toward Rushmore is a good candidate for the
kind of perspective play this section's mode name implies — e.g. the
performer's gesture appearing to physically reach the scale of the monument
behind them.

## Section I — Reaching toward the photograph (04:40–05:07, rows 34–36)

**Mode: Silhouette/shadow passage — the "reaching into the image" motif.**

Rows 34–36 form the beat map's own recurring gesture: the performer crouches
close to the screen and reaches toward a black-and-white personal portrait,
sustained and intensifying (motion rising from 4.71 to 6.08, the last
sustained energy peak before the wind-down). The beat map calls this the
emotional throughline of the whole photo sequence and recommends one
consistent treatment vocabulary across it and row 31 — this treatment
agrees, and proposes the strongest version of it: a literal crossing, where
the performer's silhouette or hand appears to breach the projection's image
plane, treated identically each time it recurs (rows 26, 31, 34–36) so it
reads as a motif built up over the piece rather than four separate ideas.

## Section J — Blackout (05:07–05:19, row 37)

**Mode: None — deliberate absence.**

Stage and projection cut to dark. The beat map is explicit that the one
motion spike here (24.87) is the lighting change registering as a frame
difference, not real performer motion, and that the emptiness itself is the
point. No treatment: this section should stay genuinely black and silent
on the visual track, a hard structural rest before the close, not a
opportunity for an effect.

## Section K — Closing tableau (05:19–05:32, row 38)

**Mode: Return to the human performer and coat.**

Lights return; the performer stands center stage in a final held pose, coat
now carried over one arm rather than worn — the duet partner set down, not
discarded. Candidate: a visual rhyme with Section A's opening stillness
(same restrained push-in or atmosphere treatment, not a new idea), so the
piece visibly closes the frame it opened rather than just stopping. No
coat-focused treatment beyond that echo — this is the moment the piece
explicitly returns to the plain human-and-coat image the whole piece
departed from, and it should read as a return, not a new flourish.

## Cross-section notes

- **Camera departures are opt-in, not default.** The entire source is one
  fixed wide shot (per `source/beatmap.md`). Sections E and H are flagged
  above as the two places a deliberate reframe would earn its keep most; every
  other section assumes the wide static frame stays the visual anchor even
  where the treatment itself gets stranger.
- **Recurring motifs, not one-off effects.** Three motifs recur across
  non-adjacent sections and should share one consistent treatment each time
  they appear, per the beat map's own cross-references: the ghost/duplication
  burst (Section B, rows 3 & 5), the image/body rhyme and reach-toward-the-
  photograph gesture (Sections H–I, rows 26/31/34–36), and the opening/closing
  stillness bookend (Sections A and K).
- **The audio does not move.** Per BRIEF.md Q1/Q2 (answered), the original
  performance audio is preserved as-is throughout; nothing in this treatment
  proposes a score change, a blend, or a replacement track. Section F's "loud
  silence" (row 21) and Section J's blackout are both cases where the visual
  treatment is asked to follow or defer to the existing audio rather than the
  reverse.
- **Generation Method stays unassigned here.** Per t-003's own note, which
  tool renders which candidate treatment depends on t-006's render-environment
  inventory (BRIEF.md Q3, still open) — this document proposes *what* each
  section should become, not *how* it gets made.
