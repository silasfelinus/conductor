# Surface, Symbol, and Ornament — four-movement comparison set

This inspiration set holds one scene constant while changing only its visual grammar. It is designed for Academy learners to compare how image-making systems organize bodies, space, light, and decoration rather than treating a style as a bag of decorative keywords.

## Fixed scene

A messenger stands at the threshold of a civic hall at sunrise, holding a sealed letter. A small dog waits beside them. The hall has two columns, one open doorway, a shallow flight of steps, and a circular emblem above the entrance. The camera is frontal, eye-level, and wide enough to show the complete figure, dog, doorway, columns, steps, and emblem.

Keep the following fixed in every render:

- one standing messenger and one small dog
- sealed letter visible in the messenger's hand
- two columns, one doorway, shallow steps, circular emblem
- sunrise lighting cue
- frontal, eye-level composition
- no extra people, vehicles, weapons, text, logos, or modern signage

## 1. Ancient Egyptian painting

### Recognition cues

- composite human pose with profile head and legs, frontal torso, and clearly described limbs
- hierarchical clarity, firm contour lines, and flat color fields
- shallow stacked space rather than optical depth
- architecture reduced to readable symbolic shapes
- orderly registers or implied horizontal bands

### Kontext prompt

> Restyle the fixed civic-hall messenger scene as an ancient Egyptian painted wall scene. Preserve the exact subject count, object inventory, frontal camera, and placement. Use crisp dark contours, flat mineral-like color fields, composite human pose, shallow stacked space, orderly horizontal organization, and symbolic architectural forms. Keep the sealed letter, dog, two columns, doorway, steps, circular emblem, and sunrise cue immediately readable. No photorealism, atmospheric perspective, modern costume, pseudo-hieroglyphic text, extra figures, or archaeological damage effects.

### Failure signs

- merely adding pyramids, scarabs, or fake hieroglyphs to a modern illustration
- photorealistic anatomy and deep perspective
- losing the letter, dog, or architectural inventory
- turning the scene into a ruined-tomb photograph

## 2. Byzantine mosaic

### Recognition cues

- frontal, solemn figure presentation
- gold or luminous field assembled from visible tesserae
- compressed space and deliberate scale rather than naturalistic depth
- jewel-like color, rhythmic folds, and strong silhouette
- emblem treated as a radiant focal device

### Kontext prompt

> Restyle the fixed civic-hall messenger scene as a Byzantine wall mosaic. Preserve the exact subject count, object inventory, frontal camera, and placement. Build the image from visible tesserae with a luminous gold ground, frontal solemn figure, compressed space, jewel-like color, rhythmic garment folds, and a radiant circular emblem. Keep the sealed letter, small dog, two columns, doorway, steps, and sunrise cue legible. No photographic lighting, Renaissance perspective, painted-brush texture, modern lettering, extra halos, extra figures, or crumbling-wall theatrics.

### Failure signs

- smooth digital painting with a mosaic filter pasted over it
- arbitrary halos that change the scene's meaning
- naturalistic deep space or cinematic lighting
- unreadable dog or letter

## 3. Gothic stained glass

### Recognition cues

- composition divided by dark lead lines into colored glass shapes
- tall, simplified figures and architecture
- transmitted light, saturated color, and jewel-window luminosity
- decorative borders that frame rather than replace the scene
- emblem integrated as a rose-window-like circular focus

### Kontext prompt

> Restyle the fixed civic-hall messenger scene as a Gothic stained-glass window. Preserve the exact subject count, object inventory, frontal camera, and placement. Divide the image into saturated translucent glass shapes separated by strong dark lead lines, with elongated simplified forms, luminous transmitted light, and a restrained ornamental border. Integrate the circular emblem as a rose-window-like focal shape while keeping the sealed letter, dog, two columns, doorway, steps, and sunrise cue clear. No realistic reflections, church interior photograph, readable text, extra saints, excessive filigree, or missing scene elements.

### Failure signs

- a normal illustration placed inside a window frame
- lead lines that do not describe form
- ornament swallowing the messenger and dog
- converting the civic hall into a cathedral interior

## 4. Art Deco poster

### Recognition cues

- geometric simplification, stepped forms, and controlled symmetry
- streamlined figure and architecture
- bold value grouping with limited, high-contrast color
- sunburst or radiating geometry used with restraint
- crisp poster hierarchy without textual dependence

### Kontext prompt

> Restyle the fixed civic-hall messenger scene as a late-1920s Art Deco poster illustration. Preserve the exact subject count, object inventory, frontal camera, and placement. Use geometric simplification, stepped architectural forms, streamlined silhouettes, crisp edges, controlled symmetry, a limited high-contrast palette, and a restrained sunrise fan behind the circular emblem. Keep the sealed letter, dog, two columns, doorway, and steps plainly readable. No typography, logos, chrome sci-fi surfaces, 1950s advertising style, photorealism, extra figures, or scene replacement.

### Failure signs

- generic retro travel poster with invented words
- chrome futurism overwhelming the historical style
- asymmetrical cinematic action pose
- missing dog, letter, or civic-hall structure

## Blind comparison exercise

Show the four outputs without labels and ask learners to identify each movement using evidence from structure, not subject matter.

1. Which image organizes the body through composite viewpoints?
2. Which image compresses space into a luminous field of tesserae?
3. Which image uses lead lines as the drawing system itself?
4. Which image turns architecture and sunrise into stepped geometric hierarchy?
5. In which output did the model merely apply a texture instead of changing visual grammar?

A defensible answer names at least two visible cues and one rejected cue for every identification.

## Reproducibility record

For every generated output, record:

- source image or source-image hash
- engine and model identifier
- complete positive and negative prompt
- seed
- width and height
- workflow or ArtJob identifier
- generation date
- any manual crop, correction, or compositing

## Promotion gate

Do not promote an output into the Academy gallery unless:

- all fixed scene elements remain present and recognizable
- the style is identifiable without relying on labels or added symbols
- the output changes spatial and formal grammar, not only surface texture
- no invented writing, logo, or culturally misleading pseudo-script appears
- source and generation metadata are complete
- a second reviewer can distinguish all four outputs in the blind exercise

This set intentionally queues no ArtJobs while the shared render relay backlog remains unhealthy. It is ready for generation through the existing Academy pipeline once normal FIFO throughput resumes.
