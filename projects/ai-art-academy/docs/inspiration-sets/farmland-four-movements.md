# Farmland at Day's End, Four Movements

A controlled comparison lesson for studying how four movements treat the same rural
landscape and its workers — from sublime spectacle down to unidealized, dignified
labor. Unlike this collection's other landscape set (`harbor-four-movements.md`,
an industrial/maritime scene), this set is rural farmland, and every movement here
(Romanticism, Hudson River School, Barbizon School, Realism) is new to the
collection — none has appeared in a prior inspiration set.

## Fixed composition

Use one source image throughout:

- a wide farmland valley viewed from a slightly raised vantage a short distance away
- a hayfield sloping down toward a line of trees along a stream
- a modest farmhouse and barn in the middle distance
- two farm workers finishing labor near a hay wagon
- grazing cattle at the field's edge
- late-afternoon light from camera left
- a broad sky filling roughly the upper half of the frame

Keep the camera position, principal silhouettes, worker count and poses, farmhouse
and barn placement, cattle, horizon, and major light direction unchanged. The
exercise is about visual language, not quietly replacing the assignment with a
different scene.

## Rights boundary

Teach movements and historically documented techniques, not imitation of a named
artist. Use only public-domain reference works from artists who satisfy
`PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md`. Do not place artist names in generation prompts, negative
prompts, presets, filenames, or promotional copy.

Learners may upload an image they own or are authorized to transform. Before
promotion, confirm that every displayed example image is public domain or
separately licensed for reproduction and that its source and rights statement are
recorded.

## 1. Romanticism

### Recognition cues

- vast, dramatic sky that dwarfs the human figures
- glowing, atmospheric light — a turning sunset, gathering storm, or breaking cloud
- figures rendered small and contemplative against the immensity of the scene
- emotional, turbulent brushwork and a moody, heightened palette
- the land presented as an object of awe first, observation second

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact farmland composition and identities of all visible people.
> Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Romantic ideas: a vast, dramatic
> sky with glowing or turbulent atmospheric light, heightened emotional color, and
> the workers rendered small and contemplative against the land's immensity. Keep
> the farmhouse, barn, cattle, workers, horizon, and camera position unchanged.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no shipwrecks, ruins, or fantasy elements replacing the farmland
- no extra or removed figures
- no photographic sharpness
- no loss of the farmhouse, barn, or cattle

### Common failure

The model often defaults to a generic "moody sky filter" without changing scale or
emotional weight. A successful result should make the sky and light feel genuinely
sublime — dwarfing, not merely darkening, the scene.

## 2. Hudson River School

### Recognition cues

- panoramic composition with a high horizon and pronounced depth
- luminous, often golden light breaking through cloud or haze
- meticulously rendered trees, grasses, water, and distant terrain
- workers and cattle kept small, used mainly to establish scale
- a staged progression from a shadowed foreground to a glowing distance
- the land presented as sublime, abundant, and morally instructive

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact farmland composition and identities of all visible people.
> Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Hudson River School ideas:
> panoramic depth, meticulous natural detail in the trees and stream, a shadowed
> foreground opening toward luminous golden distance, and workers and cattle kept
> small to establish scale. Keep the farmhouse, barn, cattle, workers, horizon, and
> camera position unchanged.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no mountains, wilderness, or terrain not present in the source scene
- no extra or removed figures
- no loss of the farmhouse or barn
- no flattened, undetailed foliage

### Common failure

The model may substitute a generic "golden hour" wash instead of the school's
specific staged depth and fine natural detail. Foreground-to-distance contrast and
meticulous rendering of trees and grasses must be genuinely present, not implied by
color alone.

## 3. Barbizon School

### Recognition cues

- a muted, earthy palette of greens, browns, and greys
- loose but controlled brushwork — looser than academic finish, tighter and more
  tonally unified than Impressionist broken color
- soft, overcast or late-day atmospheric light rather than dramatic illumination
- a specific, unidealized rural landscape rather than a composite or sublime one
- humble, unposed rural labor treated with quiet dignity, not sentimentality

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact farmland composition and identities of all visible people.
> Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Barbizon School ideas: a muted,
> earthy palette, loose but controlled brushwork, soft overcast late-day light, and
> the workers and cattle treated as ordinary, unidealized rural life with quiet
> dignity. Keep the farmhouse, barn, cattle, workers, horizon, and camera position
> unchanged.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no dramatic or theatrical sky
- no idealized or heroic posing of the workers
- no extra or removed figures
- no bright, high-key color

### Common failure

The model may simply desaturate the image rather than reorganizing brushwork and
light toward Barbizon's specific, tonally unified naturalism. The scene should read
as a particular observed place, not a generic muted landscape.

## 4. Realism

### Recognition cues

- everyday, working-class subjects treated at grand, heroic scale
- an earthy palette and truthful, unflattering light
- solid, weighty figures with none of the porcelain finish of academic art
- no mythology, no idealization, no visible fantasy
- a composition that feels observed rather than staged

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact farmland composition and identities of all visible people.
> Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Realist ideas: an earthy, truthful
> palette, unflattering natural light, and the two farm workers rendered as solid,
> weighty, heroic-scale figures worthy of the canvas on their own terms — no
> idealization, no staging. Keep the farmhouse, barn, cattle, workers, horizon, and
> camera position unchanged.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no idealized or porcelain-finish figures
- no dramatic or glowing light
- no extra or removed figures
- no loss of the farmhouse or barn

### Common failure

The model may prettify the workers instead of granting them plain, weighty
dignity. The result should look like an honest record of labor, not a softened or
picturesque version of it.

## Blind comparison exercise

Hide the movement labels and ask learners to rank each result on:

1. composition preservation
2. identity preservation
3. movement recognition
4. meaningful change in the treatment of light and sky
5. meaningful change in how the workers are scaled and regarded
6. absence of generic "art filter" behavior

Then ask learners to identify the movement and cite three visible cues before
revealing the labels.

## Reflection prompts

- Which result makes the land feel largest relative to the workers, and which
  makes the workers feel largest relative to the land?
- Where does light describe a specific observed moment versus create spectacle or
  mood?
- Which movement grants the two workers the most dignity and agency, and which
  reduces them furthest to scale-markers or atmosphere?
- Hudson River School and Barbizon School both claim "the land itself" as
  subject — what specific visual choices make one read as sublime spectacle and
  the other as humble, particular observation?
- Which result is recognizable without relying on a famous artist's signature
  mannerisms?
- Which prompt produced the most preservation failures, and what wording should be
  tightened?

## Reproducibility record

For every generated comparison, record:

- source image identifier and rights status
- generation engine and model version
- workflow or endpoint version
- complete positive and negative prompts
- seed
- dimensions
- guidance, steps, sampler, and scheduler where applicable
- LoRA path, trigger, and weight where applicable
- generation date
- ArtJob and ArtImage identifiers where available
- manual notes on composition and identity preservation

## Promotion gate

Promote a set only when:

- the four results preserve the fixed composition closely enough for a fair
  comparison
- all visible people retain consistent identity, count, role, and presentation
- movement recognition does not depend on protected artist names
- source and output rights are documented
- representation review finds no stereotyping, dehumanization, or erasure of the
  farm workers
- prompts and metadata are sufficient to reproduce or audit the exercise
- weak results and negative findings remain documented rather than quietly
  replaced
