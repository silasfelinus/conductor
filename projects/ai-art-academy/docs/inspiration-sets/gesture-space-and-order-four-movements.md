# Gesture, Space, and Order — Four-Movement Inspiration Set

## Purpose

This fixed-scene comparison set helps Academy learners distinguish a movement's visual grammar from costume, texture, or period props. Every remix uses the same subject, camera, object inventory, and narrative beat. Only the image-making system changes.

The set is designed for future preview generation, blind-recognition exercises, and side-by-side evaluation. It does not require rendering to be useful as curriculum material.

## Fixed scene

A solitary astronomer stands in a small observatory at dusk, turning from a brass telescope toward an open notebook. A circular window shows one bright star above a low horizon. The room contains exactly five supporting objects: a stool, a globe, a candle, a folded cloth, and a shallow bowl of water.

Keep these constants across all four images:

- landscape 4:3 frame;
- eye-level camera, three-quarter view;
- astronomer placed slightly left of center;
- telescope points from lower left toward upper right;
- circular window occupies the upper-right quadrant;
- no additional people, animals, architecture, or celestial bodies;
- the instant shown is the same: the astronomer has just noticed the star and is about to write.

The comparison succeeds only when a viewer can identify the movement from composition, space, line, value, and color organization rather than from pasted-on motifs.

---

## 1. High Renaissance

### Recognition cues

- stable triangular organization connecting figure, telescope, and window;
- calm, legible depth built through measured overlap and atmospheric recession;
- anatomy and drapery feel observed rather than diagrammed;
- light unifies the room instead of fragmenting it into competing effects;
- the astronomer's gesture is restrained and psychologically clear.

### Production prompt

> Recompose the fixed observatory scene through High Renaissance visual principles. Build a calm triangular arrangement linking the astronomer, telescope, and circular window. Use balanced masses, measured linear perspective, natural human proportions, softly modeled volume, restrained gesture, and a unified dusk light that carries the eye from foreground objects into believable depth. Keep every required object and the exact camera placement. The image should feel lucid, poised, and spatially coherent rather than theatrical or ornamental.

### Negative guidance

Avoid exaggerated twisting poses, extreme spotlighting, decorative gold pattern, flattened icon space, photographic lens effects, fantasy machinery, and dense symbolic clutter.

### Failure signs

- the scene is merely "old-master brown" without balanced geometry;
- the figure is dramatically contorted or emotionally overstated;
- perspective is technically deep but compositionally restless;
- period clothing is doing all the stylistic work;
- the background reads as a modern cinematic set.

---

## 2. Rococo

### Recognition cues

- asymmetrical flow replaces the Renaissance triangle without losing readability;
- pale, luminous color and soft edges make the room feel airy;
- curves echo between cloth, figure, telescope, and window;
- small objects participate in a graceful rhythm rather than strict hierarchy;
- the moment feels intimate, elegant, and lightly theatrical.

### Production prompt

> Recompose the fixed observatory scene through Rococo visual principles. Preserve the exact figure, camera, window, telescope, and five supporting objects, but organize them in a graceful asymmetrical sweep. Use pale luminous color, pearly highlights, soft transitions, buoyant curves, delicate surface variation, and an intimate sense of suspended movement. Let the folded cloth, telescope, gesture, and circular window echo one another rhythmically. Keep the image elegant and airy without adding aristocratic spectators, garden scenery, cherubs, or decorative objects beyond the fixed inventory.

### Negative guidance

Avoid heavy Baroque darkness, strict bilateral balance, flat poster geometry, sugary pink monochrome, costume-pageant excess, porcelain-doll anatomy, and extra decorative clutter.

### Failure signs

- the result is only pastel-colored Renaissance composition;
- ornament overwhelms the astronomer's action;
- asymmetry becomes random object scattering;
- the figure turns into a fashion illustration;
- the scene loses the notebook-to-star narrative beat.

---

## 3. Symbolism

### Recognition cues

- ordinary objects feel charged by association without becoming a rebus;
- spatial logic may bend to support mood, but the scene remains readable;
- color relationships carry psychological weight;
- the star, bowl of water, notebook, and circular window form a quiet conceptual network;
- the astronomer's inward attention matters more than physical action.

### Production prompt

> Recompose the fixed observatory scene through Symbolist visual principles. Keep the complete object inventory and camera structure, but let space, scale, and color bend subtly toward an inward psychological reading. The single star, circular window, bowl of water, candle, globe, and open notebook should resonate through visual relationships rather than explicit labels or added symbols. Use simplified but deliberate forms, dreamlike color intervals, quiet tension, and a contemplative figure whose attention appears divided between observation and revelation. Preserve ambiguity: suggest meaning without explaining it.

### Negative guidance

Avoid occult diagrams, written prophecies, literal halos, fantasy creatures, surreal object multiplication, horror imagery, zodiac clutter, and obvious one-to-one symbolism.

### Failure signs

- added mystical props explain the image instead of deepening it;
- the scene becomes generic dark fantasy;
- objects are randomly distorted with no visual relationship;
- symbolism depends on text written in the notebook;
- atmosphere replaces composition entirely.

---

## 4. De Stijl

### Recognition cues

- the scene is rebuilt as relationships among verticals, horizontals, rectangles, and controlled diagonals;
- depth is compressed into layered planes rather than modeled illusion;
- a limited palette and neutral fields create hierarchy;
- the telescope's diagonal is exceptional and therefore structurally important;
- recognizability survives radical simplification.

### Production prompt

> Recompose the fixed observatory scene through De Stijl principles. Preserve the astronomer, telescope, circular window, notebook, stool, globe, candle, folded cloth, and bowl of water, but translate them into a rigorous system of vertical and horizontal divisions, rectangular planes, black structural lines, neutral fields, and a restrained primary-color palette. Compress depth and eliminate naturalistic modeling. Use the telescope's lower-left to upper-right diagonal sparingly as the principal tension against the orthogonal grid. Keep the narrative legible through proportion and placement, not realism or decorative iconography.

### Negative guidance

Avoid generic Bauhaus poster typography, gradients, painterly texture, 3D extrusion, rounded app-interface shapes, excessive diagonals, random primary-color blocks, and literal blueprints.

### Failure signs

- the result is only a primary-colored filter over realistic forms;
- the grid is decorative rather than governing the composition;
- the telescope diagonal no longer creates meaningful tension;
- the astronomer disappears into an unreadable abstraction;
- text or logos are added to make it look like a poster.

## Blind-recognition exercise

Show the four outputs without movement names and ask learners to identify each one from evidence rather than vibe. A useful answer names at least two structural cues.

1. Which image organizes the scene around calm, measured depth and a stable figure-object triangle?
2. Which image turns the same inventory into an airy asymmetrical rhythm of curves and pale light?
3. Which image makes the star, water, notebook, and window feel meaningfully related without stating what they mean?
4. Which image compresses the room into an orthogonal system where one diagonal carries most of the tension?

Then ask the learner to identify one misleading surface cue in each image that should not, by itself, determine the answer.

## Reproducibility record for future renders

Every generated comparison should preserve:

- source image or source-scene identifier;
- engine and model version;
- full positive and negative prompts;
- seed;
- dimensions and aspect ratio;
- sampler, steps, guidance, and denoise/remix strength where applicable;
- LoRA name, trigger, weight, and license when used;
- ArtJob and ArtImage IDs;
- generation date;
- any manual crop, color correction, or compositing performed afterward.

## Promotion gate

Do not promote a render into the Academy inspiration gallery merely because it is attractive. Each candidate must pass all of these checks:

1. **Scene fidelity:** all required objects and the fixed narrative beat remain legible.
2. **Structural recognition:** reviewers can identify the intended movement from visual organization, not just palette or costume.
3. **No shortcut styling:** the result does not depend on pasted ornament, text labels, or a generic texture overlay.
4. **Comparability:** camera, object inventory, and subject placement remain close enough for side-by-side teaching.
5. **Metadata completeness:** the reproducibility record is complete.
6. **Policy fit:** any source art, model, and LoRA satisfy the Academy's public-domain and licensing rules for the intended use.

A beautiful failure stays useful as a failure example, but it should be labeled with the specific criterion it missed rather than quietly entering the polished gallery.
