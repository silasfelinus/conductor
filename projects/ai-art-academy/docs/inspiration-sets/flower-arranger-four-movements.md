# Flower Arranger, Four Movements

A controlled comparison lesson for studying how movements reorganize the same visual
facts across four different eras and cultures — not just four adjacent early-twentieth-
century Western styles. Every prior set in this collection (garden-gate, harbor,
night-window, one-room, everyday-modernity) draws its four movements from the same
c. 1870-1930 Western cluster (Impressionism, The Nabis, Precisionism, Tonalism,
Fauvism, Ashcan School, Social Realism, Harlem Renaissance, Metaphysical painting,
American Regionalism). This set deliberately spans four movements that never overlap
with any of those: Byzantine Mosaic (c. 500-1200 CE), Ukiyo-e (Edo-period Japan,
c. 1650-1900), Art Nouveau (c. 1890-1914), and Vienna Secession (c. 1897-1918) — so
learners compare flatness and ornament across a much wider stretch of time and
geography, rather than only within one continuous Western modernist lineage.

## Fixed composition

Use one source image throughout:

- a person kneels at a low table beneath an open archway, arranging cut flowers into
  a ceramic vase
- a pair of shears and a folded cloth rest on the table beside the vase
- three or four stems of the flowers are already placed; one is still held in the
  person's hand mid-arrangement
- the camera is frontal and slightly elevated, framed by the archway itself
- a narrow strip of garden greenery is visible through the archway behind the figure

Keep the camera position, the archway framing, the figure's identity and kneeling
pose, the vase/shears/cloth arrangement, and the held stem unchanged across all four
variants. The exercise is about visual language, not quietly replacing the assignment
with a different scene.

## Rights boundary

Teach movements and historically documented techniques, not imitation of a named
artist. Use only public-domain reference works from artists who satisfy the Academy
policy (`PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md`). Do not place artist names in generation prompts,
negative prompts, presets, filenames, or promotional copy — even where a movement's
curriculum entry names a single dominant historical figure (e.g. Vienna Secession's
sole verified example source), matching the convention already established by every
other set in this collection.

Learners may upload an image they own or are authorized to transform. Before
promotion, confirm that every displayed example image is public domain or separately
licensed for reproduction and that its source and rights statement are recorded.

## 1. Byzantine Mosaic

### Recognition cues

- images visibly built from small square tiles with grout lines between them
- radiant gold backgrounds instead of sky, garden, or architecture
- flat, frontal, elongated figures with large solemn eyes and no cast shadow
- rich jewel colors — deep blue, emerald, ruby — set against the gold field
- figures and objects float rather than occupy real perspectival space

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact archway composition and the identity of the kneeling figure. Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Byzantine mosaic ideas: the entire image built from small tessellated tiles with visible grout lines, a radiant flat gold background replacing the garden view, frontal and slightly elongated figure proportions, large solemn eyes, jewel-toned color for the vase and flowers, and no cast shadow or realistic perspective. Keep the archway, table, vase, shears, cloth, and held stem legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no religious iconography or halos not present in the source scene
- no perspective depth or receding garden
- no photographic skin texture
- no loss of the vase, shears, or held stem
- no illegible tile pattern that obscures the figure

### Common failure

The model often renders "mosaic" as a texture filter over an otherwise photographic
image. A successful result should flatten the entire picture plane — background,
figure, and objects alike — into tessellated gold and jewel-tone fields, not just add
a tiled border or overlay.

## 2. Ukiyo-e

### Recognition cues

- flat planes of color with little or no shading, bounded by confident outlines
- bold, cropped composition — the archway or table edge slicing the frame
- stylized natural forms for the garden greenery: patterned leaves, simplified stems
- gradated color skies or backgrounds (bokashi) fading between two tones
- no Western-style cast shadow; pattern and outline carry the form

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact archway composition and the identity of the kneeling figure. Reinterpret the scene through movement-level ukiyo-e woodblock-print ideas: flat bounded color planes with confident black outlines, a bold cropped framing through the archway, stylized patterned foliage for the garden view, a gradated two-tone background sky, and woodgrain-texture flatness in place of photographic shading. Keep the figure's pose, the vase, shears, cloth, and held stem legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no manga or anime character styling
- no calligraphy or seal cartouches added as decoration unrelated to the scene
- no photographic gradients or soft shading
- no loss of the archway framing
- no extra figures

### Common failure

The model may default to generic "anime" styling instead of period woodblock-print
technique. A successful result should show confident flat color bounded by clean
outlines and a genuine cropped, poster-like composition — not simplified facial
features borrowed from modern animation.

## 3. Art Nouveau

### Recognition cues

- long flowing S-curves in the figure's silhouette, the stems, and the archway itself
- the archway reimagined as an ornamental frame — vines, tendrils, or floral motifs
- flat, poster-like color with elegant contour lines
- nature stylized into ornament rather than rendered naturalistically
- a decorative border integrated into the composition

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact archway composition and the identity of the kneeling figure. Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Art Nouveau ideas: long sinuous whiplash curves in the figure's hair, clothing, and the flower stems, the archway redrawn as an ornamental frame of stylized vines and tendrils, flat poster-like color with elegant contour lines, and the garden greenery simplified into decorative pattern. Keep the figure's pose, the vase, shears, cloth, and held stem legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no literal advertising text or lettering
- no loss of the archway as the framing device
- no glossy digital gradients
- no changed vase shape or missing shears
- no cluttered ornament that hides the kneeling pose

### Common failure

The model may add generic floral filigree without reorganizing line quality. A
successful result should transform the archway and figure's contours into genuine
sinuous, unified curves — not just scatter decorative flowers around an otherwise
unchanged photographic image.

## 4. Vienna Secession

### Recognition cues

- large flat fields of gold-toned pattern (spirals, concentric eyes, rectangles)
  replacing the garden background, applied as ornament rather than illumination
- a jarring contrast between finely modeled, naturalistic hands and face and the
  totally flat, non-representational ornament surrounding them
- elongated, stylized figure proportions with little anatomical volume below the
  head and hands
- rich jewel-toned color "islands" — the vase and flowers — set within the gold field
- no cast shadow, no realistic perspective; a strong decorative border

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact archway composition and the identity of the kneeling figure. Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Vienna Secession ideas: the garden view behind the archway replaced by a flat field of gold-toned ornamental pattern (spirals, concentric eyes, rectangles), the figure's face and hands rendered with fine naturalistic detail against otherwise flattened, non-representational ornament, elongated figure proportions, jewel-toned color for the vase and flowers as islands within the gold field, and a strong decorative border. Keep the kneeling pose, the vase, shears, cloth, and held stem legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no cast shadow or realistic garden perspective
- no loss of naturalistic detail in the face and hands specifically
- no generic "gold filter" applied uniformly without the flat/naturalistic contrast
- no missing shears, cloth, or held stem
- no religious or mythological additions not present in the source scene

### Common failure

The model may apply a uniform gold tint to the whole image instead of the movement's
defining contrast: photographically modeled hands and face set against completely
flat ornamental pattern everywhere else. A successful result needs both halves of
that contrast, not just a warm color grade.

## Blind comparison exercise

Hide the movement labels and ask learners to rank each result on:

1. composition preservation
2. identity preservation
3. movement recognition
4. meaningful change in flatness/depth treatment
5. meaningful change in ornament and pattern
6. absence of generic "art filter" behavior

Then ask learners to identify the movement, era, and culture of origin, and cite three
visible cues, before revealing the labels.

## Reflection prompts

- Which result eliminates depth most completely, and which retains the most sense of
  real space?
- Byzantine Mosaic and Vienna Secession both use flat gold fields — what specifically
  distinguishes them (figure treatment, contrast with naturalistic detail, era)?
- Art Nouveau and Vienna Secession share ornamental line — where does Vienna
  Secession's added flatness/naturalism contrast show up that Art Nouveau doesn't have?
- Which movement most changes how "garden" reads, and which most changes how the
  "figure" reads?
- Which result is recognizable without relying on a named artist's signature
  mannerisms?
- What does comparing a non-Western movement (Ukiyo-e) against three Western ones
  teach that an all-Western set cannot?

## Reproducibility record

For every generated comparison, record:

- source image identifier and rights status
- generation engine and model version
- workflow or endpoint version
- complete positive and negative prompts
- seed
- dimensions
- guidance, steps, sampler, and scheduler where applicable
- LoRA path, trigger, and weight where applicable
- generation date
- ArtJob and ArtImage identifiers where available
- manual notes on composition and identity preservation

## Promotion gate

Promote a set only when:

- the four results preserve the fixed composition closely enough for a fair comparison
- the figure retains consistent identity, pose, and presentation across all four
- movement recognition does not depend on protected artist names
- source and output rights are documented
- representation review finds no stereotyping, dehumanization, or erasure of the
  figure, particularly for the Ukiyo-e variant given its non-Western source culture
- prompts and metadata are sufficient to reproduce or audit the exercise
- weak results and negative findings remain documented rather than quietly replaced
