# Flat Space, Four Traditions — 4-Image Teaching Sequence

This comparison asks a deceptively simple question: if realistic depth is not the goal, what replaces it? The same small garden scene is reorganized through Ancient Egyptian Painting, Persian Miniature Painting, Rajput Painting, and Rinpa School so learners can compare four distinct systems of flat or shallow pictorial space rather than treating “flatness” as one generic style effect.

## Shared source scene

Lock one scene and one camera position across all four entries: a small friendly robot kneels in a walled garden beside a shallow rectangular pool, reaching toward a flowering iris. A low stone bench sits behind the robot; one small bird stands at the far edge of the pool. Keep the robot, iris, pool, bench, bird, garden wall, and left-facing three-quarter composition in the same relative positions in every version. No text, inscriptions, logos, signatures, famous landmarks, or culture-specific sacred figures should be added.

> A small friendly robot kneeling in a walled garden beside a shallow rectangular pool, reaching toward one flowering iris; a low stone bench behind the robot, one small bird at the far pool edge, garden wall beyond; three-quarter view from slightly above, all objects fully visible, quiet daylight, no text, no logos, no landmark, fixed composition for a four-image educational comparison.

## 1. Ancient Egyptian Painting — ordered symbolic clarity

**Teaching goal:** Show that rejecting illusionistic depth can create a highly legible system of hierarchy, profile/frontal conventions, contour, and register-like order rather than simply making an image “primitive” or less observed.

**Prompt:**

> Repaint the fixed garden scene using the visual grammar of ancient Egyptian tomb and temple painting: flat mineral-like color fields bounded by firm dark contour, little or no cast shadow, shallow register-like organization, the robot’s head and legs oriented clearly in profile while its torso remains broad and frontal, pool and garden elements tipped upward so their important shapes stay readable, repeated plant forms arranged rhythmically, restrained earth red, ochre, blue-green, black, and cream. Preserve every source object and its narrative relationship. Do not add hieroglyphs, inscriptions, pharaoh regalia, pyramids, or copied motifs from a named work.

**Look for:**
- contour and pose make each part readable instead of modeling volume with light;
- important objects are shown from the angle that explains them most clearly;
- the pool can tip toward the viewer without becoming ordinary perspective;
- repeated plant and wall shapes create order without photorealistic depth.

**Ethics/agency question:** When a visual system standardizes bodies and gestures for clarity, what remains individual about the subject, and what becomes a role or symbol?

**Common failure:** Generic “Egypt” decoration: pyramids, random pseudo-hieroglyphs, gold jewelry, or sandstone texture pasted onto an otherwise cinematic 3-D scene.

## 2. Persian Miniature Painting — many details sharing one plane

**Teaching goal:** Demonstrate how a shallow, elevated viewpoint can hold architecture, figures, garden detail, and narrative information together without giving one vanishing point control of the whole picture.

**Prompt:**

> Repaint the identical garden scene as a Persian miniature painting: elevated shallow viewpoint, jewel-like ultramarine, vermilion, turquoise, rose, and gold accents, crisp fine contour, intricately patterned garden wall and textiles, delicate flowering plants, the pool opened upward for visibility, architecture stacked and tilted so interior and garden information can coexist, precise small-scale detail distributed across the surface, luminous flat color with minimal cast shadow. Keep the robot, iris, pool, bench, bird, and wall in the locked composition. No calligraphy, manuscript text, copied border, or imitation of a specific named miniature.

**Look for:**
- several kinds of space coexist without collapsing into one-point perspective;
- small details stay deliberate rather than becoming texture noise;
- bright color and fine line distribute attention across the image;
- the garden reads as both setting and patterned surface.

**Ethics/agency question:** Does giving equal visual care to figure, plant, architecture, and ornament enrich the robot’s world, or make the protagonist less dominant than in a modern hero image?

**Common failure:** A generic fantasy-book illustration with an ornate frame, realistic atmospheric depth, and random gold filigree instead of the miniature’s shallow spatial construction and distributed detail.

## 3. Rajput Painting — saturated fields and lyrical emphasis

**Teaching goal:** Contrast Rajput painting’s bold continuous outline, saturated color fields, and flattened architecture/landscape with the finer-grained spatial abundance of the Persian miniature, even though both can share courtly miniature formats.

**Prompt:**

> Repaint the same locked garden scene as a Rajput court painting: broad flat fields of intensely saturated deep red, yellow ochre, ultramarine, and green, confident continuous outline around the robot and garden forms, large clear eye shapes, architecture and landscape flattened into decorative bands instead of receding naturally, a plain or single-color field beyond the garden wall, sparse gold only as small trim accents, lyrical rather than photographic mood. Preserve the robot, iris, pool, bench, bird, wall, and their fixed arrangement. No manuscript text, no palace spectacle added from outside the source, and no copy of a named historical painting.

**Look for:**
- bold outline carries the figure more than shading does;
- saturated fields remain distinct instead of blending into atmospheric gradients;
- architecture and landscape function as graphic pattern;
- gold is an accent, not a blanket “luxury” effect.

**Ethics/agency question:** How does a lyrical, emblematic treatment change the apparent importance of a small everyday action such as reaching toward a flower?

**Common failure:** Mughal-style naturalistic modeling, cinematic palace concept art, or a generic “Indian miniature” blend that erases the lesson’s specific flat color and continuous-outline cues.

## 4. Rinpa School — crop, rhythm, and active emptiness

**Teaching goal:** Make negative space itself do compositional work: Rinpa’s asymmetrical cropping, seasonal motifs, bold silhouettes, pooled color, and gold- or silver-leaf grounds produce flatness through omission and rhythm rather than dense narrative detail.

**Prompt:**

> Repaint the identical garden scene as a Rinpa-school folding-screen composition: broad gold- or silver-leaf ground with no ordinary horizon, asymmetrical cropped arrangement, the flowering iris and pool edge turned into a rhythmic chain of bold simplified silhouettes with a few precise botanical details, pooled ink-and-color edges, repeating shapes separated by generous active empty space, the small robot integrated into the same decorative rhythm rather than modeled as a three-dimensional focal figure. Preserve every locked source object even when simplified. No Japanese text, signatures, copied screen composition, or named-work imitation.

**Look for:**
- empty ground feels intentional and structural rather than unfinished;
- cropping and repetition create movement across the surface;
- some botanical forms are highly specific while others simplify into silhouette;
- there is no ordinary horizon or deep landscape recession.

**Ethics/agency question:** When the robot becomes one rhythmic shape among flowers, water, and empty ground, does the image diminish the figure or place it in a less human-centered relationship with the environment?

**Common failure:** A realistic Japanese garden with a gold filter, a generic watercolor wash, or decorative irises pasted over conventional perspective without the asymmetry and active empty space.

## Comparison exercise

- Cover the labels. Which panel can you identify first from its treatment of empty space alone? Which needs figure or architecture cues?
- Compare the pool in all four images. Is it a diagram-like readable shape, an elevated narrative plane, a saturated graphic field, or part of an asymmetrical rhythm?
- Which image concentrates attention on the robot, and which deliberately distributes attention among figure, plant, architecture, pattern, and void?
- Find one place in each panel where “incorrect” perspective is doing useful explanatory or compositional work.
- Which transformations preserved the fixed source composition most faithfully without merely tracing it? Where did a style cue justify changing the apparent geometry?
- None of these four traditions depends on Renaissance one-point perspective. After seeing them together, what more precise vocabulary can replace the vague judgment “it looks flat”?
