# Keepsake Portrait, Four Movements

A controlled comparison lesson focused on portraiture specifically, rather than a genre
scene. Every prior set in this collection (garden-gate, harbor, night-window, one-room,
everyday-modernity, flower-arranger) compares how movements treat a full scene — a
figure doing something in a place. This set instead holds a single close-framed sitter
constant and asks how four movements, spanning roughly A.D. 50 to 1910 and three
distinct cultural traditions, each answer the oldest question in painting: how do you
represent one specific, present person? The four movements chosen — Fayum Mummy
Portraits, Northern Renaissance, Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, and Symbolism — have not
appeared in any prior set in this collection.

## Fixed composition

Use one source image throughout:

- a single seated figure, framed from the waist up, angled in three-quarter view with
  the head turned to face the viewer almost directly
- the figure rests one hand flat on a closed, plain book on a small table in front of
  them
- the other hand holds a single dried flower, raised roughly to chest height
- soft, even, frontal light on the face; no strong directional shadow in the source
- a plain, uncluttered wall or backdrop directly behind the figure, with no other
  objects, window, or landscape specified

Keep the figure's identity, pose, three-quarter head angle, and the book-and-flower
still life unchanged across all four variants — these are the constants a learner
checks for. Framing crop and background treatment are allowed to change where a
movement's own recognition cues require it; each movement section below states
explicitly what may change and why, rather than leaving it to guesswork.

## Rights boundary

Teach movements and historically documented techniques, not imitation of a named
artist. Use only public-domain reference works from artists who satisfy the Academy
policy (`PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md`). Do not place artist names in generation prompts,
negative prompts, presets, filenames, or promotional copy — even where a movement's
curriculum entry names specific historical figures (Northern Renaissance, Pre-Raphaelite
Brotherhood, and Symbolism all do; Fayum Mummy Portraits is anonymous by record),
matching the convention already established by every other set in this collection.

Learners may upload an image they own or are authorized to transform. Before
promotion, confirm that every displayed example image is public domain or separately
licensed for reproduction and that its source and rights statement are recorded.

## 1. Fayum Mummy Portraits

### Recognition cues

- large, dark, almond-shaped eyes with heavy brows, gazing directly at (or just past)
  the viewer
- warm, honey-toned skin built up in visible ridges of thick encaustic wax, not smooth
  glazed paint
- a plain, flat, dark or muted background with no setting, landscape, or architecture
- simple Roman-style hair and drapery, occasionally a thin gold-leaf wreath or jewelry
- tight head-and-shoulders framing, cropped close — the historical format almost never
  shows hands or objects below the chest
- even, frontal illumination with no cast shadow or single dramatic light source

### What changes for this movement

The format's own defining convention crops tighter than the fixed composition's
waist-up framing. Crop in to head-and-shoulders: the book stays out of frame entirely,
and the raised flower may brush the very bottom edge of the frame at most — do not
invent a visible hand or arm gesture the historical format never depicted. This is a
deliberate, documented exception to "keep the composition unchanged," not a loophole;
record it as such when reviewing results.

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the identity of the seated figure and their three-quarter head angle and
> direct gaze. Reinterpret the scene through movement-level Fayum mummy portrait ideas:
> crop tightly to head-and-shoulders only, large dark almond-shaped eyes with heavy
> brows, warm honey-toned skin built from thick encaustic wax brushstrokes with visible
> ridge texture, simple Roman-style hair, a plain flat dark or muted background with no
> setting or architecture, even frontal light with no cast shadow, and — if any hint of
> the held flower remains visible at the very bottom edge — no invented hand or arm
> gesture beyond that hint.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no landscape, architecture, window, or setting behind the figure
- no book, table, or full hand-and-arm gesture visible in frame
- no dramatic single-source lighting or cast shadow
- no smooth, glazed, photographic skin texture — surface must read as built-up wax
- no loss of the direct almond-eyed gaze

### Common failure

The model often treats "ancient portrait" as a sepia color-grade over an otherwise
unchanged photographic image, keeping the full waist-up framing, book, and background.
A successful result must actually re-crop the composition and flatten the background,
not just tint an unchanged photograph.

## 2. Northern Renaissance

### Recognition cues

- astonishing fine detail and hard, crisp edges — texture rendered almost microscopically
- luminous, glowing color built from layered oil glazes; cool, even northern daylight
- a deep, meticulously painted interior or landscape visible behind the figure
- symbolic everyday objects rendered with the same devotion as the figure, loaded with
  hidden meaning
- a direct, particular, un-idealized face — a specific person, not a generalized type

### What changes for this movement

The plain backdrop opens into a shallow, detailed interior: a small window with a
sliver of distant landscape, or a wall with a single meaningful object (a candle, a
small mirror) may be added behind the figure, matching this movement's own device of
loading ordinary settings with symbolic detail. The book and flower stay exactly where
they are, now rendered with the same jewel-like precision as everything else.

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the identity of the seated figure, their three-quarter head angle, and the
> book-and-flower still life exactly as posed. Reinterpret the scene through
> movement-level Northern Renaissance ideas: microscopic fine detail and crisp hard
> edges on skin, fabric, and the book's binding, luminous color built from layered oil
> glazes under cool even daylight, a shallow interior behind the figure with a small
> window or a single symbolic object rendered in the same precise detail, and a direct,
> individual, un-idealized face. Keep the seated pose, the book, and the held flower
> clearly legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no soft, hazy, or atmospheric edges anywhere in the image
- no idealized or generalized facial features
- no loss of the book or the held flower
- no warm golden-hour lighting — daylight should read cool and even
- no anachronistic modern objects introduced as the "symbolic" addition

### Common failure

The model tends to add "old painting" texture (craquelure, yellowed varnish) without
actually increasing the level of rendered detail. A successful result needs genuinely
higher fidelity on hair, fabric weave, and the book's surface — not a filter simulating
an aged canvas.

## 3. Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood

### Recognition cues

- saturated, jewel-toned color (deep reds, greens, blues) rather than muted academic
  brown glazes
- crisp, high-fidelity detail on hair and fabric — every strand and fold rendered
  rather than suggested
- flowing, often flame-red or auburn hair; medieval or historicizing dress
- densely symbolic objects placed with deliberate meaning rather than as incidental
  still life
- flattened, shallow pictorial space rather than illusionistic depth

### What changes for this movement

The plain backdrop becomes a dense, flattened field of symbolic detail — a patterned
textile hanging or close-packed flowers and foliage rather than empty wall — consistent
with this movement's preference for meaning-loaded surface over receding depth. The
sitter's hair is reinterpreted as loose, flowing, and vividly colored regardless of the
source image's original hair.

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the identity of the seated figure, their three-quarter head angle, and the
> book-and-flower still life exactly as posed. Reinterpret the scene through
> movement-level Pre-Raphaelite ideas: saturated jewel-toned color throughout, crisp
> high-fidelity rendering of flowing, flame-red or auburn hair and medieval-inspired
> dress, a densely patterned or floral backdrop replacing the plain wall, flattened
> shallow space with no illusionistic depth, and the book and flower treated as
> deliberately symbolic objects rendered in the same jewel-toned saturation as the
> figure. Keep the seated pose, the book, and the held flower clearly legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no muted or desaturated color anywhere in the image
- no photographic or illusionistic depth in the background
- no loss of the book or the held flower
- no generic "fantasy painting" styling untethered from the movement's actual
  jewel-tone, high-detail visual language
- no modern dress or props

### Common failure

The model often defaults to a generic soft-focus "romantic painting" look — hazy edges,
pastel color — which is closer to Symbolism than to the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood's
actual hallmark of crisp, saturated, obsessively detailed naturalism. A successful
result should look sharper and more saturated than a typical "old painting" prompt, not
softer.

## 4. Symbolism

### Recognition cues

- dreamlike, mysterious mood — reverie rather than a report of the real world
- muted, twilight color and soft, glowing, sourceless light
- flattened, decorative, or hazy space that resists ordinary depth
- a sense of hidden meaning — the picture reads as a riddle or symbol, not a plain scene
- allegorical or melancholy atmosphere without necessarily adding new mythological
  figures

### What changes for this movement

The plain backdrop dissolves into hazy, indistinct twilight space rather than a
legible room or landscape — depth becomes ambiguous rather than absent (unlike Fayum's
flat plane) or detailed (unlike Northern Renaissance's interior). The book and flower
remain, but read as symbolic objects charged with unstated meaning rather than ordinary
possessions.

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the identity of the seated figure, their three-quarter head angle, and the
> book-and-flower still life exactly as posed. Reinterpret the scene through
> movement-level Symbolist ideas: muted twilight color, soft glowing light with no clear
> source, a hazy and indistinct background that resists ordinary depth without becoming
> a flat plane, a dreamlike and melancholy mood, and the closed book and held flower
> rendered as if charged with unstated symbolic meaning. Keep the seated pose, the book,
> and the held flower clearly legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists
- no new mythological figures, creatures, or narrative elements added to the scene
- no bright, saturated, or high-contrast color
- no crisp architectural or landscape detail in the background
- no loss of the book or the held flower
- no literal "spooky" horror imagery — the mood is melancholy and dreamlike, not macabre

### Common failure

The model tends to render "symbolist" as a generic dark-fantasy filter — heavy vignette,
desaturated grey — without the movement's actual soft glow and twilight color. A
successful result should still feel colored (muted blues, violets, ambers) and gently
luminous, not simply dim.

## Blind comparison exercise

Hide the movement labels and ask learners to rank each result on:

1. sitter identity preservation
2. book-and-flower still-life preservation (or, for Fayum, a documented and justified
   departure from it)
3. movement recognition
4. background treatment — flat, detailed, dense/patterned, or hazy — and whether it
   matches the movement's own visual logic
5. color saturation and light source
6. absence of generic "old painting" or "fantasy filter" behavior

Then ask learners to identify the movement, era, and culture of origin, and cite three
visible cues, before revealing the labels.

## Reflection prompts

- Fayum Mummy Portraits is the only movement here that changes the *framing*, not just
  the surface treatment — why does that specific formal constraint (crop, not color)
  carry the movement's identity so strongly?
- Northern Renaissance and Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood both load ordinary objects with
  symbolic meaning — what specifically distinguishes their two approaches (detail
  level, color saturation, spatial depth)?
- Symbolism and Fayum both minimize background detail — what makes one read as "hazy
  and dreamlike" and the other as "flat and absent," and why does that difference
  matter for what each movement is trying to say about the sitter?
- Which of the four results keeps the book and flower most legible as specific
  objects, and which most successfully turns them into pure symbol? What was traded
  away to make that shift?
- Which result is recognizable without relying on a named artist's signature
  mannerisms?
- All four movements here center an individual, present person — how does "presence"
  (direct gaze, un-idealized features, plain background) versus "atmosphere" (twilight,
  ornament, symbolic staging) change what a viewer believes about who this person is?

## Reproducibility record

For every generated comparison, record:

- source image identifier and rights status
- generation engine and model version
- workflow or endpoint version
- complete positive and negative prompts
- seed
- dimensions
- guidance, steps, sampler, and scheduler where applicable
- LoRA path, trigger, and weight where applicable
- generation date
- ArtJob and ArtImage identifiers where available
- manual notes on composition and identity preservation, including whether the Fayum
  variant's documented framing exception was applied correctly

## Promotion gate

Promote a set only when:

- the four results preserve the sitter's identity, three-quarter angle, and the
  book-and-flower still life closely enough for a fair comparison — except for Fayum's
  documented framing exception, which must itself be applied consistently and not used
  to excuse unrelated composition drift
- movement recognition does not depend on protected artist names
- source and output rights are documented
- representation review finds no stereotyping, dehumanization, or erasure of the
  sitter across any of the four variants, particularly given how directly this set
  frames an individual person's face
- prompts and metadata are sufficient to reproduce or audit the exercise
- weak results and negative findings remain documented rather than quietly replaced
