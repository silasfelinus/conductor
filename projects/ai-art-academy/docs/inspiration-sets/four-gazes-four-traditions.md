# Four Gazes, Four Traditions

A controlled comparison lesson for studying how four traditions from roughly the same broad era (c. 1450-1750) construct a single sitter's presence and gaze. Mannerism dissolves the figure into elegant artifice, Baroque stages it in dramatic light, Spanish Golden Age freezes it in sculptural stillness, and Ethiopian Icon Painting confronts the viewer with a direct, unblinking frontal stare — curriculum-outline.md's own §43/§44/§45 entries already cross-reference each other as contrasting poles of the same period, so this set makes that contrast visible side by side for the first time. All four movements are new to this curriculum's inspiration-set library — none has appeared in a comparison lesson before this one.

## Fixed composition

Use one source image throughout:

- one adult figure, head and shoulders to half-length, facing the camera
- both hands visible, holding one closed book at chest height
- a plain, unpatterned wall or neutral ground behind the figure
- no visible furniture, window, or background scenery
- soft, even lighting on the face in the source photo (each entry below is free to redirect this per its own movement)
- square or portrait crop, the figure filling roughly three-quarters of the frame

Keep the person, pose, book, crop, and camera position unchanged across all four entries. The exercise is about how each tradition constructs light, space, and the sitter's gaze — not about swapping in a different person or object.

## Rights boundary

Teach movements and historically documented techniques, not imitation of a named artist. Use only public-domain reference works from artists who satisfy `PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md`. Do not place artist names in generation prompts, negative prompts, presets, filenames, or promotional copy.

Learners may upload an image they own or are authorized to transform. Before promotion, confirm that every displayed example image is public domain or separately licensed for reproduction and that its source and rights statement are recorded.

## 1. Mannerism

### Recognition cues

- impossibly elongated limbs, neck, and fingers, stretched past anatomical correctness on purpose
- a twisting, off-balance pose (the *figura serpentinata*) rather than a stable, centered stance
- smooth, cool, porcelain-like skin with a composed, faintly aloof expression
- a restrained, acidic jewel-toned palette (cool pinks, acid greens, deep blues)
- shallow, ambiguous, or compressed pictorial space — a plain dark ground rather than a legible room

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact sitter, pose, book, crop, and camera position. Repaint the figure as an Italian Mannerist court portrait: elongate the neck, fingers, and limbs past anatomical correctness, introduce a subtle twisting off-balance pose, cool the skin to a smooth porcelain tone with a composed, faintly aloof expression, restrain the palette to acidic jewel tones, and flatten the background into a plain or ambiguous dark ground. Keep the book and the sitter's identity legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists or copied canonical compositions
- no dramatic chiaroscuro or theatrical spotlight (that belongs to entry 2)
- no religious iconography, halo, or symbolic ground pattern
- no loss of the book or a recognizable human silhouette
- no literal deformity or horror framing — the distortion should read as elegant, not grotesque

### Common failure

The model may just apply a cool color grade to an otherwise anatomically normal photo. A successful result must actually stretch the figure's proportions and flatten the space, not merely shift the palette.

## 2. Baroque

### Recognition cues

- dramatic spotlight lighting: a bright figure emerging from deep surrounding darkness
- strong diagonal composition and a sense of caught motion rather than calm symmetry
- rich, saturated color and richly rendered fabric and skin texture within the lit area
- an intense, caught-mid-moment expression — mid-breath, mid-thought, alert rather than composed
- everything outside the lit figure recedes to near-black, with no visible room or furniture

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact sitter, pose, book, crop, and camera position. Repaint the figure in a Baroque chiaroscuro style: a single strong light source striking the face, hands, and book while the surrounding background recedes into deep, near-black shadow, rich saturated color and fabric texture within the lit passages, a diagonal sense of caught motion, and an alert, mid-moment expression. Keep the sitter's identity and the book clearly legible within the light.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists or specific known compositions
- no flat, evenly lit background (the darkness is the point)
- no acid or pastel palette (reserve that register for Mannerism)
- no loss of the book or hands from the lit area
- no candle, weapon, or added prop not present in the source

### Common failure

The model may simply darken the whole image uniformly instead of building a true spotlight-from-darkness contrast. A successful result needs one clear, directional light source and genuinely black recession elsewhere, not a dim filter.

## 3. Spanish Golden Age

### Recognition cues

- a deep, near-black or plain neutral background lit by a single strong source (tenebrism), quieter and more static than Baroque's diagonal drama
- sculptural, almost frozen clarity of form — every surface rendered with the same careful, unblinking attention
- a restrained, muted palette (umber, ochre, black, occasional deep red or white) rather than saturated color
- a solitary, minimally-peopled, contemplative mood rather than caught motion
- confident, visible brushwork that can look loose up close but resolves into photographic precision at a distance

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact sitter, pose, book, crop, and camera position. Repaint the figure in a Spanish Golden Age tenebrist style: a single strong light source against a near-black or plain neutral ground, a restrained palette of umber, ochre, black, and occasional deep red or white, sculptural and almost frozen clarity of form, and a quiet, still, contemplative mood rather than motion or drama. Keep the sitter and book rendered with the same careful, unblinking attention as every other surface.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists or specific known compositions
- no swirling motion, diagonal drama, or theatrical gesture (that belongs to entry 2)
- no bright or saturated color
- no loss of the book's sculptural presence
- no religious costume, halo, or symbolic prop not present in the source

### Common failure

The model may reproduce Baroque's drama instead of Spanish Golden Age's stillness — the two are easy to conflate. A successful result should feel frozen and quiet, not caught mid-motion; if the figure looks like it is about to move, it has drifted into the wrong entry.

## 4. Ethiopian Icon Painting

### Recognition cues

- large, wide-open, almond-shaped eyes gazing directly and frontally at the viewer — the tradition's single most distinctive trait
- flat, unmodeled fields of color bounded by firm black contour outlines, no cast shadow or atmospheric blending
- a warm, saturated palette dominated by red ochre, gold, and deep blue-green
- strict frontal symmetry, with the figure centered and facing the viewer directly
- simplified, linear drapery folds rather than naturalistic fabric

### Remix prompt

> Preserve the exact sitter, pose, book, crop, and camera position. Repaint the figure as an Ethiopian Solomonic-era icon: flatten every surface into unmodeled fields of warm red ochre, gold, and deep blue-green bounded by firm black contour outlines, enlarge the eyes into a wide-open, almond-shaped, direct frontal gaze at the viewer, simplify the drapery into linear folds, and center the figure in strict frontal symmetry against a plain or minimally patterned ground. Keep the book and the sitter's identity legible.

### Negative guidance

- no named artists, workshops, or specific manuscript/panel citations
- no added halo, crown, second figure, or narrative scene border
- no cast shadow, atmospheric blending, or photorealistic skin texture
- no three-quarter or profile angle — the gaze must stay frontal
- no loss of the book

### Common failure

The model may enlarge the eyes slightly while leaving the rest of the image photographic and shaded. A successful result must flatten the entire figure into unmodeled color fields with hard contour lines — the eyes are the most distinctive cue, but not the only one that has to change.

## Blind comparison exercise

Hide the movement labels and ask learners to rank each result on:

1. preservation of the source pose, book, and crop
2. whether the light behaves as spotlight-in-darkness (Baroque), still tenebrism (Spanish Golden Age), flattened color field (Ethiopian Icon), or ambiguous/compressed space (Mannerism)
3. movement recognition without artist names
4. whether the sitter's gaze reads as composed-and-aloof, caught-mid-moment, contemplative, or directly confrontational
5. absence of generic filter behavior (a color grade alone is not a style change)

Then ask learners to identify the movement and cite three visible cues before revealing the labels.

## Reflection prompts

- Baroque and Spanish Golden Age both use a single strong light source against darkness — what specifically tells them apart once the drama/stillness distinction is removed?
- Mannerism and Ethiopian Icon Painting both flatten or compress space, but for opposite reasons — what is each one flattening space *toward*?
- Which of the four treatments makes the sitter feel most like an individual, and which makes them feel most like a symbol or type?
- Ethiopian Icon Painting is the only entry built to make the viewer feel watched rather than watching — does that read clearly in the result, or does it just look like "big eyes"?
- Which prompt produced the most unauthorized additions (halo, weapon, extra figure), and what wording should be tightened?
