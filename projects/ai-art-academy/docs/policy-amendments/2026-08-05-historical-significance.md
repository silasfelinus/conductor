# Historical significance, living artists, and reusable images

Date: 2026-08-05  
Decision source: Silas's response on `ai-art-academy/t-043`  
Status: active clarification of `PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md`

## Decision

The Academy's ethical boundary is **do not turn living artists into generation-style presets**. Historical significance is not erased by a creator dying fewer than 70 years ago.

The Academy may teach, discuss, compare, and critically engage with the work of any historically significant artist who is no longer living. Cultural conversation is a valid reason to include an artist or movement in historical curriculum.

This clarification supersedes any reading of `PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md` that treats the death-plus-70 threshold as a ban on historical discussion.

## Keep three questions separate

### 1. May the Academy discuss the artist or work?

Yes, when the creator is no longer living and the inclusion has genuine historical, cultural, or educational value. The lesson may name the artist, explain the work, describe formal devices, examine patronage and power, and link to authoritative museum or archival records.

Living artists remain excluded from Academy lessons and named generation presets. They may still appear in neutral historical context when necessary to explain a movement, but the Academy does not build style imitation around them.

### 2. May the Academy display a reproduction?

Only with item-level reusable rights. Historical importance does not create image-reuse permission.

A displayed image still needs one of the accepted grants in `PUBLIC-DOMAIN-POLICY.md` section 2, such as CC0, Public Domain Mark, or equivalent unrestricted open-access terms. The work and the digitization or photograph must both be checked. A lesson may discuss a protected work without copying its image.

### 3. May the Academy use the artist as a generation preset or commercial remix anchor?

This remains the strictest lane.

- Never use a living artist's name as a generation preset.
- For deceased artists, prefer movement-level prompts unless a named-artist preset is historically useful, ethically defensible, and compatible with the commercial-generation licensing rule in `CONTROL.md`.
- Do not use protected source images as remix inputs without permission.
- Do not present generated output as a substitute for a lost, unavailable, or protected historical work.
- A culturally significant lesson may be promoted without any named-artist generation preset at all.

## Harlem Renaissance application

The Harlem Renaissance candidate may use the four verified CC0 Winold Reiss portraits as **historical-context display images**. Their role must be described honestly: Reiss was a white German-American illustrator commissioned to portray Black cultural figures for *Survey Graphic* and *The New Negro*. His portraits are evidence about publication, patronage, representation, and circulation, not a stand-in for the movement's Black artists.

Aaron Douglas, Meta Vaux Warrick Fuller, Archibald Motley, and Augusta Savage may be taught directly as historically significant artists even though their death dates fall inside the previous 70-year policy window. Their works may be displayed only where a specific image has reusable rights.

The movement-level generation preset should remain artist-anchor-free. It should teach narrative structure, publishing language, migration, institutions, community, and Black modernism without inviting imitation of a protected body of work.

## Required framing

When the Academy teaches a culturally significant movement whose most important artists do not have reusable images:

1. Say plainly why the artist matters.
2. Distinguish discussion rights from image-reuse rights.
3. Do not replace unavailable works with synthetic lookalikes.
4. Use open-access contextual material only when its relationship to the movement is explained.
5. Keep prompts movement-level when named-artist imitation would flatten cultural history into a visual costume.
6. Invite critical discussion of gaze, patronage, circulation, and who controlled representation.
