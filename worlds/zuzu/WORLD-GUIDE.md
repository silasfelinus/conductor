# Zuzu World: Setting and Compositional Lore

**Status:** approved high-level worldbuilding direction from Silas, October 9, 2026. This guide is authoritative for **general setting, species/roles and procedural adaptations** where it does not contradict the Book One chronology or locked visual cast. **Creative story spoilers are allowed in GitHub**, including unrevealed character motivations and world history. In-story discovery should still happen at the intended dramatic moment. Agents should read [the world index](README.md), Book One, locked Cast Picks, and production-specific guardrails.

## The frontier without a neat border

Zuzu travels through a **strange anthropomorphic animal world**, much of it a weird-west wasteland of homesteads, settler towns, raiders, lonely trails and things with surprisingly friendly faces. The world's edges and sense of place are **hazy**. A road, cave or settlement may be different on a later visit, not because the writers forgot its name, but because the world itself is capable of changing. In games, changes must be **causally recorded and visible**: a land shifts after a bargain, disaster, supernatural event or consequential player decision. Old visits remain historical fact.

Zuzu comes **from somewhere else**. He carries the manners, martial discipline, social obligations and worldview of a **traditional Edo-era samurai** into a frontier that often resembles a Western. He should not be rewritten as an ordinary American gunslinger or as a generic high-fantasy warrior. His growth through different stories can modify his beliefs and loyalties without erasing where he began.

### Creature design

Animals may come from any **reasonably identifiable species**. Morphology, senses, habitats and personal habits should suggest the species, even in more stylized bipedal, clothed, speaking or metaphorical forms. Physical features and culture can be hybridized imaginatively without losing the animal anchor.

**A creature's species is never shorthand for its virtue, alignment, occupation, menace or narrative fate.** A rabbit could be a kind homesteader, an unscrupulous saloon worker, an aristocratic killer or a frightened wanderer. An otter could farm the same land or work the ferry. The social role, secret agenda and disposition should be drawn independently within ecological/narrative plausibility. Avoid implying that real animal species possess innate moral properties.

Named Characters retain stable identity, species, cast design, past deeds and relationships. Unnamed extras can be procedurally assembled from **Species Facets × Occupation/Role Facets × Faction × Hidden disposition/motivation × Conditions × Location**. A player is not entitled to know a stranger's nature before interacting with them. Apparent kindness should be possible from an intimidating species; danger should be possible from a harmless-looking one.

### Known corners of the world

- **The Homestead:** The first accessible region in the new procedural board-game interpretation. It contains unsettled farms, refuges/chapels and settlements that can genuinely offer help or harbor severe danger. Rabbits and otters are possible inhabitants, but which species fills a profession is randomized and not evidence of innocence. An abbess-led religious authority is a potential major confrontation; unrevealed motivations remain private story material.
- **Storm-crow cave:** Storm Crows include **magical witches who live deep in a cave**. Their magic and social customs can be dangerous, protective, transactional or enigmatic. “Storm Crow” is a named creative faction/identity and need not make every crow automatically magical or hostile.
- **Gorilla encounters:** Gorillas are viable **high-level challenge participants**. Some may have vampirism or dark-magic conditions; others may simply be physically formidable, allies, travelers or bystanders. Vampirism is a *condition/secret*, not an attribute of gorilla biology.
- **Raiders and settlers:** Neither “settler” nor “raider” is a biological category, and both camps can contain sympathetic, deceptive or contradictory individuals. Factions may change with player action.

### Key world mysteries and the first-land antagonist

The Abbess is the central antagonist of the Homestead's first-land arc. She has spent years taking children from the surrounding frontier, using dark sacrificial rites to feed a cosmic horror and attempting to bring it fully into the world. Her convent's apparent kindness is a calculated disguise. The first confrontation should be earned through signs of missing residents, misleading hospitality and discovery rather than an unexplained immediate fight.

Humans once inhabited this world. Something went wrong. What actually happened is a major mystery, currently unresolved; its relationship to the unstable landscape, anthropomorphic inhabitants or the cosmic entity must not be invented as settled canon. Zuzu himself comes from another land and follows an Edo-era samurai code.

The Book One comic already contains the mission, the rescue and the partly manifested cosmic being. Keep Book One's exact chronology distinct from the alternate outcomes of the board game.

### Canon and disclosure

The earlier Kind Robots Character, Reward, Scenario and Dream lore was generated as **filler**, and **is not canon**. It is eligible for correction against Silas's new directions after identity-aware database matching. The Book One comic narrative remains its own canonical chronology; randomized gamebook, animation, combat and board-game routes are *possibilities*, not retroactive canon.

**No GitHub spoiler embargo.** Silas explicitly authorizes storing the full Zuzu fictional lore in source files, issues and agent prompts. Keep worldbuilding accessible to agents. Separately, reveal twists to players through appropriately paced encounters rather than front-loading them in the game UI. Actual user data, credentials, access permissions and publishing gates remain protected.

This setting guide supplies **constraints**, not a fixed procedural encounter table: the board game may vary species/roles, alliances, locations and results within those constraints. Approved locked cast ArtImages and LoRA provenance take priority over newly sampled generic illustrations.
